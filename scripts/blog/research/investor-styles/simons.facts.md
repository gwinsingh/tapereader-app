## 1. Snapshot

- Style: systematic, short-term statistical trading across stocks, futures, currencies and bonds; thousands of small positions held days to weeks; market-neutral in equities; heavy borrowing [LUX][PSI-S][DOL12].
- Instruments: listed stocks long and short (largely through derivatives), futures and forwards on commodities, financial instruments and currencies, government and mortgage securities [DOL12]. Rule in Simons's words: publicly traded, liquid, amenable to modelling [LUX].
- Holding period: basket portfolios turned over almost fully every three months; some positions held seconds [PSI-H][PSI-R]. Reviewer of Zuckerman: days or weeks; not a high-frequency trader [CFA]. One to two days up to one to two weeks [ACQ]. Ziemba's "3–8 second trades" is unsupported elsewhere [ZIEMBA].
- Leverage: fund-level equity positions 4 to 5 times investor capital [DOL12]; inside the bank options, average 11.7:1 (Deutsche) and 12.9:1 (Barclays) in 2005–06, peak 16.8:1, contractual limit up to 20:1 [PSI-R]. Zuckerman: about $12.50 of positions per $1, near $20 at times; over $60bn of positions on $5bn in 2002 [ACQ].
- Assets: Medallion $20m (1988), $270m when closed to new money (1993), $3.3bn (2000), about $5.2bn 2002–09, about $10bn 2010–18 [LUX][GL]; $10.2bn at Apr 2011 [DOL12]; about $12bn of internal capital at end 2024 [HW25]. Firm peak: regulatory assets about $130bn (gross measure) in 2021 [WRT]; $96.4bn in July 2026 [ADV]. RIEF: $25.6bn Sep 2007 [BBG07]; $7.9bn end 2013 [PSI-R]; nearly $36bn start of 2020; under $20bn Nov 2024 and Nov 2025 [II24][II25].
- Status, Nov 2026: Simons died 10 May 2024 in New York City, aged 86; cause not given by the foundation [SF][FOR]. Born 25 Apr 1938 [WJS][FOR]. Firm run by Peter Brown (chairman since 1 Jan 2021) and David Lippe as co-CEOs since 1 Jul 2024 [ADV]. Simons's estate holds 25–50% of the holding company; son Nathaniel and widow Marilyn are directors [ADV].
- Net worth at death: $31.4bn (Forbes, via [WJS]); $31.8bn (Bloomberg index, via [FOR]).

## 2. Origin

- Born Brookline (per [FOR]; [WJS] says born Cambridge, raised Brookline; [LUX] says raised in Newton), Massachusetts; father owned or ran a shoe factory [LUX]. BS mathematics MIT 1958, in three years; PhD Berkeley 1961 at 23 (thesis year 1962 in one listing) [LUX][WJS]. Taught at MIT and Harvard.
- 1964–68: code breaker at the Institute for Defense Analyses (IDA), Princeton, working for the National Security Agency [SFL][FOR]. Fired after publicly opposing the Vietnam War: a letter answering a 1967 New York Times Magazine article by IDA's president, General Maxwell Taylor [FOR], then remarks to Newsweek [LUX]. Year: 1968 in most accounts; Bloomberg 2007 says 1967 at age 29 [BBG07].
- 1968: chairman of mathematics at Stony Brook [ZS][WJS] (Lux: 1968–76; Wikipedia: 1968–78). 1974: paper with Shiing-Shen Chern, "Characteristic Forms and Geometric Invariants" (Chern–Simons theory) [LUX]. 1976: Oswald Veblen Prize in Geometry [LUX][WJS].
- Early money: a Colombian floor-tile and pipe company with MIT classmates (1961 per [LUX], 1964 per [BBG07]); soybeans at Berkeley; in 1973–74 a commodities trader multiplied the partners' money about tenfold in seven or eight months [LUX][BBG07].
- 1976–78: traded currencies with his own money, half time at Stony Brook [LUX]. 1978: left to start Monemetrics (a currency trading firm, in a strip mall on Long Island) and the fund Limroy [ZS][SFL][WRT]. Bloomberg 2007 dates Monemetrics to 1977 [BBG07]. Renamed Renaissance Technologies 1982 [ZS][DOL12].
- Limroy: fundamental at first (Fed policy, interest rates); mixed venture capital with currency trading; grew capital 25 times in ten years [LUX]. Baum (joined 1979) built a model but preferred trading on judgement [BBG07]; he quit in 1984 after steep losses ([ZS]; Wikipedia, "Leonard E. Baum", citing Zuckerman p. 25: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_E._Baum); a 40% fall in his portfolio on a bond position triggered a clause that ended his trading, per [ACQ] retelling Zuckerman (not checked against the book).
- Ax (joined 1979) and Sandor Straus ran the trading from California as Axcom from 1985 [ZS][BBG07]; Simons owned about a quarter [ACQ].
- Medallion: launched March 1988 [LUX], named for the maths prizes Simons and Ax had won [WRT]. 1988 +8.8% net; lost steadily in 1989; peak-to-trough about 30% by April 1989 [BBG07]; trading halted June 1989 [LUX]; Ax left after a dispute [BBG07]. Calendar 1989: −4.1% [LUX].
- Formative mistakes, as the record shows them: ten years of discretionary and mixed trading with large swings (Baum 1984); a first system that trended and lost (1989). By 1988 he had decided to trade wholly on models [SFL].

## 3. The source of the edge

- **Analysis (statistical):** many weak, short-lived price patterns found in cleaned historical data, combined in one model. Evidence: Simons's descriptions [LUX]; the firm's statement that predictions are right "only slightly more often than not" and rely on the law of large numbers [PSI-S]; the Labor Department notice: strategies "primarily based on technical methods" [DOL12]. Individual access: the idea yes, the scale no.
- **Speed, technology, data:** in-house execution from 1995, early electronic links, over a million lines of code, about a thousand man-years [LUX][PSI-H]; data collected and cleaned from the 1980s (Straus) [ZS]; news, filings, weather, crop and energy reports [PSI-S]. Individual: no.
- **Execution cost:** 20 traders whose job is to trade at the lowest cost without moving prices; "crucial" for narrow margins [LUX]. Campbell Harvey: very low execution costs are a likely part of the explanation [II20c]. Individual: partly (measuring own costs).
- **Structure:** (a) cheap non-recourse leverage through bank basket options, 10% down, up to 20:1, 2000 on [PSI-R]; (b) long-term tax rates on short-term trading through the same options until 2015, later reversed by settlement [PSI-R][CNBC21]; (c) capacity discipline: capped size, profits paid out every year, no outside money [DOL12]; (d) staff are the investors, pay tied to the one fund [LUX][HSU]. Individual: no.
- **Behaviour:** not overriding the system in normal times; cutting exposure when the survival of the fund was in question [LUX][ZII][PSI-R]. Individual: yes.
- **Luck or era:** Simons's own word for it [LUX]; 1990 long oil when Iraq invaded Kuwait [LUX]; early markets less efficient (T-bill and futures mispricings that no longer exist) [LUX]. But 31 years without a losing gross year across very different regimes argues against an era effect [GL][II20c].
- Unexplained remainder: Cornell: no adequate rational-market explanation [II20c].

## 4. The process

- **Finding ideas:** start with data, not theory; search for patterns that would not occur at random; test for statistical significance and consistency over time; only then ask whether some behaviour could explain it [LUX]. One or two model changes a week by 2014; mature system, small gains each time [PSI-H].
- **Forming a thesis:** no thesis per trade. A signal must be new relative to what the model already holds, then gets a weight; signals are layered [LUX]. Each forecast has a fixed, short time frame, unlike convergence trades with no deadline [LUX].
- **Timing:** forecast, trade, re-forecast "all day long" [LUX]. 100,000 to 300,000 trades a day through the bank accounts [PSI-R][PSI-H].
- **Sizing:** by the model: forecast strength, risk and trading cost; thousands of positions; in the equities portfolio of the sister fund Equimetrics no stock above 5% and net exposure per sector 2% or less (1999–2000) [LUX]. Kelly-style sizing: Ziemba says he taught Simons Kelly betting in 1992 [ZIEMBA]; Berlekamp, who had worked under John Kelly at Bell Labs in 1960 and 1962 [WB], brought it in 1989 per [ACQ] retelling Zuckerman. Neither is confirmed by the firm.
- **Managing:** market-neutral; a risk control department; senior managers adjust "the levels at which we run" in unusual conditions (Nov 2008) [PSI-R]; an allocation function set by hand moves business between banks (2011) [PSI-R]. Barrier options cap the loss on each option at the premium [PSI-S].
- **Exiting:** by the model when the forecast horizon ends or the forecast changes. No discretionary profit targets.
- Example of the whole loop failing and being repaired: March 2000 (section 7).

## 5. Risk and drawdowns

- Stated rules: never put full faith in a model (Brown's boss to Brown, 2000; the testimony does not name the boss, accounts take it to be Simons) [PSI-H]; survive first (2007) [ZII]; loss protection through non-recourse options [PSI-S]; no credit lines "of any significance" in 2000 [LUX], which changed with the options from September 2000.
- 1989: about −30% peak to trough by April; trading stopped in June for about six months; strategy rebuilt towards short-term trading with Laufer and Berlekamp [BBG07][LUX][BERL].
- March 2000: very large losses in a few days after 10–13 March; cause found (Nasdaq positions "hedged" with NYSE positions); Brown offered to resign [PSI-H]. Dollar amounts ($90m, then $80m, approaching $300m in days) come from a forum reproduction of the book and are (not verified). Year ended +98.5% net [BBG07].
- August 2007: over $1bn lost in a week, about 20%; RIEF nearly $3bn, about 10%; Simons overrode the system and cut positions; recovered the next week [ZII]. August as a whole +3.9% [BBG07]; 2007 +73.7% net [GL, own calculation]. RIEF −8.7% in August 2007 (Bloomberg, via [WRT]).
- 2008: +82.4% net at the 44% fee (gross 152.1%) [GL][II21]; the firm ran at reduced levels in November 2008 [PSI-R].
- March 2020: Medallion's month not found; year +76% net [II21]. RIEF −10.4% in the first four months of 2020 [ACQ].
- Monthly record Jan 1993–Apr 2005: 17 losing months of 148; 3 losing quarters of 49 ([WRT] citing Ziemba and Ziemba 2007; [ZIEMBA] confirms the period of the monthly data and "very few monthly losses").
- Near ruin: 1989 (fund of $20m, nearly closed); August 2007 was the one time lenders' collateral demands were in sight at scale [ZII].
- Concentration: none at position level; the concentrated risk is "duplication", many quant funds holding the same positions [PSI-R].

## 7. Signature episodes

- **Win: the 1989–90 rebuild.** Trading stopped mid-1989; Berlekamp bought control of Axcom, moved it to Berkeley, restarted late 1989 with shorter-term trading; 1990 +55% net; he sold to Simons at six times cost after 16 months [BERL][LUX]. Lesson Simons drew: trade wholly on models [SFL].
- **Win: equities, 1993–2000.** Brown and Mercer hired from IBM's speech-recognition group in 1993; breakthrough 1995 [ZS]; a separate market-neutral stock fund was folded into Medallion in 1997 after three middling years [LUX]; equities made the fund scalable from $0.8bn (1997) to $5bn (2002) [GL].
- **Win: 2000.** +98.5% net, gross 128%, profit $2.4bn on $1.9bn; S&P −9% [GL][BBG07][SP].
- **Win: 2008.** Gross 152%, profit $7.9bn on $5.2bn; net 82.4% [GL][II21].
- **Loss: 1989.** Above.
- **Loss: March 2000.** Above; fix: the faulty hedge removed; first barrier option six months later [PSI-H].
- **Loss: August 2007.** Above; Simons would make the same decision again [ZII].
- **Loss: the outside-money funds, 2007–09 and 2020.** Above.
- **Other failures:** Matrix mortgage fund 1995–98 (−20.6% in 1998) [LUX]; venture stakes (Franklin, MyPoints) [LUX].

## 8. Mistakes and criticism

- Tax: Senate report, July 2014 [PSI-R]; McCain: more than $6bn of tax avoided [PSI-H]; September 2021 settlement: up to about $7bn in tax, interest and penalties from the seven board members of 2005–15 and their spouses; Simons a further $670m; fund investors pay tax and interest but no penalties (Brown's letter, seen by Reuters) [CNBC21].
- Leverage: the options let a US fund borrow far beyond the 2:1 margin rule; Levin called it a risk to the banks [PSI-H]. Renaissance: the options were bought for leverage and loss protection, not tax [PSI-S].
- Structure as the edge: Zuckerman: cheap leverage plays a key role [II20c]; Harvey: low costs, secrecy, capacity discipline [II20c].
- Social value: Bernstein: a zero-sum transfer; liquidity that vanishes when needed [CFA].
- Returns overstated by naive compounding: Guo and Liu [GL].
- Outside investors got a different product: Medallion +80% and RIEF −16% in 2008 raised questions from investors (WSJ, May 2009, via [WJS]); 2020 again [II21].
- Mercer: joined 1993; co-CEO from 2010; funded Breitbart, Cambridge Analytica, the 2016 Trump campaign; about $22.5m to Republican causes in the 2016 cycle [WM]; announced 2 Nov 2017 that he would step down as co-CEO and leave the board on 1 Jan 2018, staying as a researcher [F17]; Bloomberg reported that Simons urged it because of morale and recruiting [CNBC17]. David Magerman, a researcher who criticised Mercer publicly, was suspended and then fired; he sued [CFA][WM]. Simons himself gave over $109m to Democratic candidates and committees from 2015 [FOR].
- Secrecy: long non-disclosure and non-compete agreements [ZS]; lawsuit against Millennium and two former researchers settled in 2007 for $20m [ZII].
- Changes after mistakes: 1989 → short-term statistical system; 2000 → loss-limiting options and distrust of any single hedge; 2007 → option terms renegotiated, awareness of crowding [PSI-S].

## 9. Psychology and routine

- His stated role: hiring scientists, open sharing of ideas, pay tied to the whole firm's results; a weekly meeting with the research group [LUX][HSU][SFL]. He credited the scientists and the collaborative atmosphere, not himself [SFL].
- Hiring: no business-school or Wall Street hires; candidates present their science to the whole firm [LUX].
- On luck: quoted in 2000 as wondering each morning whether he is lucky that day [LUX]. Closing principles of his 2010 MIT talk: do something new, work with the best people, be guided by beauty, do not give up, hope for good luck [HSU].
- On overrides: "We don't override the models" [LUX], with documented exceptions: August 2007 [ZII], November 2008 [PSI-R], and manual cut-backs in extreme volatility [LUX].
- Daily routine: NOT FOUND in any detail. Chain smoker [LUX][FOR].
- Retirement: announced October 2009, left as CEO 1 Jan 2010 [WJS]; chairman until 1 Jan 2021 [ADV].
- Philanthropy: Simons Foundation (1994, with Marilyn Simons); Math for America (2004); autism research (2003); Flatiron Institute (2016); Stony Brook gifts of $25m (2006), $60m (2008), $150m (2011) and $500m from the foundation (June 2023); over $4bn given in total [WJS][FOR].
- Family: married twice, five children. Son Paul died in 1996, aged 34, hit by a car while cycling; son Nicholas drowned in Bali in 2003, aged 24 [WJS][FOR].

## 10. Evolution and regime

- 1978–88 discretionary and mixed; 1988–89 trend-style futures system; 1990 short-term futures; 1993–2000 equities added; 2000 on leveraged through options; 2002–09 capped near $5bn; 2010–18 near $10bn [GL][ZS].
- Liked: volatility and turmoil (1994, 1998, 2000, 2008, 2020) [LUX]. Disliked: crowding among similar funds (2007) and, for the slow funds, markets driven by policy and momentum (2020) [II21].
- Last five years: Medallion reportedly +76% (2020), +30% (2024); firm-level record continues without Simons [HW25][247]. RIEF: −19.4% (2020), +20.5% (2021), +22.7% (2024), a 14% loss in October 2025 [II24][II25].

## 11. What carries over

- Investing: (i) the product sold to outsiders differs from the one the insiders keep; (ii) capacity: a strategy that works on $10bn was not offered at $100bn; (iii) fees and tax drag decide net results.
- Swing: (i) many small bets with a small tested edge; (ii) a fixed holding horizon per signal; (iii) cut gross exposure when the system behaves outside its history (2007); (iv) liquidity limits on size (RIEF's warning on large positions [II25]).
- Day: (i) expectancy and sample size (50.75% needs thousands of trades); (ii) costs and slippage as a measured variable; (iii) rules written and tested before use; (iv) overrides only to reduce risk.
- Seven questions: (1) sizing by the model; leverage raised on stronger opportunities; no discretionary adding. (2) After losses: stop and rebuild (1989); find and remove the faulty signal (2000); cut exposure (2007, 2008). (3) Gains: not applicable per trade; at fund level, profits paid out every year. (4) Market filter: none for direction; volatility helps; human judgement on regime in crises. (5) Separate books: yes, Medallion (short) against RIEF (long), never mixed. (6) Records: decades of cleaned data, every signal tested, one code base. (7) Almost all preparation: 310 staff, about 20 traders in 2000.
- Tests: sample size needed to tell an edge from luck; expectancy after measured slippage; rule-following against overrides; halving size in a drawdown.

## 12. What cannot be copied

- Leverage of 12 to 20 times without recourse; execution costs of an in-house desk; decades of proprietary data; 300 staff, 90 doctorates; tax treatment (now closed); the ability to pay out all profits and refuse outside money.

## 13. Prerequisites

- Statistical arbitrage; market-neutral; leverage and margin; Sharpe ratio; expectancy and the law of large numbers; backtest and overfitting; slippage and market impact; management and performance fees; gross and net returns; option, premium, barrier; short-term and long-term capital gains.
