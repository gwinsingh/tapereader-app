## 1. Snapshot

- Style: buys businesses and stocks of durable, well-run companies at sensible prices, holds for decades, pays for them with
  retained earnings and insurance float. Instruments: whole companies, listed stocks, preferred shares with warrants, Treasury bills.
- Holding period: years to decades. Coca-Cola held since 1988 (L-1988), American Express in the present form since the early 1990s (L-2022).
- Leverage: about 1.6 to 1 on average 1976–2011, financed largely by insurance float (NBER, abstract and section 4).
- Size: Berkshire total assets $1,303.9bn, shareholders' equity $761.4bn at 30 Sept 2026 (10-Q Q3 2026). Partnership had about $100m in 1969 (BPL, letter of 29 May 1969).
- **Status, November 2026:** age 96 (born 30 Aug 1930; letter of 18 Sept 2026 mentions his 96th birthday).
  - Announced 3 May 2025 he would hand over; board appointed Greg Abel president and CEO from 1 Jan 2026 (https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/news/may0525.pdf).
  - 10 Nov 2025 letter: "going quiet", no more annual report or long annual-meeting sessions; a yearly Thanksgiving message continues; says he feels good, moves slowly, reads with increasing difficulty, in the office five days a week (https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/news/nov1025.pdf).
  - L-2025 (first letter by Abel, 28 Feb 2026): Buffett is chairman, in the office five days a week; Abel holds responsibility for capital allocation and equity investments; Ted Weschler manages about 6% of investments; Todd Combs left for JPMorgan (https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/news/dec0825.pdf).
  - 2 May 2026 annual meeting: first led by Abel; Buffett sat in the front row and spoke briefly; arena about half full (AP via https://wtop.com/national/2026/05/berkshire-hathaways-profits-double-as-shareholders-gather-for-the-annual-meeting-on-saturday/, search summary).
  - **18 Sept 2026: became Chairman Emeritus, remains a director; son Howard G. Buffett elected chairman** (https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/news/sep1826.pdf).
  - Gifts 2026: 14 July, 8,000 A shares converted and given as 12m B shares to four family foundations; goal to dispose of all shares by 31 Dec 2034 (https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/news/jul1426.pdf). 16 Nov 2026, 6,150 A shares (9.225m B shares); he then owns 182,140 A shares and 1,162 B shares (https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/news/nov1626.pdf).
  - Net worth $148.9bn, Forbes, January 2026 (via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Buffett).
  - Health: stage I prostate cancer treated in 2012 (Wikipedia, same page). No new illness reported in 2026 sources opened.

## 2. Origin

- Born Omaha 30 Aug 1930; father a stockbroker and from 1942 a congressman (nov1025.pdf). Wharton 1947–49; BS University of Nebraska 1951; rejected by Harvard; MS Columbia 1951 under Benjamin Graham; Buffett-Falk 1951–54; Graham-Newman 1954–56 (Wikipedia).
- Bought *The Intelligent Investor* in 1949; "my financial life changed" (L-2013).
- 1951: put 65% of his $9,800 net worth into GEICO after a talk with Lorimer Davidson (L-2014). L-1994 says 70% of his portfolio.
- Three partnerships formed in 1956 (BPL, letter on 1957). Eleven entities, about $7.2m, merged into Buffett Partnership Ltd in 1962 (Wikipedia). The usual "$105,100 from seven partners" start: NOT FOUND in the letters opened (not verified).
- Fee: no fixed fee; one quarter of the gain above 6% a year (BPL, Jan 1966 letter).
- Met Charlie Munger in 1959 (L-2014, L-2023).
- Formative mistake: Berkshire Hathaway itself (see section 7).

## 4. Process

- **Finding ideas:** bottom-up, by reading. About five to six hours a day of newspapers and company reports (interview quoted by Farnam Street / TIME, https://time.com/3919319/warren-buffett-information-filter; the quote itself did not load, not verified). Deals arrive by letter or phone; Berkshire promises an answer "customarily within five minutes" (acquisition criteria, L-2014). Example: Bell Laboratories reached him by letter from its CEO in 2025 (L-2025); Forest River by letter on 21 June 2005 (L-2024).
- **Circle of competence:** its size matters little, knowing its edge matters (L-1996). He read IBM's annual report for 50 years before buying; drank Coca-Cola for 52 years before buying (L-2011, L-1989).
- **Moat:** a great business needs an enduring moat; he rules out industries of rapid change (L-2007).
- **Owner earnings:** reported earnings plus depreciation and amortisation, less the capital spending needed to hold position (L-1986).
- **Margin of safety:** used in the 1962 letter for the "generals" (BPL, letter of 24 Jan 1962).
- **Punch card:** earliest written source found is Munger's 1994 talk at USC business school, reporting what Buffett told students: a ticket with 20 slots for a lifetime (https://fs.blog/great-talks/a-lesson-on-worldly-wisdom/).
- **Timing:** no charts, no forecasts. "We never try to anticipate" fear or greed; "be fearful when others are greedy" (L-1986). Repeated in the New York Times, 17 Oct 2008, "Buy American. I Am.": buying US stocks for his personal account, until then all government bonds (https://www.nytimes.com/2008/10/17/opinion/17buffett.html, not opened; context from https://www.cnbc.com/2009/02/17/has-warren-buffett-been-buying-american-for-berkshire-hathaway-we-find-out-today.html, search summary). The S&P 500 closed at 940.55 that day, fell another 20% to 752.44 on 20 Nov 2008 and bottomed in March 2009 (same summary).
- **Sizing:** concentrated. Rule from Nov 1965: up to 40% of the partnership in one security when the facts are near certain (BPL, Jan 1966 letter). American Express was about 40% of the partnership, $13m for over 5% of the company (L-1994). Coca-Cola: $1,024m cost, 35% of the listed holdings at end 1989 (computed from L-1989: $1,804m of $5,188m). Apple: 49% of the stock portfolio at end 2023 (AR-2023).
- **Adds on weakness, not on strength:** Washington Post was down 25% at end 1974 and held (L-1985); Coca-Cola added in 1989 and 1994; Wells Fargo bought in a "chaotic market" in 1990 (L-1990); IBM: he wished the price would "languish" (L-2011); Japan added 2020–25. The three largest realised losses in 50 years (1–2% of net worth each) were in 1974–75, selling cheap stocks to buy cheaper ones (L-2014).
- **Managing:** no stops, no hedges. Holding period "forever" for outstanding businesses (L-1988).
- **Exiting:** on a changed view of the business or the price, or to fund something better. Record in section 7.
- **Borrowing:** warns against it (L-2017). But the partnership borrowed up to 25% of net worth against workouts (BPL, 1962 and 1964 letters), and Berkshire's float is leverage (NBER).

## 5. Risk and drawdowns

- Stated rules: never depend on strangers for cash; always ample cash (L-2008); no buybacks if cash would fall below $30bn (10-Q Q3 2026); no borrowed money to own Berkshire (L-2014, L-2017).
- Four "truly major dips" of Berkshire shares (L-2017): March 1973–Jan 1975, $93 → $38, −59.1%; 2–27 Oct 1987, $4,250 → $2,675, −37.1%; 19 June 1998–10 March 2000, $80,900 → $41,300, −48.9%; 19 Sept 2008–5 March 2009, $147,000 → $72,400, −50.7%.
- 2020: about −30% in February–March (https://www.fool.com/investing/2020/06/04/is-berkshire-hathaway-a-buy-while-its-down.aspx, search summary); $318,605 on 5 March to $251,000 on 20 March (https://rationalwalk.com/berkshire-hathaway-and-the-coronavirus-crash/).
- What he did: 1973–75 kept buying (Washington Post 1973; Forbes 1 Nov 1974 interview, reprints, not opened). 1987: bought Coca-Cola in 1988 (L-1988). 1998–2000: refused technology stocks, Fortune article Nov 1999. 2008–09: $14.5bn of preferreds, cash from $44.3bn to $30.6bn, BNSF (L-2008, L-2009); regretted not doing more (L-2009). 2020: sold the airlines, bought back $24.7bn of own stock in the year (XBRL).
- Never near ruin at Berkshire. Near-disasters: Salomon 1991, USAir 1994.
- Concentration: five holdings were 79% of the stock portfolio at end 2023, 71% at end 2024, 65% at end 2025 and at 30 Sept 2026 (AR-2023 to AR-2025, 10-Q Q3 2026).

## 7. Signature trades

Wins

1. **American Express, 1964–66.** After the salad-oil scandal. About 40% of the partnership, $13m, over 5% of the company (L-1994). Sale price in 1967–68: NOT FOUND in letters. Bought again 1991–95 for $1.3bn; 151.6m shares worth $56.1bn at end 2025, dividends $479m in 2025 (L-2022, L-2025).
2. **See's Candies, 1972.** $25m through Blue Chip Stamps; pre-tax earnings under $5m on $8m of capital; he nearly refused to go above $25m, sellers asked $30m (L-2007, L-2014). Pre-tax earnings since: $1.35bn to 2007, $1.9bn to 2014, with $32–40m reinvested. Lesson: the value of brands and pricing power.
3. **Washington Post, 1973.** $10.6m; down 25% at end 1974 ($8m); $221m at end 1985 (L-1985); $840.6m at end 1997 (L-1997); swapped for a business in 2014 (L-2014).
4. **GEICO, 1976 and 1995–96.** $45.7m by 1980 for 33.3% while the insurer was near insolvency; share rose to about 50% through buybacks; $2.3bn for the other half, closed 2 Jan 1996 (L-1995, L-1996).
5. **Coca-Cola, 1988–94.** $592.5m at end 1988, $1,024m at end 1989, $1.3bn by 1994 for what is now 400m shares. Dividend $75m in 1994, $816m in 2025; value $34.4bn at 30 Sept 2026 (L-1988, L-1989, L-2022, L-2025, 13F Q3 2026).
6. **Apple, 2016–.** Cost $6.7bn end 2016, $21.0bn end 2017, $36.0bn end 2018; $31.1bn for 907.6m shares after trims (L-2016 to L-2021). Value $174.3bn at end 2023 (905.6m shares, 13F). Sold to 300m shares by 30 Sept 2024 ($75.1bn at end 2024); 227.9m shares, $75.9bn at 30 Sept 2026 (13F filings). Total equity sales in 2024: $143.4bn against $9.2bn of purchases (XBRL). At the May 2024 meeting he tied the sales to the 21% tax rate (https://kiplinger.com/investing/why-did-warren-buffett-slash-his-stake-in-apple-stock, search summary).
7. **BYD, 2008–2025.** $232m for 225m shares (L-2017 table); $7.7bn at end 2021 (L-2021); sold from 2022, fully out by 2025 (https://fortune.com/2025/09/22/warren-buffett-sold-stake-byd-tesla/, search summary).
8. **Japanese trading houses, from 4 July 2019.** Five companies; cost $15.4bn, value $35.4bn at end 2025; financed with yen bonds at an average 1.2% (L-2023, L-2025).
9. **PetroChina, 2002–07.** $488m, sold for $4bn when market value reached his estimate (L-2007).

Losses and mistakes

1. **Berkshire Hathaway, 1962–65.** First shares at $7.50 in Dec 1962 as a cigar butt; refused a tender at $11.375 over an eighth of a point, bought control instead (May 1965), more than 25% of the partnership in a failing mill. Putting the insurance business inside it "diverted $100 billion or so" to strangers (L-2014). "My mistake" that "plagued us for two decades" (L-2024).
2. **Dexter Shoe, 1993.** $433m paid with 25,203 A shares; value went to zero; cost to shareholders $3.5bn at 2007 prices, $5.7bn at 2014 prices (L-2007, L-2014). Lesson: do not pay with shares.
3. **Salomon, 1987–97.** $700m of 9% preferred, convertible at $38 (L-1987); Treasury-auction scandal 1991, interim chairman from 18 Aug 1991 (L-1991); merged into Travelers 1997 (L-1997). Ended in profit.
4. **USAir, 1989.** $358m of 9.25% preferred (L-1989); dividend suspended, written down by $268.5m in 1994, "sloppy analysis" (L-1994); recovered with a profit by 1997–98 (L-1997).
5. **ConocoPhillips and two Irish banks, 2008.** Bought oil near the peak, cost "several billion"; Irish banks $244m → $27m (L-2008).
6. **Tesco, 2012–14.** 415m shares, cost $2.3bn; slow selling; after-tax loss $444m (L-2014).
7. **IBM, 2011–18.** 63.9m shares for $10.9bn (L-2011); out by May 2018 (https://www.cnbc.com/2018/05/04/warren-buffett-says-berkshire-hathaway-has-sold-completely-out-of-ibm.html, search summary). Roughly flat before dividends (secondary estimates, not verified).
8. **Kraft Heinz, 2013–.** Heinz 2013: $4.25bn for 53% of the common plus preferred shares later redeemed for $8.32bn; after the 2015 merger 325.4m shares cost $9.8bn (L-2015). Write-downs: $3bn in early 2019, when he said Berkshire overpaid for Kraft (Reuters, https://business-standard.com/article/reuters/warren-buffett-says-berkshire-overpaid-for-kraft-heinz-119022500786_1.html, search summary); $5.0bn pre-tax ($3.76bn after tax) in Q2 2025 (10-Q Q3 2026, nov0726.pdf). At 30 Sept 2026: 27.5% owned, market value $7.4bn, $1.4bn below carrying value (10-Q, 13F). Sept 2025: he said he was disappointed by the planned split (https://fortune.com/2025/09/03/warren-buffet-kraft-heinz-57-billion-faceplant/). Jan 2026: shares registered for possible resale; split later halted; Abel: no immediate sale (https://www.ttnews.com/articles/berkshire-kraft-heinz-stake, search summary). L-2025: return "well short of adequate".
9. **Precision Castparts, 2016.** $235 a share, $37.2bn with debt (https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/news/aug1015.pdf); $11bn write-down in 2020, "I paid too much" (L-2020).
10. **Airlines, 2016–20.** $7–8bn in four carriers; all sold in April 2020 at a large loss, "my mistake" (https://www.cnbc.com/2020/05/02/warren-buffett-says-berkshire-sold-its-entire-position-in-airlines-because-of-the-coronavirus.html, search summary). Under Abel the 13F for 30 Sept 2026 lists 64.45m Delta shares, $5.4bn.

Sales: McDonald's 1998 "a very big mistake" (L-1998). Freddie Mac and Fannie Mae nearly all sold in 2000 (L-2000). PetroChina 2007 at his value estimate. Wells Fargo: bought from 1989–90, about 10% stake, cut from 2017, out by Q1 2022 (https://www.investmentnews.com/wirehouses/buffett-exits-wells-fargo-stake-ending-decades-long-bet-on-bank/221579, search summary).

## 8. Criticism

- **Lagging since 2009:** computed above: 12.9% a year against 14.8% for 2009–25. His own words: "no possibility of eye-popping performance" (L-2023); "our size takes its toll" (nov1025.pdf).
- **Cash drag:** David Rolfe of Wedgewood Partners sold Berkshire in 2019, citing the cash pile, Kraft Heinz, IBM and missed winners (https://www.foxbusiness.com/money/berkshire-hathaway-investor-bails-buffett, search summary).
- **Missing technology:** "I blew it" on Google, "too dumb" on Amazon, 2017 meeting (https://www.cnbc.com/amp/2017/05/06/warren-buffett-admits-he-made-a-mistake-on-google.html, search summary). Berkshire now holds 106m Alphabet shares, $36.4bn (13F Q3 2026).
- **Sokol, 2011:** David Sokol bought 96,060 Lubrizol shares on 5–7 Jan 2011 before proposing the deal; Buffett's release of 30 March 2011 said neither thought the purchases unlawful (https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/news/mar3011.pdf). The audit committee on 27 April 2011 found a breach of company policy; critics including Janet Tavakoli faulted Buffett's first response (https://fortune.com/2011/04/27/berkshire-finally-scolds-sokol/, search summary).
- **Wells Fargo:** stayed a top holder through the 2016 fake-accounts scandal (above).
- **Image against practice:** David Dayen, The Nation, 15 Feb 2018: the fortune rests on moats that are market power (https://www.thenation.com/article/archive/special-investigation-the-dirty-secret-behind-warren-buffetts-billions/). ProPublica 2021: "true tax rate" 0.1% of wealth growth 2014–18, a method tax experts dispute (https://www.propublica.org/article/the-secret-irs-files-trove-of-never-before-seen-records-reveal-how-the-wealthiest-avoid-income-tax, search summary). ProPublica 2023: personal trades in stocks Berkshire was trading, at least three cases, $466m of personal sales 2000–19 (https://www.propublica.org/article/warren-buffett-privately-traded-stocks-berkshire-hathaway-ethics-irs, search summary).
- **Succession risk:** Berkshire +10.9% against +17.9% for the index in 2025, the hand-over year (L-2025).
- Legal: no regulatory action against him found.

## 9. Psychology and routine

- Office five days a week at 96; same house since 1958 (nov1025.pdf).
- Reviews mistakes in public: "mistake" or "error" 16 times in the 2019–23 letters (L-2024); "Mistakes of the First Twenty-five Years" (L-1989).
- Munger as the corrective voice; died 28 Nov 2023 (L-2023). "Thumb-sucking" = delaying the correction of a mistake (L-2014, L-2024).
- No journal documented.

## 10. Evolution and regime

## 11. Carry-over: answers to the seven questions

1. Sizing: large from the start when certain; adds on price weakness; never on a breakout. Cuts on a changed view of the business, slowly, and he calls the slowness a fault.
2. After losses: no size rule. Size follows opportunity, not recent results. Buys more in drawdowns.
3. Keeping gains: no targets, no trailing exits; sells on valuation (PetroChina), on a broken thesis (airlines, IBM), or for taxes and price (Apple 2024).
4. Market filter: denies timing; in practice the amount of cash follows the supply of bargains (1969, 1999, 2024–26). The indicator is valuation, not trend.
5. Separate books: yes in the partnership (generals, workouts, controls, reported separately: 1966 gains by category, BPL Jan 1967 letter). Personal account separate from Berkshire. A short-term trade that became permanent: Berkshire itself.
6. Records: yearly public letters with named mistakes since 1957.
7. Preparation against execution: almost all preparation; a handful of decisions a year.
