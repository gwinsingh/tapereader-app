## 1. Snapshot

- Style: concentrated value investing in out-of-favour stocks, plus occasional large bets against whole markets or sectors made with instruments whose loss is limited to the premium (credit default swaps in 2005–07, listed put options since 2021) [MSN][VF][EDGAR].
- Instruments: US and Asian small and mid-cap stocks; credit default swaps (2004–08); listed puts and calls; from 2025–26, by his own newsletter, also plain short positions (reported by CNBC, 28 Sep 2026: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/28/michael-burry-believes-the-ai-bubble-may-burst-sooner-than-he-first-believed.html). Private holdings in farmland and gold (Bloomberg TV interview reported by InvestmentNews, 12 Sep 2010: https://www.investmentnews.com/alternatives/guess-what-michael-burry-is-buying-now/31334).
- Holding period: MSN era, turnover above 50% a year, sells after a quick 40–50% gain [MSN]. Mortgage trade: about three years (May 2005 to about March 2008) [VF][FCIC]. 13F era: most positions held one to three quarters (rows range from 1 to 33 and the list changes almost completely within a year) [EDGAR].
- Leverage: early years none, and he avoided shorting stocks [VF]. Mortgage trade: notional far above fund assets (about $1.8bn of mortgage swaps and $6.6bn of corporate swaps against a fund of about $600m) but with loss limited to premiums [VAN][VF]. GameStop 13D says margin borrowing "may" be used [13D].
- Assets: Scion Capital $600m at end of 2004 [VF]; "peak about $1bn" [HFA] (single outlet). Scion Asset Management: about $343m in Sept 2019 (Bloomberg via InvestmentNews, 4 Sep 2019: https://www.investmentnews.com/real-life-investor-portrayed-in-the-big-short-sees-bubble-in-passive-investing-81165); $154,933,555 in four discretionary accounts in the Form ADV filed 28 Mar 2025 [IAPD].
- Status, Nov 2026: age 55 (born 19 June 1971; date via https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Burry citing History vs Hollywood). Scion Asset Management's SEC registration terminated 10 Nov 2025 [IAPD]; outside capital returned by end of 2025 per a letter dated 27 Oct 2025 (Financial Times via Irish Times, 14 Nov 2025: https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2025/11/14/big-short-investor-michael-burry-to-close-hedge-fund-as-he-warns-on-valuations). Since 24 Nov 2025 publishes the paid Substack newsletter "Cassandra Unchained" and trades his own money (https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/24/michael-burry-launches-newsletter-to-lay-out-his-ai-bubble-views-after-deregistering-hedge-fund.html). No conviction or regulatory sanction found; the adviser record shows no disclosure events [IAPD]. He kept a California physician's licence (Wikipedia, citing the state record).

## 2. Origin

- Own biography [MSN]: born in New York, grew up mostly in San Jose, California (Wikipedia gives San Jose as birthplace, citing Business Insider; his own text is closer to the primary record). UCLA: economics major with pre-med courses. Vanderbilt School of Medicine (enrolled 1993 by his own account [VAN]; MD 1997 per Wikipedia citing Vanderbilt Medicine). Neurology residency at Stanford from 1998 [VF]. "I left medicine on June 30, 2000" [MSN].
- Eye: lost his left eye to a childhood cancer at age two; has said so himself [VAN]. Asperger's syndrome: he has said he has it [VAN]; per Lewis the recognition followed his son's diagnosis, when Burry was 35 [VF].
- Early investing in public: message-board posts on Silicon Investor from 1996 (Wikipedia, no inline source; Moneyweek, 12 May 2017: https://moneyweek.com/466835/466835-2). Started a finance web site in early 1997, later ValueStocks.net, which he says Forbes twice named Best of the Web; freelance writing for MSN [MSN]. Lewis: blogged between midnight and 3 am while working 16-hour hospital shifts [VF].
- San Francisco Chronicle reported on 21 Feb 2000 that he was short Amazon (cited by Burry himself in his first Substack post, per CNBC 24 Nov 2025, above). (Chronicle article not opened.)
- MSN "Strategy Lab" rounds in 2000 and 2001: a public model portfolio with journal entries [MSN]. In the column that opened a later round he said his picks over the previous two rounds had returned just over 23% in about 14 months while the S&P 500 fell about 22% and the Nasdaq about 58% (via https://acquirersmultiple.com/2017/12/michael-burry-dont-be-seduced-by-too-good-to-be-true-higher-yield-investments/).
- Scion Capital: fund launched 1 Nov 2000 [L2000][VF]. At the start he had $40,000 of assets against $145,000 of student loans; a settlement after his father's death supplied $20,000 from his mother and $10,000 from each of three brothers [VF]. Gotham Capital (Joel Greenblatt) offered "a million dollars" for "a quarter" of the firm, paid after tax at his request; White Mountains paid $600,000 for a further piece and promised $10m to invest [VF]. His first letter records the stakes as options: Gotham Capital V a five-year option on 22.50% of the management company, White Mountains up to 15.44%; he had over $1m of his own money in the fund [L2000]. Named after Terry Brooks's *The Scions of Shannara* [VF].
- Fees: no asset-based fee, only actual expenses (well under 1% of assets) and a performance fee above a 6% net hurdle [VF][L2000]. Per a book summary he began charging a management fee after 2007 (https://www.shortform.com/blog/side-pocket-hedge-fund/) (not verified).
- Formative experience: the investor revolt of 2006–07 (section 5).

## 4. The process

- Finding ideas (early): screens of out-of-favour industries by enterprise value to EBITDA; then free cash flow, off-balance-sheet items, adjusted book value; ignores price-earnings ratios, distrusts return on equity, prefers minimal debt; "rare birds" (asset plays, arbitrage, stocks under two-thirds of net working capital less liabilities, Buffett-type franchises at good prices) [MSN]. "Ick investing": taking a special interest in stocks whose first reaction is disgust (letter of Oct 2001 quoted in [VF]); example Avanti Corp: $100m cash, $100m a year free cash flow, market value $250m while executives faced prison [VF].
- Thesis: margin of safety, aim to avoid permanent loss; no catalyst required for stocks [MSN]. For the mortgage trade the thesis had a mechanism and a date: two-year teaser rates on 2005 loans would reset in 2007 (example 6% to 11%) and cause defaults; he expected the trade to take about three years [VF].
- Timing the entry (early): "barebones technical analysis"; buy within 10% to 15% of a 52-week low that has shown support; rarely more than 15% above technical support (journal 9 Aug 2000) [MSN].
- Sizing: MSN era 12 to 18 stocks, fully invested [MSN]. 13F era: 1 to 33 rows; top five share positions between 29% and 100% of reported share value [EDGAR]. Largest known bet relative to capital: the 2005–07 swaps (above). Reported cost of the Sept 2025 Palantir puts: 50,000 contracts at $1.84 = $9.2m, against a reported fund of about $155m, i.e. about 6% (his X post, reported by Stocktwits: https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/big-short-investor-michael-burry-mocks-media-over-his-palantir-short-bet/cLPBq3CREdv ; and https://blog.syzgroup.com/slow-food-for-thought/michael-burry-beyond-the-big-short).
- Adds to winners or losers: in 2005 he added to the mortgage trade as banks allowed larger size ($10m, then $100m a trade) before it had moved either way [VF]; in 2026 he added Nvidia puts while earlier ones were losing (TheStreet via Yahoo, 11 Apr 2026: https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/big-short-michael-burry-sends-220700103.html). So: adds to positions that are against him when the thesis is intact.
- Managing: early rule, cut a stock that breaks to a new low, except rare birds; example: sold Caterpillar on 28 Sep 2000 as it broke to new lows [MSN]. Later: time-based management of options (sold December 2026 puts in Sept 2026 to avoid time decay; IBTimes UK, 10 Sep 2026: https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/michael-burry-exits-nvidia-put-options-ai-bubble-concerns-1818993); bought Nvidia calls in Aug 2026 as a hedge on his short (same source).
- Exiting: mortgage swaps sold into strength from mid-2007 to about March 2008, before the worst of the crisis [FCIC][Moneyweek]; index puts of mid-2023 gone within a quarter [EDGAR]; GameStop sold in Q4 2020 before the squeeze [EDGAR].
- Guesswork: how he sizes today, his real exposure and results since 2008. Nothing in filings shows cash, shorts, swaps, foreign stocks or private assets.

## 5. Risk and drawdowns

- Stated rules: margin of safety; no single position may ruin the portfolio; cut on a new low [MSN]. Preference for instruments with a defined maximum loss: "my downside was defined and certain" (his words on swaps, in [VF]).
- Worst drawdown on record: 2006, −18.16% [HFA] (−18.4% in summaries of Lewis's book) while the S&P 500 returned +15.79% with dividends; a gap of about 34 points. Cause: premiums on the swaps plus banks marking the swaps against him at each month end [VF].
- What he did: closed the Hong Kong office, cut pay, laid off staff (Lewis: half the staff), considered liquidating in December 2006, sold the corporate swaps (billions of notional) at low prices, kept the mortgage swaps and put them in a side pocket so they could not be redeemed [VAN][VF]. Investors threatened lawsuits [VAN]; Greenblatt pressed him to drop the trade and questioned the side pocket (book summary: https://www.litcharts.com/lit/the-big-short/chapter-8).
- Swap contracts carried triggers: a counterparty could end the deal if the fund's assets fell by set amounts (about 15% in a month, 30% in a quarter, 40% in a year as he described them) [FCIC]. So the redemptions were a real threat to the trade, not only to the business.
- Close to ruin: the fund, yes, in late 2006 by his own account [VAN]. Personally no.
- Concentration: see the 13F table in section 6.

## 7. Signature trades

### Win 1: the mortgage trade, 2005–08

- 2003: began studying mortgage pools and mortgage insurers [FCIC]. 2004: bought swaps on companies exposed to housing [VF]. 19 Mar 2005: idea of swaps on subprime mortgage bonds; he judged Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, Deutsche Bank, Bank of America, UBS, Merrill Lynch and Citigroup likeliest to survive and did not call Bear Stearns or Lehman Brothers [VF].
- 19 May 2005: first trades, $60m of swaps from Deutsche Bank, $10m on each of six bonds [VF] ([VAN]: agreed in May, executed early June). Price by rating: about 0.20% a year on AAA, 0.50% on A, 2% on BBB tranches; BBB tranches would be wiped out by pool losses of about 7% [VF]. [FCIC]: roughly 0.85% to 2.75% a year on the tranches he bought.
- Late June 2005: Goldman raised the trade size to $100m. End of July 2005: $750m. October 2005 letter: "at least a billion dollars". Fund about $600m [VF].
- Peak notional by his own later figures: about $1.8bn of mortgage bonds and about $6.6bn of corporate credit (AIG, Countrywide, Washington Mutual, Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac among them), nine dealers [VAN]. FCIC staff, reading a 2007 letter, put the peak short at $6.7bn in late 2006 [FCIC]. ## 13. What carries over: the seven questions

1. Sizing up when right, cutting when wrong: early rule, cut on a new low; later, size through loss-limited instruments (premium at risk). Adds when the thesis is intact even if the price is against him (2005, 2026). No evidence of adding on strength.
2. After a string of losses: 2006: cut costs, sold other positions to keep the main one; 2025: returned outside money. No rule for reducing size found.
3. Keeping gains: sells into strength (40–50% pops in 2000; swaps in 2007–08; GameStop in 2020). No trailing exit found. Leaves large gains behind (GameStop).
4. Market-wide filter: explicitly none in 2001 ("I care little about the level of the general market" [MSN]); later he forms strong views about bubbles and acts on them with puts.
5. Separate books: a side pocket for the swaps in 2006; in 2019–20 two funds and a separate account held GameStop [13D]. No short-term trade known to have become a long holding; the Tesla puts were "a trade".
6. Record-keeping: public journals with reasons for each buy and sell (MSN 2000–01); long quarterly letters.
7. Preparation against execution: almost all preparation: reading documents for months before a handful of trades.
