## 1. Snapshot

- **Style in one line:** systematic global macro. Decision rules about how economies work are written down, coded and run
  across many markets; a second, passive product (All Weather) holds a risk-balanced mix of assets.
- **Instruments and markets:** more than 150 liquid markets: stocks, bonds, currencies, commodities, largely through
  futures and swaps (II-2020). Fortune counted "roughly 100 categories" in 2009, with the weight on currencies and fixed
  income (F-2009).
- **Holding period:** NOT FOUND as a stated figure (looked in AWS, F-2009, II articles). One observed case: a short
  position on European stocks grew to $10.5bn in June 2022 and was below $1bn by mid-August 2022, about two months
  (https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/bridgewater-doubles-bet-against-european-stocks-to-10-5-billion-1.1782741 ;
  https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-08-17/bridgewater-cuts-back-european-short-wagers-to-below-1-billion).
- **Leverage:** Pure Alpha about 4 to 1 in 2009 (F-2009). Volatility targets of 12% and 18% a year for the two Pure Alpha
  versions (HFJ; https://www.hedgeweek.com/bridgewater-aum-down-by-18-as-firm-shrinks-flagship-fund/). The retail All
  Weather ETF showed notional exposures adding to about 194% of assets on 20 Nov 2026 (own sum of the four lines on
  https://www.ssga.com/us/en/intermediary/etfs/spdr-bridgewater-all-weather-etf-allw).
- **Assets at peak:** in the range $160–170bn. $163bn at end-February 2020
  (https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2020-06-15/bridgewater-s-assets-shrank-15-in-virus-fueled-trading-slump);
  about $162bn on 30 Sep 2019 (WIKI-BW); "about $160bn" in Nov 2017
  (https://finance.yahoo.com/news/bridgewater-world-apos-largest-hedge-160548233.html). The often-quoted "$168bn" peak
  is `(not verified)`: sources disagree on the year (2019 or 2022) and none fetched is primary.
- **Assets now:** about $102bn in mid-2026 (R-H1-26; HW-JUN26; HW-OCT26). $92.1bn at end-2024 after an 18.1% fall in
  that year (https://www.hedgeweek.com/bridgewater-aum-down-by-18-as-firm-shrinks-flagship-fund/, citing a regulatory
  filing reported by Reuters). $92bn at end-September 2025 (II-2026).
- **Status, November 2026:** Dalio (born 8 Aug 1949, age 77; WIKI-RD) no longer manages outside money. He gave up
  voting control on 30 Sep 2022 (F-CTRL), sold his last shares and left the board in July 2025
  (https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ray-dalio-sells-remaining-stake-214411051.html). He runs the Dalio Family Office,
  writes, and warns in public about debt. Not convicted of anything; no regulatory action found (see section 8).

## 2. Origin

- Born in Jackson Heights, Queens; raised in Manhasset, Long Island. BA in finance, C.W. Post College (Long Island
  University). MBA, Harvard Business School, 1973 (WIKI-RD; HFJ).
- Says he was hooked on investing at age 12 (II-2017).
- Clerk on the New York Stock Exchange floor in August 1971 when Nixon ended dollar–gold convertibility. He expected
  stocks to fall; the Dow rose almost 4% the next day. Lesson he states: a single lifetime of experience is too narrow, so
  study history (AWS, p. 2).
- Director of commodities at Dominick & Dominick; then futures trader and broker at Shearson Hayden Stone in 1974;
  dismissed after hitting his boss at a New Year's Eve party in 1974 (WIKI-RD; single source for the dismissal).
- **1975:** founded Bridgewater in his New York apartment (AWS p. 3 calls it a brownstone apartment; principles.com
  calls it a two-bedroom apartment: https://www.principles.com/principles/). First business: risk consulting for companies and a
  paid daily letter, *Bridgewater Daily Observations* (AWS p. 3).
- Early client work: a synthetic hedge of corn and soymeal so a chicken producer could quote McDonald's a fixed price;
  the McNugget launched in 1983 (AWS p. 3). Managed up to $700m of corporate liabilities before managing assets (AWS p. 3).
- **1981:** office moved to Connecticut (WIKI-BW).
- **1982, the formative mistake.** He had concluded that US banks had lent emerging countries more than could be
  repaid, and warned of a depression as bad as the 1930s. Mexico defaulted in August 1982. He testified to Congress and
  appeared on *Wall Street Week*. The Federal Reserve eased, stocks rose and the economy recovered. He lost his own and
  clients' money, let every employee go, was the firm's only employee, and borrowed $4,000 from his father (II-2017;
  FERRISS; https://fortune.com/2025/08/13/billionaire-ray-dalio-broke-early-career-borrowed-4000-from-dad/).
  - **Origin of the story:** his own later retelling (*Principles*, 2017; TED talk, April 2017; interviews). The
    testimony and TV appearance are public events; the $4,000 loan and the scale of the loss rest on his account only.
    No audited figure for the 1982 loss exists. Grade D for the number, B for the event.
  - **What he says it taught him:** (a) stop asking whether he is right and ask "how do I know I'm right?" (FERRISS);
    (b) look for the smartest people who disagree; (c) diversify so the same expected return comes with less risk;
    (d) study periods before his own lifetime (II-2017).
- **1987:** the World Bank pension fund opened a $5m bond account; first asset-management mandate. The benchmark was
  treated as the neutral position and the active bets as a separate "alpha" (AWS p. 3). WIKI-BW names the World Bank
  chief investment officer as Hilda Ochoa-Brillembourg.
- **1990:** memo to Rusty Olson, pension chief of a large US consumer-goods maker (identified elsewhere as Kodak:
  WIKI-BW, WIKI-RD), on balancing stocks with long-duration bonds at equal risk; this became the first "alpha overlay"
  account (AWS pp. 4–5). The AWS paper itself does not name Kodak.
- **1991:** Pure Alpha launched (AWS p. 6; II-2020 dates inception to December 1991). WIKI-BW's product section says 1989;
  the firm's own paper says 1991, which is the primary record.
- **1996:** All Weather launched, at first only for Dalio's family trust (AWS pp. 2, 7–8).
- **1997:** Bridgewater advised the US Treasury on the design of inflation-protected bonds (TIPS) (AWS p. 7).

## 4. The process, step by step

- **Finding ideas:** top-down. A Monday meeting called "What's Going on in the World" with about 75 people and a
  600-page research pack sent on Fridays (II-2017). The daily letter is still produced (AWS p. 3).
- **Forming a thesis:** look for "another one of those": match today to a historical type of event, then apply rules
  that held in past cases (AWS p. 2). The economic model: productivity growth plus a short-term debt cycle of about 5–8
  years plus a long-term debt cycle of about 75–100 years; four ways to reduce debt (spending cuts, defaults and
  restructuring, redistribution, money printing); three rules of thumb: debt should not rise faster than income, income
  should not rise faster than productivity, raise productivity (video page: https://www.economicprinciples.org/how-the-economic-machine-works ;
  transcript: https://singjupost.com/economy-really-work-ray-dalio-transcript/).
  - Example: research on past deleveragings led the firm to see a bubble in late 2006 and to warn in 2007 of reckless
    lending; positions in 2006–07 were long government bonds, yen and gold and short risky assets (II-2013).
- **Timing the entry:** by rule, not by chart pattern. No source shows Dalio using price charts for entries. The
  crisis gauge triggered on a date (30 Sep 2008), after which the fund went to "high alert" (F-2009). A reviewer of
  Copeland's book says trend following gave Bridgewater part of its early edge (search summary of
  https://www.toptradersunplugged.com/?p=14181; `(not verified)` in a fetched page).
- **Sizing:** by risk, not by dollars or conviction. Each return stream is scaled so the whole portfolio hits a
  volatility target (12% or 18%). Typical count of trades open at once: 30 to 40 (ValueWalk excerpt, weak source:
  https://es.scribd.com/document/272032249/Valuewalk-com-Ray-Dalio-Part-Two-Bridgewater-Grows). Largest position as a
  share of capital: NOT FOUND (looked in F-2009, II-2013, Bloomberg position reports). Largest disclosed: shorts on
  European companies of $22bn (2018), $14bn (2020) and $10.5bn (June 2022)
  (https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/bridgewater-doubles-bet-against-european-stocks-to-10-5-billion-1.1782741).
  - Adds to winners or losers: one documented case of adding with the move. The 2022 Europe short nearly doubled in a
    week while markets were falling (same source). No documented case of averaging down found.
- **Managing:** no standing bias to be long or short (II-2013). Risk was cut in March 2020 and stayed low until about
  August 2020 (F-2020). No use of price stop-loss orders found (NOT FOUND; looked in F-2009, II-2013, FERRISS).
- **Exiting:** when the rules change sign. No profit targets found. The Europe short was largely closed within two
  months (Bloomberg, 17 Aug 2022, link above).
- **Systemising:** he writes down the criteria for each decision, turns them into algorithms, and runs the computer's
  decision next to his own (FERRISS). By 1990–91 he says he had roughly a thousand tested rules to choose from; the best
  were back-tested together over long periods and, on paper, raised return per unit of risk by three to five times (HG).

### The Holy Grail chart (exact contents)

- Made about 1990–91 by Brian Gold, a Dartmouth maths graduate who joined in 1990 (HG).
- X axis: number of assets or alphas in the portfolio, 1 to 20. Y axis: annual portfolio standard deviation, 10% down to 1%.
  Five lines for average correlation between the assets: 60%, 40%, 20%, 10%, 0%. Each asset has 10% risk.
- Right-hand scale printed on the chart: return-to-risk ratio and probability of losing money in a year at each risk level:

| Portfolio risk | Return / risk | Chance of a losing year |
|---|---|---|
| 10% | 0.25 | 40% |
| 9% | 0.28 | 39% |
| 8% | 0.31 | 38% |
| 7% | 0.36 | 36% |
| 6% | 0.42 | 34% |
| 5% | 0.50 | 31% |
| 4% | 0.63 | 26% |
| 3% | 0.83 | 20% |
| 2% | 1.25 | 11% |
| 1% | 2.50 | 1% |

  (The scale implies each asset earns 2.5% a year above cash with 10% risk.)
- His claims: 15 to 20 good uncorrelated streams sharply cut risk without cutting expected return (HG); a thousand
  stocks that are 60% correlated diversify little more than five (HG); 15 uncorrelated bets cut risk by more than 80%
  and raise return-to-risk about fivefold (FERRISS, paraphrased).
- **Own arithmetic (formula: risk = 10% × √(1/N + ρ(1 − 1/N))), not from a source:**

| Assets (N) | ρ = 0 | ρ = 0.1 | ρ = 0.2 | ρ = 0.4 | ρ = 0.6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10.0 | 10.0 | 10.0 | 10.0 | 10.0 |
| 2 | 7.1 | 7.4 | 7.7 | 8.4 | 8.9 |
| 3 | 5.8 | 6.3 | 6.8 | 7.7 | 8.6 |
| 5 | 4.5 | 5.3 | 6.0 | 7.2 | 8.2 |
| 10 | 3.2 | 4.4 | 5.3 | 6.8 | 8.0 |
| 15 | 2.6 | 4.0 | 5.0 | 6.6 | 7.9 |
| 20 | 2.2 | 3.8 | 4.9 | 6.6 | 7.9 |

  At N = 15 and zero correlation, risk falls 74% and return-to-risk rises 3.9 times. "About 80%" and "five times" need
  about 25 streams. The spoken figures round up; the printed chart is consistent with the formula.

### All Weather and risk parity (exact contents)

- return = cash + beta + alpha. Betas are few, cheap and pay more than cash over time; alpha is a zero-sum contest (AWS p. 4).
- A typical institutional portfolio has about 60% of its dollars in stocks and so almost all of its risk in stocks (AWS p. 4).
  - **Illustration (own arithmetic, assumed inputs, not Bridgewater data):** stocks at 16% volatility, bonds at 6%,
    zero correlation. Risk shares = (weight × volatility)². Stocks: (0.6 × 16)² = 92.2; bonds: (0.4 × 6)² = 5.8. Stocks
    carry 94% of the portfolio's variance with 60% of the money.
- Seen as return per unit of risk, asset classes are about equal; a low-risk asset can be levered up to stock-like risk (AWS p. 5).
- Four boxes: growth rising or falling, inflation rising or falling, each against what markets expect. 25% of risk in each (AWS p. 7):

| | Growth | Inflation |
|---|---|---|
| **Rising** | Equities, commodities, corporate credit, emerging-market credit | Inflation-linked bonds, commodities, emerging-market credit |
| **Falling** | Nominal bonds, inflation-linked bonds | Equities, nominal bonds |

- Leverage footnote: $10 in the S&P 500 and $10 in bonds is dominated by stock risk; $5 in stocks and $15 in 10-year
  bonds is more balanced but returns less; add some leverage to the $5/$15 mix and it matches the stock return with less
  risk (AWS p. 8, note 2).
- The strategy is described as passive: the best portfolio they could build with no forecast (AWS p. 3).
- "Risk parity" was a consultant's label, adopted later (AWS p. 8).
- **The popular "All Seasons" version** (Tony Robbins, *Money: Master the Game*, 2014, from a conversation with Dalio):
  30% stocks, 40% long-term Treasuries, 15% intermediate Treasuries, 7.5% gold, 7.5% commodities, no leverage
  (https://awealthofcommonsense.com/back-testing-tony-robbins-weather-portfolio/ ;
  https://www.optimizedportfolio.com/all-weather-portfolio/).
- **How the real thing differs:** leverage; inflation-linked bonds; global markets; weights set by risk, not fixed
  percentages. Evidence: the State Street Bridgewater All Weather ETF (ticker ALLW, launched 5 Mar 2025, expense ratio
  0.85%, $1.72bn on 20 Nov 2026) held global nominal bonds 75.71%, inflation-linked bonds 43.63%, global equities 42.24%,
  commodities 32.29% of assets; target volatility 10–12%; since inception to 31 Oct 2026 +12.47% a year at NAV against
  +18.92% for the MSCI ACWI IMI world stock index; 2026 to 31 Oct +6.23% against +12.50%
  (https://www.ssga.com/us/en/intermediary/etfs/spdr-bridgewater-all-weather-etf-allw). In 2009 the fund's mix was
  reported as 40% inflation-linked bonds, 30% T-bills, 20% Treasury bonds, 10% gold (WIKI-BW; single source).

## 5. Risk and drawdowns

- **Stated rules:** no systematic directional bias (II-2013). Volatility targets of 12% / 18% (HFJ). A stated personal
  drawdown limit of one third, with the arithmetic that a 50% loss needs a 100% gain to recover (HFJ). Target
  return-to-risk ratio near 1 (HFJ).
- **Worst drawdowns:**
  - 1982: near ruin; firm reduced to its founder (II-2017). Size unknown.
  - PA II, 2020: about −20% in the first quarter (Bloomberg, 3 Apr 2020: https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2020-04-03/bridgewater-s-main-hedge-fund-loses-about-20-in-first-quarter);
    −21% by mid-March per Dalio's client note, All Weather −14% at the same date (Business Insider on the note:
    https://businessinsider.in/finance/news/bridgewaters-all-weather-and-pure-alpha-funds-are-down-double-digits-heres-what-billionaire-ray-dalio-just-told-investors-about-his-coronavirus-game-plan-/amp_articleshow/74697664.cms);
    −18.6% for the year through August (F-2020); −12.6% for the year (https://fortune.com/2022/07/06/how-ray-dalio-flagship-hedge-fund-beat-bear-market-in-first-half-2022/).
    PA 12%: worst drawdown 13%, in 2020, and no other double-digit drawdown (HFJ).
  - PA II, Oct–Nov 2022: about −20% in two months, cutting the year's gain from 34.6% (30 Sep) to 7.8% (30 Nov)
    (F-CTRL for September; Bloomberg December 2022 report via search summary, original copy at
    https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/bridgewater-erases-most-of-its-2022-gains-after-two-month-rout-1.1854089.amp.html
    returned 404 on 23 Nov 2026).
  - PA II, 2023: −7.6%, all of it in the last two months (https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-01-04/bridgewater-s-flagship-pure-alpha-fund-lost-7-6-last-year).
  - All Weather: about −20% in 2008 (F-2009); −3.9% in 2013 (https://fortune.com/2014/01/09/ray-dalios-all-weather-fund-goes-cold/);
    −22% in 2022 for the 10%-volatility version (F-RP); −27.2% for 2022 to 30 Sep (F-CTRL).
- **What they did in and after drawdowns:** 1982: cut the firm to nothing and rebuilt with rules. 2020: cut risk in
  March, were slow to add it back and missed the rebound; risk back to normal by about August; dozens of staff cut;
  clients withdrew a net $3.5bn in seven months; a later Bloomberg headline refers to "model tweaks" (F-2020;
  https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2020-11-06/bridgewater-loss-stuck-at-18-6-in-main-fund-after-model-tweaks).
  After 2022–23: capped Pure Alpha at 20–30% below its roughly $100bn peak, cut about 100 jobs (8% of about 1,300),
  created an AI unit (https://www.hedgeweek.com/bridgewater-cap-funds-and-cut-jobs/). Pure Alpha was cut to $61bn
  against an internal target of $50–60bn (https://www.hedgeweek.com/bridgewater-aum-down-by-18-as-firm-shrinks-flagship-fund/).
- **Concentration:** not disclosed for the whole book. US-listed stock holdings (13F) were $22.99bn on 30 Sep 2026;
  largest lines: S&P 500 ETF SPY 11.29%, IVV 5.02%, Nvidia 2.67%, Broadcom 2.01%, Lam Research 1.51%
  (https://www.ctee.com.tw/news/20261117700900-430702). The 13F leaves out futures, bonds, currencies and shorts, so it
  shows a small part of the portfolio.

## 11. What carries over, by time frame

**Investing (years)**
- Balance by risk, not by dollars. Evidence: AWS pp. 4–8. Application: look at how much of a portfolio's swings come
  from stocks alone (in a 60/40 mix, almost all).
- Separate the passive holding from the active bets (cash + beta + alpha, AWS p. 4). Evidence: his own trust money went
  into All Weather, not Pure Alpha (AWS p. 7–8); his family office's 13F at end-2025 was $503m, over three-quarters in a
  gold ETF, plus Treasury and index ETFs (https://www.bloomberglinea.com/mercados/family-office-de-ray-dalio-revela-una-apuesta-de-us500-millones-por-acciones-de-eeuu/).
- Accept that a balanced portfolio lags stocks in stock booms and still has losing years (−22% in 2022).
- Public forecasts by a famous investor are a poor timing tool: 1982, 2018, 2020 calls against the fund's own results.

**Swing trading (days to weeks)**
- Count independent bets, not positions. Evidence: HG chart: assets that are 60% correlated stop diversifying after
  about five. Application: five leading stocks from one sector in one market behave like one or two bets.
- A regime filter decides how hard to press. Evidence: growth/inflation boxes; depression gauge that changed the
  fund's mode on 30 Sep 2008 (F-2009).
- Giving back gains is unsolved even here: +34.6% → about +9% in Q4 2022; all of 2023's loss in two months. The
  process had no visible trailing rule.

**Day trading (minutes to hours)**
- Write the reason and the criteria before the trade, then test the rule on history (FERRISS; Fortune 13 Aug 2025).
- Log every mistake and review it when calm (issue log; Pain Button; FERRISS).
- Run the rule and the discretionary decision side by side and compare (FERRISS).

**The seven questions**
1. *Sizing up when right, cutting when wrong; adding to winners?* Size comes from risk and correlation, not conviction;
   1982 was the conviction bet and he designed the system against repeating it (HG; II-2017). One case of adding with
   the move: Europe short nearly doubled in a week in June 2022. One case of cutting: March 2020 (F-2020). No rule for
   adding on pullbacks found.
2. *After a string of losses?* A stated drawdown limit of one third (HFJ). In 2020 risk was cut after the loss and
   restored about five months later; the cut meant missing the rebound (F-2020). No published step-down formula: NOT FOUND.
3. *Keeping gains?* No targets, no trailing exit found. Positions change when rules change. The record shows large
   give-backs (2022, 2023).
4. *Market-wide filter?* Yes. The whole method is a filter: where growth, inflation and central-bank liquidity stand
   against what is priced in, plus a crisis gauge (AWS p. 6; F-2009).
5. *Separate books by horizon?* Yes: Pure Alpha (active) and All Weather (passive) are separate funds. One early
   client, a car maker's pension plan, ended with about 70% beta (All Weather) and 30% alpha (Pure Alpha) (AWS p. 8).
   A short-term trade becoming a long-term holding: NOT FOUND.
6. *Record-keeping?* Written criteria for every decision since the early 1980s; rules coded and back-tested; issue log;
   Dot Collector; Daily Observations as a daily written record of views since 1975.
7. *Preparation against execution?* NOT FOUND in hours. The structure implies nearly all preparation: research and rule
   building by hundreds of people; execution by system. Only "a few hundred" of about 2,000 staff worked on investing
   at the peak (https://rationalreminder.ca/podcast/292).

**Tests worth running (hypotheses)**
- H1: On days or weeks when three or more open positions are in the same sector or all move with the index, the
  account's swing is close to that of one position three times the size. Check: pairwise correlation of position
  returns from a trade log; compare result variance on "one-theme" days against "mixed" days.
- H2: Trades with a reason and exit criteria written before entry have a higher average result in R than trades
  without. Check: tag the two groups in a journal and compare expectancy and spread.
- H3: A simple market-state filter (for example index above or below a moving average, or a 2×2 of index direction and
  volatility rising or falling) separates the periods when gains were kept from the periods when they were given back.
  Check: label each trade by the box it was opened in; compare peak-to-exit give-back by box.
- H4: Cutting size after a drawdown lowers the depth of the drawdown and also lowers the recovery (as in 2020). Check:
  replay a trade history at half size after N consecutive losses or after a loss of X R, and compare both numbers.

## 12. What cannot be copied

- Scale and staff: about 1,300 employees in 2023 (Hedgeweek, March 2023), about 2,000 at peak (Copeland); decades of
  coded rules and data.
- Leverage terms and instruments: 4:1 with futures and swaps across 150 markets; All Weather's bond leverage.
- Institutional terms: 2 and 20 fees, long client tenure (12 years average, Semafor), clients who stayed through 13
  weak years. This patience funded the 2025 recovery.
- Access: central banks, sovereign funds, finance ministries as clients and contacts.
- Truly uncorrelated streams: currencies, rates, commodities, credit, equities in many countries. A stock-only account
  sits on the 40–60% correlation lines of the chart, where extra positions add little.
- The record's best years (2008, 2010) depended on leveraged bond and currency positions unavailable at small size.

## 13. Prerequisites

- **Global macro:** trading whole markets (stock indexes, bonds, currencies, commodities) on views about economies.
- **Systematic:** decisions made by written rules run by computer, not case-by-case judgement.
- **Alpha and beta:** beta is the return from simply holding a market; alpha is the extra return from skill.
- **Correlation:** how closely two things move together, from −1 (opposite) through 0 (unrelated) to +1 (together).
- **Volatility / standard deviation:** the usual size of an asset's swings, in percent a year.
- **Return-to-risk ratio (Sharpe ratio):** return above cash divided by volatility.
- **Leverage:** borrowing, or using futures, to hold more than the account's cash.
- **Risk parity:** sizing each asset so it contributes the same amount of risk, which means holding more of calm assets.
- **Nominal and inflation-linked bonds:** ordinary bonds pay fixed money; inflation-linked bonds (TIPS in the US) rise with prices.
- **Deleveraging:** an economy-wide reduction of debt, by cuts, defaults, redistribution or money printing.
- **60/40:** 60% stocks and 40% bonds, the standard yardstick portfolio.
- **Drawdown:** the fall from a peak in account value to the following low.
- **13F and Form ADV:** US filings; 13F lists US-listed stock holdings each quarter; ADV describes an adviser's business.
- **LCH ranking:** a yearly estimate of dollars earned for clients, after fees, since each hedge fund began.
