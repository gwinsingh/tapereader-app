## 1. Snapshot

- Style: builder and chief executive of a multi-strategy hedge fund (many specialist teams, each kept close to market-neutral, under central risk control, with leverage), and majority owner of a separate market maker. Not a single-book trader since the 1990s.
- Citadel instruments: stocks long and short, government bonds, interest-rate derivatives, currencies, corporate credit and convertibles, commodities including physical natural gas, quantitative strategies [BRO]. Five strategies: equities; fixed income and macro; commodities; credit; quantitative [BRO][RISK23].
- Holding period: NOT FOUND as a firm figure; ranges from intraday (quantitative) to months (fundamental equity, commodities).
- Leverage now: regulatory (gross) assets $570,621,709,022 [ADV] against $65,017,503,000 of net assets under management at 31 Dec 2025 [BRO] = about 8.8 times. Gross regulatory assets include borrowed positions, much of it government bonds financed by repo (Citadel Global Fixed Income Master Fund Ltd alone: $331.3bn gross [ADV]); the figure is balance-sheet size, not economic risk. Brochure: sub-funds "generally invest on a highly leveraged basis" with no set limit [BRO].
- Leverage in the past: stock positions 3 to 6 times (2001) [II01]. October 2008: a blog rumour said 13:1, the firm said 4:1 [F08]; "7:1" in Chicago Magazine, June 2011, as cited by [WP-C] (not verified, article not opened).
- Assets: $4.6m at the start, Nov 1990 [BM05] (the same article also gives $4.2m); about $200m in 1994 [II01]; $2bn by 1998, $6bn in 2001 [II01]; about $12bn in 2005 [BM05]; $13bn April 2007 [F07]; $16bn Nov 2007 (Bloomberg via InvestmentNews, 26 Nov 2007: https://www.investmentnews.com/alternatives/citadel-said-to-book-27-return/12519); $15bn Dec 2008 [F08]; $24bn Feb 2016 (https://fortune.com/2016/02/18/citadel-is-cutting-jobs/); $62.3bn end 2022 [LCH23]; $65.0bn at 31 Dec 2025 [BRO]; about $69bn early June 2026 (Hedgeweek, 3 Jul 2026: https://www.hedgeweek.com/citadel-posts-broad-gains-as-tactical-trading-strategy-sidesteps-quant-selloff/); about $71bn in August 2026 (Reuters, 5 Aug 2026, as carried by KFGO: https://kfgo.com/?p=1337262). Forbes shows $77bn on 23 Nov 2026 (https://www.forbes.com/profile/ken-griffin/); the basis of that figure is not stated.
- Headcount: Citadel Advisors 3,284 employees, 1,314 in investment functions [ADV]. About 5,000 across both firms, in "hundreds of teams of six to 10" [F26]. Citadel Securities about 1,800 (2025) [WP-CS].
- Status, Nov 2026: age 58 (born 15 Oct 1968). Founder, Chief Executive Officer and Co-Chief Investment Officer of Citadel [BRO]; founder and principal shareholder of Citadel Securities [TEST] (non-executive chairman; CEO is Peng Zhao since 2017 [WP-CS]). Still managing outside money. Forbes net worth $54.9bn on 23 Nov 2026, rank 34 (Forbes profile above).
- Ownership: Griffin owned roughly 80% of Citadel Securities after the January 2022 stake sale (Bloomberg, 11 Jan 2022, headline and search summary: https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2022-01-11/griffin-fortune-soars-to-28-billion-on-citadel-securities-deal) (not verified); [WP-G] says about 80% of Citadel LLC. [BRO]: Citadel principals and related persons own "a substantial majority" of Citadel Securities.

## 2. Origin

- Born in Daytona Beach, Florida; raised in Boca Raton; father a project manager at General Electric; ran a small educational-software mail-order business in high school [F07][WP-G].
- Harvard College 1986–89, economics, graduated in three years [F07].
- First trade: put options on Home Shopping Network in 1986, about $5,000 profit [II01].
- **The satellite dish.** Earliest account found: [II01], Sept 2001. In autumn 1987 a dish for real-time quotes hung outside his third-floor window at Cabot House, as recalled by the former senior tutor Julian Chang; Harvard barred student businesses but the house treated his Florida partnership as off-campus activity; Griffin: "I can't believe they let me put it up." By 2007 Fortune tells it as "the story goes" [F07]. The source is therefore a named Harvard official plus Griffin, 14 years after the event.
- First funds: Convertible Hedge Fund #1, $265,000 (his grandmother among the investors; a retiree, Saul Golkin, $50,000); short going into the 19 Oct 1987 crash and profited; Convertible Hedge Fund #2 then raised $750,000 [II01]. A former Computerland salesman, Rush Simonson, sued in 2006 claiming he wrote the convertible-pricing program; he dropped the suit in January 2007 and Griffin acknowledged they had been partners [F07].
- Frank Meyer, Glenwood (Chicago fund of funds): gave him $1m to trade after graduation [F07]; "70% in a year" is from Chicago Magazine 2011 as cited by [WP-G] (not verified).
- Founding: sources differ. (a) 1 November 1990 with $4.6m [BM05; Institutional Investor, 24 June 2008: https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b18bl3cwkhgvn6/kenneth-griffin; NYT DealBook 11 Aug 2011 via [WP-C]]. (b) [II01]: began trading convertible arbitrage for Glenwood in September 1990 and raised $18m for Wellington Partners in 1991, opening with five people; [F26] also says the first fund had $18m. Both can be true: $4.6m at the start in November 1990, $18m once Wellington Partners was raised. [BRO] says only "in the investment advisory business since 1990". Firm first named Wellington Financial Group, renamed Citadel in 1994 [WP-C].
- Formative loss, early: 1994, Wellington −4.3%; about a third of capital withdrawn; assets fell to about $120m [II01][BM05]. Response: from 1 August 1998 investors had to accept long lock-ups or leave [II01].
- Formative loss, main: 2008 (section 5).

- His own statement of the edge: a strategy built on competitive "moats", a team that learns fast, sector expertise; "We're here to win" [GSB25]. Even his best stock pickers are right about 54% of the time and the market-making win rate is about 52% [GSB25]: the edge per decision is small and is multiplied by volume, diversification and leverage.

## 4. The process (how the firm is organised and run)

- **Idea generation is decentralised.** Portfolio managers with small teams run separate books within one of the five strategies. Equities has several units: Citadel Global Equities (2001), Surveyor Capital (2008), Ashler Capital (2018), plus international (Citadel website, via search summary: https://www.citadel.com/what-we-do/equities/) (not verified, site blocks automated reading). Surveyor had about 31 portfolio managers and Ashler 10 in 2020 by a count of LinkedIn profiles (https://www.efinancialcareers.co.uk/news/2020/02/surveyor-capital-ashler-capital-jobs) (not verified). Griffin: "several dozen" portfolio manager teams in the sector model [GSB25]. Total number of teams and capital per portfolio manager: NOT FOUND in a reliable source.
- **Risk is centralised.** The Portfolio Construction and Risk Group (also written Portfolio Construction Group), more than 50 staff, headed by chief risk officer Joanna Welsh (hired 2017 from Tudor). A portfolio committee chaired by Welsh gives each portfolio manager an annual "volatility allocation", down to single risk factors. Central guidelines set how each portfolio must perform in stress tests; limits on concentration, funding, cross-asset sensitivity and liquidity. Equity portfolios must be beta neutral and keep a minimum share of stock-specific risk; a cap on ETF holdings; only stocks above a minimum daily turnover; in several businesses the ratio of stress loss to volatility is capped [RISK23]. Risk Management Center: real-time view of all portfolios, floor-to-ceiling screens in Chicago and a 35-by-8-foot screen in New York [RISK18][RISK23][RISK24]. Managers "are not allowed to gravitate towards the most popular stocks" [RISK24].
- **Drawdown limits.** [RISK23] mentions drawdown limits and "fundamental checking points" for opportunistic trades but gives no thresholds. Specific Citadel numbers (how far a book may fall before capital is cut or the team is closed): NOT FOUND. Figures that circulate (capital halved at 5%, closed at 7.5%) describe Millennium in an unsourced personal post and are not used.
- **Evidence that losing books are closed.** Feb 2016: over a dozen people cut at Surveyor after early losses, head replaced (https://fortune.com/2016/02/18/citadel-is-cutting-jobs/). 2018–19: Aptigon stock unit cut, then closed (Bloomberg headlines, 6 Mar 2018 and Mar 2019, via search) (not verified). March 2026: a credit portfolio manager left after losses (Bloomberg headline, 27 Mar 2026: https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-27/citadel-s-barratt-departs-after-losses-on-spirit-other-trades) (not verified). Fortune 2007: former staff said the bottom 10% were dismissed each year [F07].
- **Capital is rationed.** Profits are regularly paid out so the funds do not outgrow their opportunities: about $7bn after 2022 and about $7bn after 2023 (Fortune/Bloomberg, 12 Dec 2023: https://fortune.com/2023/12/12/ken-griffins-citadel-return-7-billion-to-clients/; Reuters wording on the 2022 payout via search summary); at end 2024 the payout was optional and only about $300m left (Hedgeweek citing Bloomberg, 3 Jan 2025: https://www.hedgeweek.com/majority-of-citadel-investors-decline-offer-to-redeem-profits/); about $5bn after 2025, taking assets from $72bn to $67bn (Tekedia citing CNBC, 23 Dec 2025: https://www.tekedia.com/citadel-to-return-5bn-in-2025-profits-to-investors-trimming-assets-to-67bn-amid-subdued-performance/). Cumulative: about $25bn since 2017 at Jan 2025 (Hedgeweek above); over $32bn at Jan 2026 [LCH26]; $18bn in voluntary payouts since 2018 per the prospectus [PROS].
- **Fees.** Funds reimburse all costs of Citadel's service companies, including pay of all employees and office costs; some funds also pay a 1% management fee that generally offsets the performance allocation; a performance allocation above a high-water mark each 31 December (rate not stated in the brochure) [BRO]. History: expenses above 3% a year in 2001 [II01]; 3–6% of assets [BM05]; 8.75% in 2005 plus 20% of profits [F07]. 2021–Sept 2024, three largest funds: gross gains $56.8bn, pass-through expenses $17bn (mostly pay), management and performance fees $7.5bn, net to investors about $30bn [PROS]: about 43% of gross gains went to costs and fees. Brief's figure of about $12bn charged in 2022: NOT FOUND in an opened source.
- **Talent.** Internships: more than 115,900 applications for 2026, over 350 interns, acceptance 0.36% (the two figures do not reconcile exactly); pay about $4,300–$5,800 a week (Fortune, 11 June 2026: https://fortune.com/2026/06/11/115k-young-people-applied-citadel-internships-350-made-cut-acceptance-rate-0-36/). Non-competes: analysts at least 12 months, higher-paid staff up to two years, length tied to pay (Hedgeweek citing Bloomberg, 14 Aug 2026: https://www.hedgeweek.com/citadel-tightens-staff-restrictions-with-non-competes-of-up-to-two-years/); Florida law allowing garden leave up to four years, backed by Griffin, in force July 2025 (same). Staff and principals held 18% of the multi-strategy funds [PROS]. Turnover: "Chicago's revolving door", at least 15 senior managers lost in five years to 2007 [F07]; at least 12 since 2002 [BM05].
- **Investors.** 61% institutions, 18% Citadel principals and staff, 12% family offices, 9% funds of funds [PROS].
- **Griffin's role.** Co-CIO; checks the firm's profit and loss every day; relies on partners who challenge him when he is emotional about a position [GSB25].

## 5. Risk and drawdowns

- Stated rules: positions sized before the trade; no concentration by region, asset class, horizon, sector or single security; tail risk "a foundational principle" [RISK24]; stress tests "near obsessional" [RISK23].
- **1994:** Wellington −4.3%; a third of capital withdrawn [II01].
- **1998:** +30.5%; lock-ups from 1 Aug 1998 let it buy while others sold [II01].
- **2008:** Kensington −54.95% (Institutional Investor, 26 Aug 2009: https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2btg43xi8sjmcrtft7uo0/premium/citadels-kensington-is-still-below-mark-despite-rebound); both main funds about −55%. Sequence [F08]: bought financial-sector assets after Bear Stearns (March); built convertible arbitrage in the summer; Lehman 15 Sept; financing for positions became nearly impossible; SEC short-selling ban (news 17 Sept) broke the hedges of convertible arbitrage; convertibles were about a quarter of the losses; corporate bonds hedged with credit default swaps lost as the gap between bonds and swaps widened; funds −20% at end Sept ("single worst month, by far"); about −35% by 24 Oct. Margin calls came and were met from cash and the trading platforms. Credit default swaps on Citadel's own bonds traded wider than Lehman's had before its failure. The Fed asked counterparties about their exposure.
- **Friday 24 October 2008** [F08]: James Forese of Citigroup phoned and urged him to go public; call announced about 12:30 pm for 3:30 pm; more than 1,000 dialled in; 25-minute delay; the call lasted 12 minutes; Griffin and COO Gerald Beeson said 30% of capital was in cash and an $8bn credit line was available.
- **"CNBC vans outside":** widely repeated as Griffin's later retelling; [F08] says only that the rumours ran "on CNBC". Not found in an opened source (not verified).
- **How close:** Griffin, 2026: "It was a near-death experience"; one morning he was not sure the firm would still be in business that night [SLF26].
- **Gate:** letter of 12 Dec 2008 suspended year-end withdrawals from Kensington and Wellington after requests of about $1.2bn (search summaries of Bloomberg/Reuters coverage: https://www.davemanuel.com/2008/12/15/citadel-suspends-redemptions-in-kensington-and-wellington-funds/) (not verified, pages not opened); withdrawals barred for 10 months (Chicago Tribune 13 Dec 2008 via [WP-C]).
- **Recovery:** Kensington +43% to end July 2009 and still 35% short of its high-water mark (II, Aug 2009, above); +62% for 2009 (Chicago Magazine 2011 via [WP-C]) (not verified); 2010 Wellington +11.11%, Kensington +10.75% (Institutional Investor via search summary) (not verified); 2011 about +20%; high-water mark crossed 17 January 2012 (Reuters, Jan 2012: https://in.marketscreener.com/news/latest/Citadel-s-main-hedge-funds-clear-high-water-mark-13979477, via search summary). Arithmetic check: 0.45 × 1.62 × 1.111 × 1.20 = 0.97, plus about 2.5% in early January 2012 = about 1.00. Consistent.
- **What changed after 2008, in his words** [SLF26]: before 2008 large trading firms funded like banks, near or below interbank rates, and assumed credit would slow, not stop; non-banks had no central-bank backstop; lesson: "don't pretend to be a bank unless you are a bank." [F08]: closing a $1bn fund-of-funds business and reinsurance; fewer complex strategies; long-short equity to be the core. Term financing and unencumbered cash details: NOT FOUND in an opened source beyond the above.
- **2016:** main fund −6.5% by mid-February (Fortune, 18 Feb 2016, above); full year +5.06% (Institutional Investor, 13 Jan 2017, figure seen in a search summary: https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/b18bk2145hpk69/citadel-posts-smallest-gain-in-years-in-tough-year-for-multistrats).
- **2025:** −1.7% in February (largest monthly fall since May 2021) and a further −1.7% in early March as multi-strategy funds cut positions together; Bank of England governor Andrew Bailey warned on the effect (Hedgeweek citing Bloomberg, 13 Mar 2025: https://www.hedgeweek.com/multi-strats-troubled-by-trump-trade-war/). Wellington +2.5% at mid-year (CNBC, 1 Jul 2025: https://www.nbcchicago.com/news/business/money-report/billionaire-ken-griffins-hedge-funds-at-citadel-are-all-in-the-green-for-2025/3780143/); +10.2% for the year.
- Concentration: by design, none. Top-position shares: NOT FOUND (13F not analysed; it lists thousands of rows including market-making-style option positions).
