How to use this

This document has one job: help build a morning watchlist that fits this trader. It is descriptive first, prescriptive second, and that ordering is deliberate.

The single most important thing to know before filtering anything. Across 53 statistical tests on 28 pre-trade variables — gap, RVOL, float, dollar volume, ATR, ADR, opening-range size, distance to the moving averages, prior-close location, VWAP position — not one separates his winning trades from his losing ones. Comparing his top 25 trades against his bottom 25 directly, every p-value exceeds 0.05.

So do not try to predict outcome from stock characteristics. It does not work in this trader’s data, and a filter built that way will be fitting noise. What the sections below give you instead is an envelope — the names he can physically trade and size — plus a small number of conditions that are reliably negative, which is a different and better-supported kind of claim.

The strategy in one page

SetupOpening Range Breakout, and only that. 197 of 248 practice trades and 58 of 71 live trades. Everything else he tried — mean reversion, break of high-of-day, ABCD, VWAP bounce — lost money and has been dropped.
Opening range09:30–09:35 ET (5 minutes)
DirectionLong only. 13 of 319 trades were short, and that ended in August: all 71 live trades were long.
Entry styleAnticipation, not confirmation. 67% of entries are placed inside the opening range, before the break completes. He deliberately does not wait for volume confirmation — in an ORB the volume arrives after the break, and waiting is too late.
SizingConstant dollar risk. He marks the invalidation level on the chart, and the platform back-solves the share count. Currently $18 for a starter and $28 full size; was $14 earlier.
Scaling inPyramids on strength. Each add is another ~1 unit of risk, and the whole-position stop is re-set at the same instant, so total exposure stays near one unit while size grows. He never adds while the position is underwater.
TargetNominally 2.5R, discretionary in practice. He met it on 4 of 70 live trades — each add raises the reference risk, so the target inflates as the position builds.
Risk ruleA max-loss rule, not a trade-count cap. Tested and correct: trades taken after a bad start are a coin flip, not a bleed.
Shape26% win rate · avg winner +2.56R · avg loser −0.74R · payoff 3.5×

The consequence for stock selection: a low win rate with a fat right tail means the watchlist’s job is not to find likely winners. It is to find names capable of a large move that he can size correctly and add into. A name that can only ever travel 1R is useless to him even if it goes his way.

The sizing constraint — the real universe filter

This is mechanical, not statistical, and it is the strongest reason to exclude a name.

He risks about 0.27× the 30-minute ATR per share and buys whole shares only, so his share count is roughly 66 ÷ 30mATR at an $18 unit. On a very volatile or very expensive name that collapses to a handful of shares, and two things break at once: whole-share rounding pushes realised risk well off the intended unit, and a 3-share position cannot be pyramided — which is the mechanism his entire edge runs through.

Exclude names whose 30-min ATR exceeds ~$13 Recommended
At an $18 unit that is fewer than 5 shares. Rounding error on risk becomes severe, and he cannot add — so the trade cannot become the kind of trade that pays him.
1–4 shares: n=29, win 14%, expR −0.44 [−0.75, −0.06], sumR −12.9

Entry timing

He is early. Median entry is 09:35; the median first trade of the day is 09:31.

09:35–09:40 is the dead zone Strong
This is exactly the “chasing the fifth-minute candle” he says he avoids — the window where a failed opening-range break gets bought. It is where the range has just completed and the obvious move has already been made. A watchlist should push him to be positioned before 09:35 or to wait until 09:40.
live −0.52R (n=12) · practice −0.59R (n=40) · every other window +0.21R
09:30–09:35 and 09:40–09:45 carry the book Descriptive
43% of trades happen while the range is still forming (+30.1R), and the 09:40–09:45 window is his best at +0.69R over 34 trades. After 10:00 he is flat to slightly negative and rarely trades at all.

What this means for the watchlist. The median session is slightly negative and the profit comes from a handful of sessions where a name trends for 15–40 minutes and he can add into it two or three times. So the candidate you want is not “likely to pop” — it is a name with a credible reason to trend for half an hour, liquid enough to add into, and volatile enough to matter but not so volatile that $18 buys four shares.

What does not predict anything

Do not build filters on these. Each was tested on 319 trades across two independent books.

Any single pre-trade market variable Well-powered null
Gap, RVOL, float, dollar volume, ATR, ADR, ADR%, opening-range size, OR-as-%-of-ATR, prior-close location, distance to the 20 and 50 SMA, %VWAP, distance to prior-day high/close/low, breakout volume ratio, VIX. Top 25 trades versus bottom 25: every p-value above 0.05.
53 median-split tests · excluding known-broken columns, 2 of 49 reached p<0.05 — exactly chance
Entry extension beyond the opening range Null
Twelve different ways of measuring “how extended was the entry”, two definitions of entry price, both books: nothing. He already enters conservatively — median entry is below the opening-range high.
Breakout volume ratio Not knowable
It is measured on the first bar after 09:35 that breaks the range. It is a property of the day, not of his trade, and it does not exist yet when he enters in anticipation.
Consecutive-candle counts Broken
The #1m, #5m and #1H columns in his sheet counted the entry bar’s close, which prints after the entry. Ignore any historical values. Corrected and lagged, all three are a clean null.
