A war, an oil shock and a new Fed chair set the stage

The 2026 drop did not start from a quiet year. A war with Iran pushed oil above $100 twice, inflation turned up again, and a new Federal Reserve chair raised interest rates on 16 September, the first increase since 2023. The stock market kept rising through all of it until 6 October.

February to May: the war sell-off and the recovery

US and Israeli strikes on Iran began on Saturday 28 February 2026. Iran blocked the Strait of Hormuz, the sea lane that carries much of the Gulf’s oil. Brent crude went from $72.48 on 27 February to $118.35 on 31 March Wikipedia, economic impact of the 2026 Iran war. The S&P 500 made its low of that sell-off at 6,316.91 on 30 March. A ceasefire on 7–8 April produced oil’s largest one-day fall since April 2020, but Brent still touched $126 during the day on 30 April Wikipedia, oil market chronology.

Kevin Warsh was sworn in as Fed chair on 22 May after a 54–45 Senate vote, the narrowest ever for the post Wikipedia, Kevin Warsh. The policy rate he inherited was 3.50–3.75%, where it had stood since the cuts ended in December 2025.

June to August: oil falls, then rises again; stocks climb

  • 5 June. A first warning in chips. The S&P 500 fell 2.64% and the Nasdaq Composite 4.18% after Broadcom did not raise its AI outlook and a strong jobs report lifted bond yields. The SpaceX share sale, then the largest ever, was a week away CNBC, 5 Jun 2026.
  • 17 June. The US and Iran signed a memorandum of understanding. US crude (WTI) closed under $70 on 24 June and reached $68.58 on 1 July. On the same day as the memorandum, Warsh’s first meeting left rates unchanged.
  • 22 June. The semiconductor index (PHLX SOX) made its closing high for the year. It never regained it.
  • 8 July. The president declared the memorandum “over”. Iran said the strait was closed again. Brent was back above $100 by 23 July.
  • 29 July. The Fed held rates by 9 votes to 3. The three dissenters wanted to raise them Federal Reserve, 29 Jul 2026.
  • 5 and 14 August. The Dow made its record close on 5 August. Small caps (Russell 2000), mid caps and industrials made their highs on 14 August.

On 24 July the S&P 500 stood at 7,411.98, up 17.3% from the March low.

The path to the 16 September rate rise

Three things came together in early September.

  1. Oil. Fighting escalated and Brent closed above $100 on 9 September and stayed there. Diesel hit a record $6.06 a gallon on 11 September.
  2. Inflation. The consumer price index (CPI) rose 0.4% in August, 3.4% over twelve months. In September it rose 0.6%, taking the twelve-month rate to 3.7% BLS, 14 Oct 2026.
  3. Bonds. The 10-year Treasury yield touched 5% on 14 September for the first time since 2023 MarketWatch, 14 Sep 2026.

Futures markets had put the chance of a September rise at 33% in mid-August. By 15 September it was 92.5%. On 16 September the Fed raised its target range by a quarter point to 3.75–4.00%, by 12 votes to 0. The statement said the move would support “a timelier return” of inflation to 2% Federal Reserve, 16 Sep 2026. The officials’ median projection for the policy rate was 4.1% at the end of both 2026 and 2027, which implied one more rise and no cuts Fed projections, 16 Sep 2026.

Stocks were higher before the announcement and turned down during the press conference. The S&P 500 closed down 0.45% at 7,551.81 and bank shares fell 3–4% CNBC, 16 Sep 2026. Three weeks later the index was at a record.

What to take from it
  • The first rate rise of a new cycle came three weeks before the top, not after it.
  • Oil above $100 and a 10-year yield above 5% were both in place before the index peaked.

The index peaked on 6 October; most of the market had peaked earlier

The S&P 500 made its record close of 7,818.93 on 6 October 2026. By then communication services, chips, the Dow and small caps were already below their highs, and margin debt had peaked in June. The last things to peak were the defensive groups and Apple, in November.

The day of the top and the day after

On 6 October the S&P 500 rose 0.58% to its 28th record close of the year. Chipmakers led and yields and oil eased InvestmentNews, 6 Oct 2026. The VIX (the options market’s gauge of expected S&P 500 swings) closed at 15.01. Nothing in that day’s news marked it as a top.

On 7 October the 10-year yield reached 5.365%, its highest since April 2002, and the minutes of the September meeting showed most officials expected another rise by year-end CNBC, 7 Oct 2026. The index slipped 0.22%. The first real damage came on 16 October: the S&P 500 fell 1.1% and chips had their worst week since June, on doubts about AI companies borrowing heavily at these yields AP, 16 Oct 2026.

What to take from it
  • The index top was the last of a long series of tops among its leaders. The chip index peaked 15 weeks before the S&P 500.
  • For four weeks after 6 October the average stock held up: the equal-weight index made its own high on 4 November.
  • Lower highs and lower lows were visible in closing prices from 23 October, with the index still within 3% of its record.

The 5 November break and the seven-day slide to 20 November

Two episodes did most of the damage. On 5 November the S&P 500 fell 4.36% in one session. From 12 to 20 November it fell seven days running and lost 9.3% from its 11 November close, ending at 6,910.39.

3–6 November: the election and the break

Democrats won both chambers of Congress on 3 November. On 4 November the Dow rose 3.0%, its best day after a midterm election since 1982, and the S&P 500 gained 1.58% CNN, 4 Nov 2026. Chips and data-centre stocks already fell that day.

On 5 November the reading changed. A candidate who had campaigned against data-centre projects had won the governorship of Texas, and 44 of 45 local ballots to restrict them had passed The Next Web, 6 Nov 2026. The S&P 500 closed at 7,353.73, the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.52%, and the chip index fell about 12%. It was the worst day since April 2025 CNBC, 5 Nov 2026.

The market’s moving from the promise of AI to the price of AI. — Ted Parkhill, Incline Investment Management, 5 Nov 2026

9–11 November: the last bounce

The index rose 2.35% on 9 November and closed at 7,619.38 on 11 November, a fourth lower high. The week ended up 2.5% even though October CPI came in above forecasts and Nvidia’s chief executive resigned on 12 November The Glass Desk, 13 Nov 2026.

Where it stood at the close on 20 November

  • S&P 500 −11.6% from its record; Nasdaq Composite −18.1%; Nasdaq-100 −19.8%; Russell 2000 −15.9% and the Dow −11.4% from their August highs. The week was −8.6% for the S&P 500.
  • The S&P 500 was 6.0% below its 200-day average (the mean close of the last 200 sessions).
  • Of about 660 large stocks, 35% were above their 50-day average and 13.9% closed at new 52-week lows. Only 20% rose on the day.
  • The VIX closed at 28.44 on 19 November. Press reports put it above 30 on 20 November, with a spike near 40 during the day not verified against the study’s data.
  • Since 6 October: chips (SMH) −36.7%, technology −26.2%, staples +10.6%, health care +6.1%, long Treasuries (TLT) +5.8%, gold −15.5%.
What to take from it
  • Until 18 November this was a fall in the leaders with the average stock roughly flat. On 19 and 20 November it spread to almost everything.
  • The 5 November low held for seven sessions and then gave way on a quiet 1% day, before the largest falls.
  • The two breaks, on 5 and 19 November, each came straight after what had looked like relief: a 1.58% rally the day before, and a 1.3% gain at the open.
