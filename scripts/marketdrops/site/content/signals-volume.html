Capitulation gauges mark a zone, not a day

Heavy volume, 90% down days, extreme put buying, surges in new lows and bearish surveys all cluster near lows. Each has also appeared months early. The final low of a multi-leg decline usually came on ordinary volume, after these readings had peaked.

Volume climax: not required at the final low

In the study’s table, volume on the day of the low was 1.5 times normal or more at 4 of 36 turns and at 15 of 91 failed rallies. It did not separate them. The median was 1.1 times normal in both groups.

90% days

A 90% down day has nine-tenths of volume and of points in falling stocks. Paul Desmond of Lowry Research held that one such day means nothing, that declines with two or more tend to produce a series, and that the low is confirmed by a 90% up day or two 80% up days. One outside audit since 1960 counts 9 bottoms identified, 3 missed, 16 extra signals and 3 false ones. The Dow Theory report In the study’s data a 90% up day within ten sessions followed 10 of 13 turns and also 40 of 51 failed rallies.

Put/call ratios and the Arms index

The put/call ratio divides the volume of put options (bets on a fall) by call options. From 1989 to 1999, extremes in the put/call ratio, the Arms index (which compares the volume in falling stocks with their number) and the VIX were followed by above-average index returns over 10 to 30 days. Simon & Wiggins, 2001 A later study found the predictive part of option volume is the non-public part, who is opening which position; the published ratio adds little. Pan & Poteshman, 2006 In the study’s data the highest total put/call reading of the 2007–09 decline came on 6 October 2008, five months before the low; in 2018 it came two sessions before.

New lows

The common claim is that the number of stocks at new lows peaks before the index does. In the study’s data that held in the multi-leg declines: 69.0% of stocks at new lows in autumn 2008 against 31.4% on 9 March 2009; 34.8% in June 2022 against 15.5% on 12 October; 23.7% in January 2016 against 17.7% on 11 February. The Reformed Broker, 12 Feb 2016 In single-leg declines (1999, 2011, both 2018 drops, 2023, 2025) new lows peaked on the day of the low. Measured against the immediately preceding swing low, only 1 of 9 turns had fewer new lows, so the comparison depends on which earlier low is used.

Surveys

The share of bears in the weekly AAII survey of individual investors reached 70.3% on 5 March 2009, 67.0% on 19 October 1990, 61.9% on 3 April 2025 and 60.9% on 22 September 2022. It also reached 59% in January and March 2008, a year before the low, and only 52.1% at the March 2020 low. AAII’s own study of 1987–2013 found that unusually high bearishness (above 50.8%) was followed by a gain of only 2.8% over six months, because of the run of early signals in 2008. Insider buying and fund flows

Surges in buying by company insiders are often cited at lows (November 2008, March 2009, August 2011, March 2020). not verified The academic test usually cited poses the question of whether aggregate insider trading predicts the market; its summary does not state the answer. Seyhun, 1988 For fund flows the evidence is that money follows prices: inflows go with gains in the same month, and 85% of that gain reverses within four months. Ben-Rephael, Kandel & Wohl, 2012 Record outflows mark heavy selling, not its end.

What to take from it
  • “No capitulation yet” was not a reason the low was not in: 2009, 2011, 2016 and 2022 all bottomed on ordinary volume.
  • Sentiment extremes opened a zone that lasted days in 2018 and 2025 and a year in 2008.
  • The combination seen at final lows of multi-leg declines was a lower price on a lower VIX and normal volume, well after the panic readings.
Limits No tested success rate was found for new-low divergences, insider buying or survey extremes as entry signals. The study’s breadth data cover today’s index members from 1998, which leaves out stocks that failed.
