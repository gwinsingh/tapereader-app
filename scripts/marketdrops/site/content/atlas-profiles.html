1987: the crash (−33.5% in 101 days, −20.5% of it in one session)

The Fed was raising rates into an expensive market, and a mechanical selling programme met a market that could not absorb it. The low of the crash day was never broken. The closing low came seven weeks later, quietly.

Cause. Prices had outrun earnings and interest rates were rising worldwide. On 14 October 1987 a House bill to end tax breaks for mergers and a poor trade report hit the market, and the S&P 500 fell more than 9% that week. On Monday 19 October, portfolio insurance (a programme that sells index futures automatically as prices fall) accounted for about 40% of futures selling outside market makers. Carlson, Federal Reserve, 2007

Fed. It had been tightening: the discount rate rose to 6% on 4 September 1987 not verified in a primary source. Before the open on 20 October it issued one sentence saying it stood ready “to serve as a source of liquidity”. The funds rate fell from over 7.5% to about 7% that day. Federal Reserve History

Earnings and credit. There was no recession. S&P 500 earnings per share rose from $14.48 in 1986 to $17.50 in 1987 and $23.75 in 1988. S&P / Shiller data The spread of Baa corporate bonds over the 10-year Treasury peaked at 2.68 points on 22 October, 30 sessions before the closing low.

What marked the low, in order

  1. 19 October: −20.5% to 224.84. The old volatility index (VXO) closed at 150.2, its record.
  2. 20 October: Fed statement, futures halted, corporate buyback announcements. Intraday low 216.46; close 236.83.
  3. 21 October: +9.1%. 26 October: −8.3% to 227.67, which held.
  4. 4 December: closing low 223.92, 0.9 points under the crash-day close, with the volatility index at 47.6 and volume at 1.0 times normal.
  5. Session 6 after that low: a follow-through day (a gain of 1.25% or more on higher volume, four or more sessions after a low). Back above the 50-day average in 10 sessions (+11.3%); above the 200-day average only after 126 sessions (+19.3%).

Retest. Yes. The December close undercut the October close by 0.4%; the intraday low held. After December the lowest close was 5.1% above the low.

What led afterwards. In 1988 the Russell 2000 index of small companies rose 22.4% against 12.4% for the S&P 500. Sector detail was not found. The old high was regained on 26 July 1989.

What to take from it
  • The panic low and the closing low were seven weeks apart. Fear peaked at the first; price bottomed at the second.
  • The cause was removed within a day (liquidity), and earnings were rising. The damage was to price, not to profits.

1990: Kuwait and recession (−19.9% in three months)

An oil shock landed on an economy already entering recession. The index made three lows, each lower, on less fear each time. The Fed did not cut until 12 sessions after the last one.

Cause. Iraq invaded Kuwait on 2 August 1990. Oil went from about $17 in July to a peak of $46 in mid-October. Wikipedia, 1990 oil price shock A recession ran from July 1990 to March 1991, so the market peak (16 July) and the business-cycle peak fell in the same month. NBER Banks and savings institutions were failing, and the junk-bond market had lost its main dealer in February.

Fed. One cut on 13 July, before the invasion. Nothing while oil rose. Cuts resumed on 29 October and continued to 4.00% by December 1991. Federal Reserve

Earnings and credit. Earnings per share fell from $22.87 in 1989 to $15.97 in 1991, about 30%, and troughed well after the price low. The Baa spread peaked at 2.45 points on 12 December, 43 sessions after the equity low.

What marked the low, in order

  1. 23 August: first low, 307.06. The VIX (the index of expected S&P 500 volatility from option prices) closed at 36.5, its high for the episode.
  2. 27 September and 11 October: lower lows (300.97, then 295.46) with the VIX lower, at 34.0 on 11 October. Oil peaked the same fortnight.
  3. Session 5: follow-through day (18 October). Back above the 50-day average in 17 sessions (+6.5%).
  4. 29 October: first Fed cut after the invasion.
  5. December: credit spreads peaked.
  6. 17 January 1991: the air war began; the index rose 3.7%. It closed above the 200-day average on session 72 (+13.3%).
  7. 5 February 1991: a breadth thrust (ten-day advancing issues 2.17 times declining issues on the NYSE). Deemer New closing high on 13 February.

Retest. The August and September lows were both undercut. The October low was not: the lowest close in the next 60 sessions was 2.2% above it (session 12), and the January 1991 low was about 5% above.

What led afterwards. Growth stocks. The Nasdaq Composite rose 57% from 16 October 1990 to 17 April 1991. From the 11 October close to the end of 1991, Amgen rose 390%, Home Depot 271% and Microsoft 186%.

What to take from it
  • The low came five months before the recession ended and before the first rate cut. Credit and earnings were still getting worse.
  • Three lows, falling fear: the pattern of a multi-leg decline.

1994: the bond massacre (−8.9%, then a year of going sideways)

The Fed doubled its policy rate in twelve months and bonds had one of their worst years. Stocks fell less than 9% and bottomed after the second of seven increases, because profits were rising about 40%.

This decline is not in the study’s table of drops, which starts at 10%.

Cause and Fed. On 4 February 1994 the Fed raised its rate for the first time since 1989, from 3.00% to 3.25%. Federal Reserve, 4 Feb 1994 Six more increases followed, including 0.75 points on 15 November, to 6.00% on 1 February 1995. The 10-year Treasury yield rose from 5.81% on 3 February to 8.05% on 7 November 1994. FRED Leveraged bond positions were unwound worldwide. Wikipedia

Earnings and credit. Earnings per share rose from $21.89 in 1993 to $30.60 in 1994. Corporate credit was calm: the Baa spread narrowed through the year. Stress appeared only in December, with the Orange County bankruptcy (6 December) and the Mexican devaluation (20 December).

What marked the low, in order

  1. 2 February: closing peak, 482.00. The VIX had been at 9.9 on 28 January.
  2. 4 February: first increase; index −2.3%.
  3. 4 April: low, 438.92, after the second increase (22 March). The VIX closed at 23.9, its high, the same day.
  4. 24 June and 8 December: higher lows (442.80, 445.45), while bond yields kept rising until 7 November.
  5. 1 February 1995: last increase. 14 February: new closing high.

Retest. None of the April low. The Nasdaq Composite peaked later (18 March) and bottomed later (24 June), down 13.7%.

What led afterwards. In 1995 the S&P 500 rose 34.1% and the Nasdaq Composite 39.9%; Intel rose about 78%. Broader sector leadership was not verified.

What to take from it
  • A rate-increase cycle with rising profits compressed valuations and produced a range, not a bear market.
  • Stocks bottomed ten months before the last increase. Waiting for the Fed to stop would have meant waiting through the whole base.

1998: Russia and LTCM (−19% in six weeks)

A foreign default broke a set of leveraged trades and the index fell 19.3% in 31 sessions. The first Fed cut was sold. The low was retested to within 0.2% five weeks later, and a surprise cut a week after that started a rise to new highs in six weeks.

Cause. On 17 August 1998 Russia devalued and defaulted on its domestic debt. The hedge fund Long-Term Capital Management lost 44% in August; on 23 September fourteen firms put in about $3.6bn to take it over, arranged but not funded by the New York Fed. Federal Reserve History

Fed. On hold at 5.50% going in. It cut 0.25 points on 29 September (the index fell 3.1% and 3.0% on the next two days), cut again between meetings on 15 October (index +4.2%), and a third time on 17 November. Federal Reserve, 15 Oct 1998

Earnings and credit. No recession. Earnings per share dipped 5% in 1998 and rose 28% in 1999. The Baa spread peaked at 2.77 points on 16 October, 33 sessions after the closing low and a week after the intraday low.

What marked the low, in order

  1. 31 August: −6.8% to 957.28, the closing low. VIX 44.3. Volume peaked the next session at 2.0 times normal.
  2. Session 5: follow-through day. The index closed above its 200-day average on session 16 (+11.4%) and lost it the next day.
  3. 10 September: VIX 45.3. It stayed above 40 through the month.
  4. 23 and 29 September: the rescue, then the first cut, which disappointed.
  5. 8 October (session 27): intraday low 923.32, close 959.44, 0.2% above the August close. Small-company and Nasdaq indices closed at clear new lows that day; the S&P 500 did not. The research file has the VIX at 45.7 that day; it falls outside the window the study’s table uses.
  6. 15 October: the surprise cut. Back above the 50-day average on session 32 (+9.4%). New closing high on 23 November.

Retest. Yes, a textbook one: to within 0.2% on a close, 3.5% lower intraday. The deepest pullback in the first 60 sessions was 10.0%.

What led afterwards. The prior leaders. The Nasdaq Composite rose 54.5% from 8 October to the end of the year; Amazon rose 273%, Cisco 99%, Microsoft 52%. Small companies had peaked in April (Russell 2000 −36.9% to 8 October) and were still below that high a year later.

What to take from it
  • The first cut did not mark the low; the second, unexpected one did.
  • Large stocks held the August low while small stocks broke it. The divergence appeared on the retest day.

2011: downgrade and euro crisis (−19.4%, most of it in eleven sessions)

The index fell 16.8% between 22 July and 8 August, then spent eight weeks in a range before one more low on 3 October. Fear and volume peaked in August. The October low was lower in price and calmer.

Cause. Two shocks overlapped. The US debt-ceiling fight ended on 2 August, and Standard & Poor’s cut the US credit rating to AA+ on the evening of Friday 5 August. Wikipedia In Europe, fear spread to Italian and Spanish debt. Wikipedia

Fed. Already at zero. On 9 August it promised low rates “at least through mid-2013” (index +4.7%). Federal Reserve, 9 Aug 2011 On 21 September it announced a $400bn swap of short for long Treasuries (index −2.9%, then −3.2%). Federal Reserve, 21 Sep 2011 On 30 November six central banks cut the price of dollar loans to foreign banks (index +4.3%).

Earnings and credit. No US recession; earnings per share rose 12% in 2011. The Baa spread peaked at 3.39 points on 27 September, four sessions before the low.

What marked the low, in order

  1. 8 August: −6.7% to 1119.46. VIX 48.0, its high. SPY (the S&P 500 fund) traded 3.1 times its 50-day average volume.
  2. 9 August: Fed pledge and a reversal. A retest on 19–22 August held.
  3. 3 October: closing low 1099.23, 1.8% under August, with the VIX at 45.5. In the study’s data 33.5% of stocks were at 52-week lows, the most of the episode, and 8.9% were above their 200-day average.
  4. 4 October: intraday low 1074.77, then a close 2.3% higher.
  5. Back above the 50-day average on session 5 (+8.7%). Follow-through day on session 11 (18 October). Above the 200-day average on session 18 (+16.9%), lost again two sessions later.
  6. 25 November (session 38): a higher low, 5.4% above the October close. Then the swap-line action and the European Central Bank’s three-year loans in December.

On the way down the index closed back above its 20-day average 11 times and its 50-day average 5 times before the low.

Retest. The August low was undercut in October. The October low was approached to within 5.4%; the deepest pullback in the first 60 sessions was 9.8%.

What led afterwards. Over the next 63 sessions: home construction +43.7%, oil and gas producers +37.1%, regional banks +35.4%, against +16.7% for the index fund. Over 252 sessions home construction was +131.1% and biotechnology +67.0%. Gold, Treasuries and solar lagged.

What to take from it
  • The panic was in August; the low was in October. Waiting for a second panic meant missing it.
  • The index reclaimed its averages 16 times on the way down. A reclaim by itself did not separate the turn from the failures.

2015–16: China, oil and the first rate increase (−14.2% in two legs)

A flash crash in August 2015, a recovery to within 1% of the high, then a second fall after the Fed’s first increase. Price made its low on 11 February 2016, the same day as oil and corporate credit, with far less fear than in August.

Cause. China’s slowdown and the yuan devaluation of 11 August 2015, oil falling to $26, and a first Fed increase approaching while profits were shrinking. Wikipedia On 24 August SPY opened 5.2% lower and there were 1,278 trading halts in stocks and funds. SEC staff, Dec 2015

Fed. It raised rates on 16 December 2015, the first increase since 2006. Federal Reserve On 16 March 2016 its projections for the year were cut from four increases to two. Federal Reserve The next increase came in December 2016.

Earnings and credit. An earnings recession: the first quarter of 2016 was the fourth straight year-on-year decline. FactSet via CBS The Baa spread rose from 2.75 points to a peak of 3.63 on 11 February 2016.

What marked the low, in order

  1. 24–25 August 2015: VIX 53.3 intraday, 40.7 at the close, the high of the episode; first-leg low 1867.61 (−12.4%). SPY volume 4.3 times its average.
  2. 28–29 September: a retest that held. Rally of 13.0% to 3 November.
  3. 20 January 2016: stocks at new lows peaked (23.7% of the study’s stocks; over 42% of NYSE issues per SentimenTrader).
  4. 11 February: closing low 1829.08 with the VIX at 28.1 and 17.7% of stocks at new lows. Oil, high-yield bonds and the Baa spread turned the same day.
  5. Back above the 50-day average on session 9 (+6.7%). The study’s rule finds the first follow-through day on the S&P 500 on session 12; practitioners cite 17 February on the Nasdaq.
  6. 3 March: a breadth thrust on the S&P 1500. StockCharts 11 March (session 20): close above the 200-day average (+10.6%).
  7. 16 March: the Fed’s lower projections.

Retest. The August low was undercut in January and February. The February low was not retested: the deepest pullback in the next 60 sessions was 2.5%.

What led afterwards. The most beaten-down groups first. Over 63 sessions: Brazil +53.7%, metals and mining +50.2%, oil and gas producers +43.3%, against +13.5% for the index fund. Over 252 sessions metals and mining were +143.0%, regional banks +73.9% and semiconductors +66.4%.

What to take from it
  • Fear peaked 118 sessions before the price low. The final low was a lower price on a much lower VIX and fewer new lows.
  • Equities turned on the day oil and credit turned, five weeks before the Fed changed its guidance.

2020: COVID (−33.9% in 23 sessions)

The fastest fall of this size on record ended the morning the Fed promised unlimited purchases and a backstop for corporate credit. Fear had peaked a week earlier. There was no retest.

Cause. The pandemic shutdown, with an oil-price war from 9 March. Market-wide trading halts were triggered on 9, 12, 16 and 18 March. Wikipedia

Fed. An emergency cut of 0.50 points on 3 March; a cut to zero with $700bn of bond purchases on Sunday 15 March; lending programmes for commercial paper, dealers and money funds on 17–18 March; and at 8am on 23 March purchases “in the amounts needed” plus two facilities to buy corporate bonds. Federal Reserve, 23 Mar 2020 Congress’s $2.2tn relief act was signed on 27 March.

Earnings and credit. Calendar-2020 earnings fell 11.4%. FactSet, 12 Feb 2021 The high-yield spread peaked at 1,087 basis points on 23 March. CFA Institute The Baa spread peaked the same day at 4.31 points.

What marked the low, in order

  1. 12 March: −9.5%, the heaviest SPY volume of the episode.
  2. 16 March: −12.0%. VIX closed at 82.7, its record; 53.6% of the study’s stocks were at new lows.
  3. 23 March: closing low 2237.40 with the VIX at 61.6 and volume at 2.05 times normal. The policy announcement came before the open.
  4. 24–26 March: +9.4%, +1.2%, +6.2%. Each day had over 80% of issues and volume rising. SentimenTrader
  5. Session 8 (2 April): follow-through day.
  6. Session 18: back above the 50-day average, already 28.5% off the low. Session 45 (27 May): above the 200-day average, 35.7% off the low.
  7. 3 June: the NYSE breadth thrust, 50 sessions after the low.

Retest. None. The lowest close after session 5 was 10.4% above the low. The index never closed back above its 20-day or 50-day average on the way down.

What led afterwards. Over 63 sessions: the ARK Innovation fund +85.9%, oil and gas producers +84.5%, home construction +84.0%, against +39.9% for the index fund. Over 252 sessions: solar +299.1%, retail +226.5%, ARK Innovation +222.4%. Treasuries and the dollar fell.

What to take from it
  • The low was a policy event at a known hour. Fear, volume and new lows had all peaked five to seven sessions earlier.
  • Every trend filter was late: the 200-day average was reclaimed after more than a third of the fall had been recovered.

2022: the rate-increase bear market (−25.4% over nine months)

A slow decline with no panic. Six rallies of 5% or more failed, the largest stopping one point under the 200-day average. The low came on a day of bad inflation news, and the VIX was lower than it had been in March.

Cause. Inflation at a forty-year high and the fastest rate increases since the 1980s. Wikipedia

Fed. First increase on 16 March; four of 0.75 points from 15 June to 2 November; then smaller steps until July 2023. On 26 August the chair said policy would bring “pain to households and businesses”, and the index fell 3.4%. Powell, 26 Aug 2022 The 2-year Treasury yield rose 3.5 points during the decline.

Earnings and credit. Earnings fell year on year in the three quarters after the low, from late 2022 to mid-2023. FactSet, 1 Dec 2023 The Baa spread peaked at 2.42 points in July, 71 sessions before the low. Credit did not confirm the October low.

What marked the low, in order

  1. 7 March: VIX closing high for the whole decline, 36.5.
  2. 16 June: first major low, 3666.77 (−23.6%). New lows peaked here: 34.8% of the study’s stocks.
  3. 16 August: the summer rally of 17.4% reached 4325.28 intraday against a 200-day average of 4326.18, and failed.
  4. 12 October: closing low 3577.03, VIX 33.6, 15.5% of stocks at new lows, volume 0.96 times normal.
  5. 13 October: inflation came in above forecasts; the index made its intraday low (3491.58) and closed 2.6% higher.
  6. Session 7 (21 October): follow-through day. Session 12: back above the 50-day average (+9.1%).
  7. 10 November: a lower inflation reading; index +5.5%. BLS, 10 Nov 2022 Session 34 (30 November): above the 200-day average (+14.1%), lost three days later.
  8. 28 December: Nasdaq Composite closing low. 12 January 2023: NYSE breadth thrust. Deemer

On the way down the index closed back above its 20-day average 13 times and its 50-day average 6 times.

Retest. The June low was undercut in September and October. After October, the lowest close once a rally had developed was 4.0% above the low (3 November); the late-December low was about 6% above. The deepest pullback in the first 60 sessions was 7.3%.

What led afterwards. Over 63 sessions: China internet +52.6%, Germany +40.8%, oil services +37.8%, semiconductors +29.7%, against +11.8% for the index fund. Over 252 sessions: semiconductors +71.0%, technology +46.0%, software +42.5%. Energy, the leader of 2022, returned 16.0%. Buying dips began to pay again after the low: the average session after a down day was −0.20% in the first half of 2022 and +0.27% in the first half of 2023.

What to take from it
  • A bear market can run its course without a volatility spike, a volume climax or an inverted VIX curve.
  • The low came nine months before the last rate increase and before earnings troughed. What changed was the direction of inflation.

April 2025: the tariff crash (−18.9% in 34 sessions)

A policy shock made the low and a policy reversal ended it. Fear, volume, credit and price all peaked within two sessions. The index was at a new record 55 sessions later.

Cause. Tariffs on nearly all imports, announced after the close on 2 April 2025. Wikipedia The index was already 10.1% below its 19 February record by 13 March. It fell 4.8% and 6.0% on 3 and 4 April.

Fed. On hold at 4.25–4.50%. On 4 April the chair said the Fed was “well positioned to wait”. Powell, 4 Apr 2025 It did not cut until September. The 10-year Treasury yield rose from 3.98% to 4.49% between 4 and 11 April while stocks and the dollar fell.

Earnings and credit. No earnings recession: first-quarter earnings rose 13.3%, though second-quarter estimates were cut 4.0%. FactSet, 30 May 2025 The high-yield spread peaked at 4.61 points on 7 April, mild beside 2016 or 2020. FRED

What marked the low, in order

  1. 7 April: intraday low 4835.04 (−21.4% from the intraday high), VIX 60.1 intraday, SPY volume 4.1 times its average. A false report of a pause moved the index 8% within the session.
  2. 8 April: a gap up was sold; closing low 4982.77 with the VIX at 52.3, its closing high. 29.4% of the study’s stocks were at new lows.
  3. 9 April: a 90-day pause was announced; index +9.5%, Nasdaq Composite +12.2%.
  4. 21 April (session 8): a higher low, 3.5% above the closing low.
  5. Session 9 (22 April): follow-through day. 24 April: a Zweig breadth thrust (defined on the breadth page). Yardeni, 28 Apr 2025
  6. Session 16: back above the 50-day average (+12.5%). Session 23 (12 May, the US–China truce): above the 200-day average (+17.3%).

Retest. No. The 21 April low held 3.5% above; the deepest pullback in the first 60 sessions was 5.5%.

What led afterwards. Largely the leaders from before. Over 63 sessions: uranium +82.8%, ARK Innovation +81.6%, semiconductors +59.8%, against +26.4% for the index fund. Over 252 sessions semiconductors were +143.5% and metals and mining +134.0%. Staples and health care lagged.

What to take from it
  • When one decision causes a fall, reversing it can end the fall in a day. Nothing on the chart on 8 April showed that the reversal was coming.
  • The 50-day average crossed below the 200-day on 14 April, four sessions after the low.

Declines while the Fed was raising rates: three sizes, three endings

Since 1987, declines during rate-increase cycles have come in three kinds. With profits rising they stopped at 7–12% while increases continued. Falls of 19% or more ended only when the Fed changed its stance or its pace. In 2000 the end of the increases did not help.

The longer record

In the study’s table of 35 completed drops of 10% or more since 1950, 20 had the Fed raising rates at the peak or at the low. Seven of those 20 reached −20% (35%), against 11 of all 35 (31%). A rate-increase cycle by itself did not make a deep fall more likely.

What to take from it
  • What separated the shallow cases (1994, 2004, 2006) from the deep ones was profits: rising in the first group, falling or about to fall in 2000 and 2022.
  • In the shallow cases stocks bottomed long before the last increase. In the deep ones the low waited for a change in the Fed’s pace or words.
Limits Fourteen episodes, read with hindsight. “What ended it” is a reading of the dates, not a tested cause.
