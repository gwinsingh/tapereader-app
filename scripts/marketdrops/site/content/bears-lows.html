At both final lows, fear and volume had already peaked

The lowest price did not come on the worst day. In 2002 the volume and volatility extremes came in July and August, eleven weeks before the low of 9 October. In 2008–09 they came in October and November, three and a half to five months before the low of 9 March 2009. Growth indices held above their earlier lows both times. The 2003 advance began after a retest; 2009 had none.

What had failed before

None of these signs was clean. Single 90% up-volume days had failed during 2008. A writer who tested them noted on 11 March 2009 that a 90% up day straight after a new low had been followed by lower prices in every recent case; he changed his view two days later when the second one arrived. Quantifiable Edges, 11 Mar 2009 A fund manager who looked for improvement in market internals wrote on 9 March 2009 that he saw none; it existed in the Nasdaq 100, semiconductors, emerging markets, credit and the VIX. What to take from it
  • In both bears the final low came on a lower VIX and lower volume than an earlier low.
  • In 2008–09 credit turned three months before shares. In 2002 credit turned with shares.
  • Sentiment was most negative at the March 2009 low itself, and in 2003 at the retest, not at the October 2002 low.
  • 2009 produced a breadth thrust and no retest. 2002–03 produced neither a thrust nor a clean low.
