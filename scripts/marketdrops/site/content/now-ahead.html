What named observers have said

Opinion is split. The cautious voices point to broken trends, rates and the scale of AI spending. The constructive ones point to rising estimates, light positioning and a fall that is in valuations only. No Fed official had commented on the week’s fall itself by 20 November.

What they say would mark a low

  • Breadth washed out. Krinsky says lasting lows usually come with fewer than 40%, often fewer than 20%, of S&P 500 stocks above their 50-day average. He put the figure at 50% on 20 November; the study’s count of about 660 large stocks gives 35%. The two use different lists of stocks.
  • The last leaders giving way. Krinsky notes that Apple was 3% below its high and that a final low with Apple so close to its peak would be without precedent.
  • Yields and trend. One daily market summary listed better breadth, stable Treasury yields and a return above the 200-day average Motley Fool, 19 Nov 2026.
  • Time. CNBC’s review of past breaks in technology found prices steadying over three to six months, with choppy trading first.
  • Rare company. Bespoke found only five earlier weeks in which the S&P 500 fell every day with at least four falls of 1% or more: in 1962, 1974, 1978, after 11 September 2001 and in October 2008.
