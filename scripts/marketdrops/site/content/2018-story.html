The top was made in stages, and the index was the last part to turn

The S&P 500 made its closing high on 20 September 2018 at 2,930.75 and its closing low on 24 December 2018 at 2,351.10: −19.8% in 65 sessions. Margin debt, half of the sectors, small caps and foreign markets had peaked earlier. The stated causes were a Federal Reserve that kept tightening, a trade war with China and slowing growth abroad.

2017 and January 2018

The advance into 26 January 2018 was steep. In the twelve months to that day the index gained 25.1% and stood 14.0% above its 200-day average (study price data). The VIX (the index of expected volatility priced into S&P 500 options) closed at 11.08 FRED. The forward P/E (price divided by the next twelve months’ expected earnings) began 2018 at 18.4 CNBC, 31 Dec 2018. Margin debt (money borrowed from brokers against securities) rose $23bn in January alone to $665.7bn FINRA.

The fuel was the corporate tax cut of December 2017. Analysts raised their 2018 earnings-growth estimate from 9.5% to 16.8% between 30 November 2017 and 28 February 2018 FactSet, 21 Dec 2018. A 10.2% correction in nine sessions followed in February (see the page on that episode). The index needed seven months, to 24 August, to make a new closing high.

The September record was carried by technology, consumer discretionary and health care. On 20 September the index was 6.6% above its 200-day average, the VIX was 11.8 and the average daily range of the previous 63 sessions was 0.66% (study price data). The forward P/E was 16.9, against a five-year average of 16.3 FactSet, 21 Sep 2018. Nothing on the index chart looked wrong.

Policy was tightening on two fronts. On 26 September the Fed raised its rate to 2.00–2.25% and dropped the word “accommodative” from its statement Value Line, 26 Sep 2018. In October its balance-sheet runoff (letting bonds mature without replacing them) reached the full planned pace of $50bn a month CNBC, 20 Sep 2017. Tariffs of 10% on $200bn of Chinese goods took effect on 24 September trade-war chronology.

What to take from it
  • The index high came eight months after the first sectors peaked and four months after margin debt peaked.
  • At the high the index itself showed low volatility and an ordinary valuation. The warnings were in what was not confirming.

October: a rate shock, then good earnings were sold

The first leg ran from 3 to 29 October. It began in the bond market, not in stocks. It ended with companies that beat earnings estimates falling on the news.

3–11 October: yields, then a break without news

After the close on 3 October the Fed chair, Jerome Powell, said rates were still “a long way from neutral” (neutral is the rate that neither speeds nor slows the economy) CNBC, 3 Oct 2018. The Dow had made an intraday record that day and oil settled at a four-year high of $76.41 CNN, 8 Nov 2018. The 10-year Treasury yield went from 3.05% on 2 October to 3.23% on 5 October, the highest since 2011 FRED. Technology and other growth stocks fell first, on 4 and 5 October.

On 10 October the S&P 500 fell 3.3% with no news trigger, its fifth straight loss Reuters, 10 Oct 2018. JPMorgan’s Marko Kolanovic attributed most of the selling to option hedging by dealers and other rule-driven funds Bloomberg, 12 Oct 2018. That day the VIX closed above the three-month VIX for the first time in the episode, a sign that traders were paying more for immediate protection than for later protection. On 11 October a soft inflation report produced a morning rally that was sold; the Dow closed down 546 points Value Line, 11 Oct 2018.

12–29 October: a three-day bounce, then earnings

Bank results and strong data lifted the index 2.2% on 16 October. Two sessions later the gain was being sold again. Then the earnings reactions turned harsh:

  • 23 Oct: Caterpillar (tariff costs) and 3M (lower guidance) fell 7.6% and 4.4%. The Dow was down 549 points intraday and closed down 126 Reuters, 23 Oct 2018.
  • 24 Oct: Texas Instruments said demand was slowing. The S&P 500 fell 3.1% to 2,656.10 and erased its gain for the year; the Nasdaq fell 4.4%, its worst day since August 2011 Reuters, 24 Oct 2018.
  • 26 Oct: Amazon (−7.8%) and Alphabet missed on revenue. FactSet reported that companies which had beaten estimates were down 1.5% on average around their reports, the worst reaction to beats since 2011 FactSet, 29 Oct 2018.
  • 29 Oct: a strong open reversed on a report that the US was preparing tariffs on all remaining Chinese imports. The index closed at 2,641.25, the low of the month AP, 29 Oct 2018.

What was visible

Rallies started in the morning and died in the afternoon. Third-quarter earnings growth was the best since 2010, and it did not matter. Morgan Stanley’s strategist wrote on 29 October that a sector-by-sector decline was becoming a general one CNBC, 29 Oct 2018. One thing held: the emerging-markets fund (EEM) made its low on 29 October and did not go lower in December (study price data).

What to take from it
  • The first break came a week after the bond market moved, on a day without news.
  • Stocks falling on good earnings was measurable by 26 October, two months before the low.

November: a 6.5% rally failed, then the leaders broke

From the 29 October low the index rose 6.5% in seven sessions. The rally ended the day the Fed met without softening its tone. The second leg down took out the stocks that had led the market for two years.

30 October – 7 November: the rally

The triggers were an oversold market, well-received Facebook results, a friendly Trump–Xi telephone call and the midterm elections. On 7 November, the day after the vote split Congress, the index gained 2.1% to 2,813, its best day after a midterm since 1982 Fortune, 7 Nov 2018. That was the high.

8–23 November: the failure

  • 8 Nov: the Fed held rates and signalled a December rise. The 10-year yield closed at 3.24% and the 2-year at 2.98%, both the highs of the cycle FRED. Oil closed lower for a tenth day and entered a bear market CNN, 8 Nov 2018.
  • 12 Nov: warnings from Apple suppliers. Dow −602 Value Line, 12 Nov 2018.
  • 16 Nov: Nvidia fell 19% after guiding revenue to $2.70bn against $3.40bn expected, blaming unsold graphics cards after the crypto-mining boom Fortune, 15 Nov 2018.
  • 19 Nov: all five FAANG stocks (Facebook, Apple, Amazon, Netflix, Alphabet) were now at least 20% below their highs; Facebook −38.8%, Netflix −36.1% Fortune, 19 Nov 2018.
  • 20 Nov: Target and other retailers fell on higher sales and profits. Dow −550 Value Line, 20 Nov 2018.
  • 23 Nov: in a half-session the index closed 10% below its high for the first time, a new closing low (study price data).
Buying the dip has not worked in 2018 for the first time since 2002. — Michael Wilson, Morgan Stanley, 19 Nov 2018

Source for the quotation: CNBC, 19 Nov 2018.

What was visible

There was no panic yet. The total put/call ratio (put options traded divided by call options; high means fear) was only 0.92 on 20 November Cboe. One quiet change: the relative-strength lines (price divided by the S&P 500) of the Nasdaq 100, large growth, momentum and software funds made their lows on 19 November. From then on growth fell no faster than the index, 23 sessions before the index low (study price data).

On 28 November Powell said rates were “just below” the range of neutral estimates. The index rose 2.3%, yet more stocks made 52-week lows than highs that day Value Line, 28 Nov 2018.

What to take from it
  • A 6.5% rally in seven sessions on good headlines was fully reversed within twelve more sessions.
  • The leaders’ washout on 19–20 November was not the index low. The index fell another 10.7% from the 23 November close.

December: the truce rally lasted one day, and the Fed did not give way

A second rally of 6.0% ended on 3 December. The last leg ran through doubts about the trade truce, an inverted yield curve, a Fed rate rise, a government shutdown and year-end selling. The capitulation readings came on 20 and 21 December; the low came on a quiet half-day.

3–12 December

On Saturday 1 December the US and China agreed a 90-day tariff truce. On 3 December the index gapped up and gained 1.1%, 6.0% above the 23 November close. The same day the 5-year Treasury yield fell below the 3-year, the first inversion of the yield curve (short rates above long rates, a classic recession warning) since 2007 FRED. On 4 December doubts about what had been agreed erased the whole rally: −3.2% Fortune, 4 Dec 2018.

On 6 December news of the arrest of Huawei’s finance chief sent the Dow down 785 points; it closed down 79. On 7 December a weak jobs report brought −2.3%. On 10 December the index traded below its October low and closed higher, which looked like a failed breakdown. It was undone within four sessions SentimenTrader, 11 Dec 2018. By 12 December a survey of individual investors showed 20.9% bulls and 48.9% bears, and US stock funds had lost a record $46.2bn in a week AAII CNBC, 14 Dec 2018. The index was still more than 10% above its eventual low.

14–21 December: the cascade

  • 14 Dec: weak Chinese data; Johnson & Johnson −10%. S&P 500 −1.9% to 2,599.95. Selling of losers for tax purposes picked up from this day, according to DataTrek Yahoo Finance, 19 Jan 2019.
  • 17 Dec: −2.1% to 2,545.94, the lowest close since October 2017.
  • 19 Dec: the Fed raised rates to 2.25–2.50%. It cut its forecast for 2019 from three rises to two, but Powell said the balance-sheet runoff was on “automatic pilot”. The Dow went from +300 to a close of −352 Value Line, 19 Dec 2018.
  • 20 Dec: total put/call ratio 1.82, the highest of 2018 Cboe.
  • 21 Dec: quarterly options expiry. −2.1% to 2,416.58; US volume 15.18bn shares, 72% above the 20-day average; the Nasdaq was 20% below its high Reuters, 21 Dec 2018.

24 December: the low

A partial government shutdown began on 22 December. On Sunday 23 December the Treasury Secretary announced he had called the heads of six large banks and been told they had ample liquidity. Nobody had been asking about bank liquidity, and the statement unsettled investors. On Monday the President wrote that the Fed was the economy’s only problem AP, 24 Dec 2018. In a session that closed at 1 pm the index fell 2.7% to 2,351.10.

What to take from it
  • Extreme pessimism was on record on 12 December, 8 sessions and more than 10% before the low.
  • The heaviest volume and the highest put/call readings came one and two sessions before the low, not on it.
  • The low itself was a thin holiday session after official reassurance had backfired.

The turn: seven sessions near the low, then no second chance

The index rose 5.0% on 26 December without real news. It made a higher low on 3 January on bad news, and on 4 January the Fed chair said the Fed would be patient. There was no retest. The index made a new record close on 23 April 2019.

26 December – 4 January

  • 26 Dec: +5.0% to 2,467.70, the best day since March 2009. The reasons offered were thin: an adviser said Powell’s job was safe, holiday sales were strong, oil rose 9%, and pension funds had an estimated $60bn of shares to buy before year-end AP, 26 Dec 2018 Fortune, 28 Dec 2018.
  • 27 Dec: down 2.8% intraday, closed +0.9%: the largest upward reversal since 2010.
  • 2–3 Jan: Apple cut its revenue forecast and the ISM factory index had its largest fall since 2008. The index lost 2.5% on 3 January and closed 4.1% above the low. The VIX was 25.45, against 36.07 at the low.
  • 4 Jan: payrolls +312,000; Powell said the Fed would be “patient”. S&P 500 +3.4% to 2,531.94, with seven NYSE stocks rising for each one falling Value Line, 4 Jan 2019. This was session 7 after the low and the follow-through day (a strong gain on higher volume from the fourth day of a rally, the confirmation signal used by Investor’s Business Daily).

The +7.7% on 4 January is 2,531.94 against 2,351.10 from the cited reports; the other gains are from the study’s price data. The weakest close between sessions 5 and 60 after the low was the 3 January close, 4.1% above it. Many strategists expected a return to the low because both earlier lows of 2018 had been tested; it did not come.

News kept being bad for a while: Chinese growth at its slowest since 1990 (21–22 January), earnings forecasts still being cut. The index absorbed it. In the reporting season of January and February, companies that beat estimates gained 2.1% on average and those that missed lost only 0.8% FactSet, 1 Mar 2019. On 20 March the Fed forecast no rate rises for 2019 and set September as the end of its balance-sheet runoff Federal Reserve, 20 Mar 2019.

What to take from it
  • A buyer had seven sessions, 26 December to 4 January, within about 8% of the low. After that the index did not come back.
  • The sequence was: 5% day, higher low on bad news with less fear, second broad up day plus a change in Fed language, then breadth and trend repair.
  • The cause of the decline, Fed policy, changed five sessions after the low. In February 2018 and October 2018 no cause had changed, and both lows were tested.
Limits This is one episode. The sequence is easy to read afterwards; on 26 December it was one more large up day in a decline that had already produced two failed rallies of 6%.
