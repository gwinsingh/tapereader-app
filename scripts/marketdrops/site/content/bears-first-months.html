Six weeks in, the two great bears looked like ordinary corrections

Thirty sessions after its peak the S&P 500 was down 6.8% in 2000 and 8.0% in 2007. Corrections that ended within months were as deep or deeper at the same point. What differed was under the index: which groups had already broken, the Federal Reserve’s direction, and credit.

Depth and speed did not separate the cases

The study marks the first close 10% below the 52-week high. In 2000 it came on 14 April, 15 sessions after the peak. In 2007 it came on 26 November, 33 sessions after the peak. Sixty-three sessions later the index was 11.3% higher in 2000 and 1.9% lower in 2007.

2000: a correction that healed, then a second peak

The S&P 500 fell 11.1% in the three weeks to 14 April 2000 and then recovered. On 1 September 2000 it closed at 1,520.77, 0.4% below the March record. The Nasdaq Composite was a different picture: 37.3% down at its 23 May low, and still 16% below its peak on the day the S&P retested. AP market report, 1 Sep 2000

Market reports in March and April 2000 carried strategists describing the Nasdaq’s fall as a healthy correction, and one called a bottom on 4 April, the day of a 13.6% intraday plunge. AP market report, 4 Apr 2000 The Fed’s report to Congress in July 2000 said company earnings had mostly beaten expectations and that forecasts were still being revised higher. Fed Monetary Policy Report, Jul 2000 Wall Street strategists’ year-end targets for 2000 could not be retrieved: not verified.

2007: a 10% dip, a rally on Fed speeches, an official forecast of no recession

The S&P 500 was 10.1% below its peak on 26 November 2007. Two Fed speeches on 28 and 29 November signalled more rate cuts, and the index rose 7.7% in two weeks. The November low broke on 15 January 2008.

On 11 December 2007 the Fed’s staff told the rate-setting committee that their forecast showed slow growth and no recession. The National Bureau of Economic Research later dated the start of the recession to that same month. FOMC transcript, 11 Dec 2007 NBER, 1 Dec 2008

Despite all the financial turmoil, the economy avoids recession. — Fed staff director, FOMC meeting, 11 Dec 2007

The same measures in November 2026

  • S&P 500 −11.6% in 33 sessions from the 6 October 2026 record. Nasdaq 100 −19.8%. The semiconductor index is 42.5% below its 22 June 2026 high.
  • The average stock has barely moved. The equal-weight S&P 500 is −2.3% since 6 October, the median of about 635 large stocks is −1.3%, and 47% of them are higher than on 6 October.
  • Consumer staples +10.6% and health care +6.1% since the peak; technology −26.2%. The strongest fifth of stocks by prior 12-month return is down 22% at the median.
What to take from it
  • Depth after six weeks carried no information: bears and corrections overlapped completely.
  • In both bears, officials and earnings forecasts gave no warning at this stage.
  • The differences were in leadership, the Fed’s direction, the yield curve, credit and new supply. Each of these was visible at the time, and each had also given false signals in other years.
Limits Two bears are a small sample. The 1998 and 2015 corrections also began with small companies and transports lagging. Press quotations from the time could not be retrieved.
