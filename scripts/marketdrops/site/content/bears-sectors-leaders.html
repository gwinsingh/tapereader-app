One bear had many places to hide, the other had almost none

In 2000–02 the damage was in technology and telecoms; the median large stock that survived was roughly flat and half of them rose. In 2007–09 almost everything fell by half. In both, the groups that had resisted cracked in the last leg, several groups bottomed before the index, and most leaders of the next advance had made their own lows months earlier.

  • 2000–02 was a bear in the leaders. The fifth of stocks with the best returns in the year before the peak fell 47.4% at the median; the weakest fifth rose 39.8%. By sector the median technology stock fell 75.6% while the median staples stock rose 32.7% and the median property company 38.4%.
  • 2007–09 was a bear in everything. Only 2.5% of stocks rose. Prior strength made no difference: the strongest fifth fell 55.5% and the weakest 50.4%. The equal-weight S&P 500 fell more than the index (−59.2% against −55.2%).
  • Calendar years tell the same story. An equal-weighted index of about 1,700 stocks rose 9.6% in 2000 and 10.9% in 2001 before falling 17.1% in 2002. Property trusts returned +26.4%, +13.9% and +3.8% in those three years. Nareit annual returns

What defied each bear, and when it cracked

  • 2000–02. Homebuilders, health insurers, defence contractors, tobacco and discount retailers made their lows between October 1999 and the week the Nasdaq peaked, then rose for two years. From 24 March 2000 to 9 October 2002: NVR +410%, Lockheed Martin +241%, UnitedHealth +217%, AutoZone +203%, Lennar +139% (study data). Berkshire Hathaway made its low on 10 March 2000, the day of the Nasdaq peak.
  • They broke late. Most of these winners topped between March and July 2002 and lost 20–45% into October: NVR −29% from 2 May, Toll Brothers −43% from 14 May, Philip Morris −37% from 4 June.
  • 2007–09. Energy, materials, steel, fertiliser and solar shares led until May and June 2008, eight months into the bear, then fell 70–90% by November. After Lehman only Treasuries (+25% for the long-bond fund over the whole bear), gold (+24%), the dollar and a few discount retailers held: AutoZone +22%, Ross Stores +10%, Wal-Mart +7%.
  • The defensive groups went last. From 20 November 2008 to 9 March 2009 utilities lost 13%, staples 12% and health care 7% while technology was flat.

In 2002–03 the pattern showed at the retest instead: on 11 March 2003 the Nasdaq was 14% and the semiconductor index 30% above their October lows, while transports, staples and European markets made new lows.

Leaders of the next advance

From 2003. Fifteen of the later big winners reached a 52-week high within seven weeks of 15 October 2002, and every one had made its own low 12 to 31 months before the index: eBay (low December 2000), Sohu and NetEase (April and July 2001), Amazon (28 September 2001), Cognizant, Coach, UnitedHealth, Career Education, Teva, Gilead, Garmin. A second wave broke out in the thirteen weeks after 17 March 2003: Netflix, Starbucks, Amgen, the homebuilders, Research In Motion, Freeport-McMoRan, Marvell. Apple made its low late, on 17 April 2003, five weeks after the index retest.

From 2009. The growth leaders made their lows between 27 October and 10 December 2008 and stood well above them when the index bottomed on 9 March 2009: Netflix +115%, Freeport-McMoRan +92%, Amazon +73%, MercadoLibre +70%, Priceline +66%, Baidu +48%, Goldman Sachs +42%, Salesforce +41%, Chipotle +27%. Netflix made a 52-week high on 17 March 2009, six sessions after the low. A second group bottomed with the index and gave no early sign: Lululemon, Ulta, Las Vegas Sands and the large banks.

What rebounded hardest

In both cases the fifth of stocks that had fallen most gained most in the next year: +70.5% in 2002–03 and +150.5% in 2009–10 at the median, against +25.6% and +40.2% for the fifth that had fallen least. The winners of each bear lagged afterwards: in 2009 Wal-Mart lost 4.7% and McDonald’s gained 0.4%.

What to take from it
  • 2000–02 punished what had risen most; 2007–09 punished everything, and financials most.
  • Resisting a bear for two years did not protect a group in its last three months.
  • In 2008–09 most growth groups stopped falling on 20 November 2008, 72 sessions before the index low.
  • Two kinds of next-cycle winner existed: stocks holding far above their own lows as the index bottomed, and late casualties that bottomed with it.
