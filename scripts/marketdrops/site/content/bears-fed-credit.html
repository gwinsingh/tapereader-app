The Fed cut by hundreds of basis points in both bears, and shares kept falling

In 2001 the Federal Reserve cut its policy rate by 475 basis points (4.75 percentage points) and the S&P 500 still fell 42% from the first cut to the low. In 2007–08 it cut by 325 basis points in seven months and the index fell 55% from the first cut. The first problem was profits and overbuilt capacity; the second was the solvency of lenders.

2001–02: cheap money could not restore profits

  • The downturn was not caused by tight money, so easy money did not reverse it. It was led by a fall in profits, then by cuts in stocks of goods and in business investment. BIS Annual Report 2002
  • Too much had been built. Telephone companies were investing at a rate of about 33% of their capital in 2000 against a long-run 18%. Spending on communications equipment fell more than 30% in 2001. New York Fed, 2004
  • Profits of non-financial companies fell at an annual rate of about 45% in late 2000 and about 25% a year through 2001. Fed Monetary Policy Report, Feb 2002
  • The cuts did work where borrowing responds to rates: housing, cars and consumer spending. The recession lasted eight months.
  • The 2002 leg happened with the rate flat at 1.75%. It was about trust in accounts after Enron and WorldCom.

  • Losses sat on thin capital. The large investment banks held up to 40 dollars of assets for each dollar of equity, so a fall of less than 3% in asset values could erase a firm. Financial Crisis Inquiry Commission report
  • Short-term funding ran. Lenders in the repo market (overnight loans against securities) demanded more collateral or left, which forced sales, which lowered prices again. Brunnermeier, 2009 Gorton and Metrick, 2009
  • The Fed chairman said in January 2009 that the effect of the rate cuts had been incomplete because credit markets were not working. Bernanke, 13 Jan 2009
  • Mortgage rates fell only when the Fed bought mortgage bonds itself: from 6.04% on 20 November to 5.10% on 31 December 2008.

What to take from it
  • A first rate cut was not a floor in either bear. The index fell 42% and 55% after it.
  • What ended 2007–09 was capital, guarantees and asset purchases. What ended 2000–02 was earnings stabilising at lower valuations.
  • In November 2026 the Fed is raising rates (16 September 2026) and the Baa spread has widened only 0.03–0.10 point since the peak.
Limits Credit spreads widened in every correction too: by a full point in three months in 1998. High-yield spread history could not be retrieved; the Baa spread stands in.
