February 2018: a volatility accident inside an earnings boom

The S&P 500 fell 10.2% in nine sessions, from the closing high of 26 January 2018 to the closing low of 8 February. The trigger was a wage number. The speed came from funds that had bet on calm markets and were forced to unwind in one afternoon.

The set-up

On 26 January the index stood 14.0% above its 200-day average after gaining 25.1% in a year (study price data). The VIX (expected volatility priced into index options) was 11.08. Margin debt had jumped $23bn in January to $665.7bn FINRA. A popular trade was to sell volatility through exchange-traded notes such as XIV, which held $1.9bn.

The sequence

  • 2 Feb. The jobs report showed hourly earnings up 2.9% on the year BLS, 2 Feb 2018. Faster wages meant faster Fed tightening. Bond yields rose and the Dow fell 666 points.
  • 5 Feb. Selling accelerated in the afternoon. The Dow lost 1,175 points (−4.6%). The VIX went from 17.31 to 37.32. Products built to profit from falling volatility had to buy volatility futures into the close, which pushed volatility higher still. XIV lost about 85% after hours CNBC, 6 Feb 2018.
  • 6 Feb. The VIX touched 50.3 intraday. Credit Suisse announced it would wind up XIV. JPMorgan estimated that volatility-driven funds might sell about $100bn of stocks in total.
  • 8 Feb. −3.8% to 2,581, the closing low AP, 8 Feb 2018.
  • 9 Feb. The index fell further during the morning, bottomed in the early afternoon and closed higher Value Line, 9 Feb 2018. That intraday low was not broken again.

Fear was milder than it would be in December. The total put/call ratio (puts traded divided by calls) peaked at 1.31 on 9 February, against 1.82 on 20 December Cboe. Only 7.3% of the study’s stocks were at 52-week lows at the closing low, against 39.0% on 24 December. Earnings were growing 24.6% FactSet, 1 Jun 2018.

A fast rebound, then two tests of the low

The first rebound was quick: a follow-through day on session 4 and closes back above the 20-day EMA (exponential moving average) and the 50-day average by session 5, 5.8% above the low. The index then spent seven weeks going back down to the same closing level.

  • The VIX closed below 20 within four sessions of the low.
  • First test, 22–23 March. Tariffs on Chinese goods were announced; the Dow fell 724 points on 22 March NPR, 22 Mar 2018. The total put/call ratio reached 1.54 on 23 March, higher than in February. The exact close that day is not verified.
  • Second test, 2 April. China retaliated and the President attacked Amazon Value Line, 2 Apr 2018. The close on session 35 after the low matched the 8 February close to within 0.1% (study price data). The VIX was 23.62, against 33.46 at the February low.

So the largest pullback in the 60 sessions after the low was −7.3%, and it returned the index to its starting point with much less fear. Nearly half of the study’s stocks (46.3%) made their own low in this second phase, five or more sessions after the index’s closing low. Several sectors did too: financials and staples on 23 March, energy on 28 March, health care, materials and consumer discretionary on 2 April, industrials on 9 April. Technology, the Nasdaq 100, chips, software and small caps held their 8 February lows.

What to take from it
  • The rebound confirmed within five sessions and was still followed by a full test of the low seven weeks later.
  • On the test, the index matched its low while the VIX was ten points lower and the leading groups stayed above theirs.

What led afterwards

Growth led and it showed at once. The groups that would break in the fourth quarter were the leaders of this recovery.

Stocks at new 52-week highs within five sessions of the low included Fortinet, Arista, Amazon, HubSpot, Lululemon, Shopify, Workday and Adobe; 29.4% of the universe made a new high within 20 sessions, against 4.5% after the December low. The best performers over 126 sessions were Twilio (+226%), Etsy (+147%), Dexcom (+126%), Okta (+99%), Chipotle (+83%) and AMD (+70%).

The index made a new closing record on 24 August Fortune, 24 Aug 2018. Industrials, materials, financials and staples never regained their January highs, and emerging markets kept falling. The recovery was narrower than the advance it followed.

Limits February differed from December in kind: a positioning shock with earnings rising, against a policy and growth scare with estimates falling. The claim that the 9 February intraday low touched the 200-day average is not verified.
