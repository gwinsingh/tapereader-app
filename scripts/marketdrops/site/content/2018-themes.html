The leaders of 2017–18 were the stocks that fell most

The themes that carried the market into September 2018 were large technology, chips, cloud software, the tax-cut earnings boom, cannabis and small-cap biotech. In the study’s universe, the fifth of stocks with the largest gains in the prior year fell most in the decline. Unless stated otherwise, all percentages on this page are from the study’s price data and run from the index high of 20 September to the index low of 24 December 2018.

The top fifth of the universe by prior-year gain (median +62%) had a median fall of −23.6%, against −16% to −21% for the other fifths. The same group then had the best median gain 126 sessions after the low, +39.9%.

What fell most, what held, and when the holdouts cracked

Energy, small caps, banks and the speculative end of growth fell most. Utilities, real estate, staples and gold held. The defensive groups gave way only in the last sessions, and all of them made their lows on 24 December with the index.

These figures use one fixed window. Measured from each group’s own high the falls are larger, because most groups peaked earlier: energy −31.2% from May, financials −26.1% and industrials −25.2% from January, the Russell 2000 −27.3% from 31 August CNBC, 24 Dec 2018.

Among single stocks the gainers were gold miners (AngloGold +45%, Agnico Eagle +16%, Newmont +10%), utilities (Duke +5%, American Electric Power +4%) and steady consumer names (Starbucks +8%, McDonald’s +7%, AutoZone +8%, Procter & Gamble +3%). In all, 8.5% of the 532 stocks rose and 66% fell 20% or more.

When the defensives cracked

Utilities closed at a one-year high on 4 December, a day the index lost 3.2% CNBC, 4 Dec 2018. They fell 4.3% on 24 December, their worst day since August 2011. At the low 63% of utility stocks in the universe were still above their 200-day average, against 0% of energy stocks and 1.4% of industrials and of financials. At year-end strategists’ favourite sectors for 2019 were the defensive ones, which then traded at higher valuations than technology Reuters, 29 Dec 2018.

What to take from it
  • The last groups to be sold were the ones that had held all quarter; that happened on the low day.
  • The consensus at the low favoured the groups that then lagged: utilities were 8.3 points behind the index 126 sessions later.

What turned before the index

Very little made its price low early. Gold miners, emerging markets, Chinese large caps and solar did. For chips, homebuilders and growth, the price low was 24 December, but their relative-strength lines (price divided by the S&P 500) had turned up weeks before.

Press reports at the time also dated the emerging-markets low to late October CNBC, 27 Nov 2018.

Limits A relative-strength low is only known afterwards. Homebuilders and chips are often remembered as having “bottomed in October”; on price they did not. They fell with the index to 24 December and merely fell less.

What led afterwards

The first weeks belonged to the most beaten-down stocks. The lasting leaders were software, chips, solar, housing and recent IPOs. Energy, retail, banks, small caps and the defensive sectors lagged.

Depth of fall and size of bounce

In the first 21 sessions the stocks that had fallen most bounced most: the fifth with the deepest falls (median −33.3%) gained a median 21.4%, the fifth that fell least gained 7.3%. By 126 sessions the link had almost gone (+28.3% against +21.1%), and at 252 sessions the middle fifths were ahead (+47% to +48%). Oil producers show the pattern: +26.6% in 21 sessions, then nothing for a year. DataTrek observed the same thing in January: the worst stocks of 2018 led the first weeks, a rebound of tax-loss victims Yahoo Finance, 19 Jan 2019.

Two kinds of leader appear. Software and payments names fell less than the index or about as much, and were at new highs within 8 to 30 sessions. Chips and other deep fallers needed 55 to 140 sessions to reach new highs and did most of their gaining later in 2019. Contemporary technicians noted the first kind in real time: on 15 January Oppenheimer pointed to Salesforce’s higher low in December and PayPal’s break through resistance, and the software index was the first major group at an all-time high on 22 February CNBC, 15 Jan 2019 StockCharts, 25 Feb 2019.

What to take from it
  • Depth of fall predicted the size of the first month’s bounce and little beyond it.
  • The lasting leaders were visible in two ways: they stopped underperforming weeks before the low (19 November for software and growth), and they reached new 52-week highs within about 30 sessions of it.
  • Half of the old leadership (FAANG, crypto-linked chips, cannabis, Chinese internet) did not lead the next advance.
