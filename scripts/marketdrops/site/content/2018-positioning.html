Borrowed money peaked four months before prices and did not come back with them

Margin debt (money borrowed from brokers against securities) peaked in May 2018, made a lower high at the September price record, fell 17% from its peak by December and was still 14% below it in March 2019 with the index near its old high.

  • October −$40.5bn, November −$15.1bn, December −$38.3bn. The fourth-quarter fall of $93.8bn was the largest since the fourth quarter of 2008 Wolf Street, 18 Jan 2019.
  • Cash went up as debt went down: free credit balances in margin accounts rose from $178.8bn in November to $190.7bn in December.
  • As a share of the economy, the FINRA peak was roughly 3.2% of GDP not verified (own arithmetic). The study’s long-run margin series is a different, lower-level measure: about 1.5% of GDP in January 2018, with year-on-year growth of +27% in January 2018, +16% in July 2018 and −12% in January 2019 (study data).
What to take from it
  • Margin debt gave a divergence at the top: a lower high in September against a higher high in price.
  • It gave no signal at the low; the monthly figure for December was published in mid-January.

Hedge funds were at post-crisis highs in leverage and crowded into the same stocks

The Federal Reserve flagged hedge-fund leverage in November 2018 and reported in May 2019 that it had come down late in the year. The most popular hedge-fund stocks fell more than the market. Exact leverage figures from prime brokers (the bank units that lend to hedge funds) were not found.

  • Fed, November 2018: some indicators put hedge-fund leverage at post-crisis highs; a broad measure rose by about one-third over 2016 and 2017 Financial Stability Report, Nov 2018.
  • Fed, May 2019: gross leverage rose through early 2018 and fell at the end of the year as volatility rose, most clearly at equity funds Financial Stability Report, May 2019.
  • Crowding: Goldman Sachs’s basket of the most popular hedge-fund holdings lagged the S&P 500 by more than seven percentage points from mid-June to November 2018, and the 50 most popular stocks made up 32% of hedge-fund long positions. These figures come from press summaries of bank notes whose pages could not be opened not verified.
  • Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley prime-brokerage net and gross leverage for October–December 2018: not verified, no figures found.
  • Results: the HFRI composite index of hedge funds lost 4.7% in 2018, its worst year since 2011 CMC Markets. On Preqin’s count 59% of funds lost money, and 746 funds closed against 609 opened Chief Investment Officer. Clients withdrew $22.5bn in the fourth quarter Bloomberg, 18 Jan 2019.

The crowded positions were the 2017–18 leaders. Nvidia lost 54% in the fourth quarter, the worst stock in the S&P 500 CNBC, 21 Dec 2018. In the study’s universe, the fifth of stocks that had risen most in the year before the high (median +62%) fell most in the decline (median −23.6%).

Rule-driven selling and thin order books made each leg faster

Several kinds of funds sell automatically when volatility rises or trends break. Their selling met an order book with a third of its usual depth. Bank estimates of the amounts differ widely and should be treated as rough.

Systematic strategies

  • 10 Oct: JPMorgan’s Kolanovic named option hedging by dealers as the largest forced seller of the day, said trend-following funds had largely finished, and expected volatility-targeting funds (which cut holdings as volatility rises) to sell for several more days. He judged systematic selling about 70% complete on 12 October Bloomberg, 12 Oct 2018.
  • 4 Dec: a Nomura strategist estimated that trend-following funds added about $42bn of exposure on the 3 December rally and cut it to a fifth the next day. A Nomura colleague and other banks disputed this AFR excerpt, 6 Dec 2018.
  • S&P 500 option open interest reached a record above 21 million contracts in December S&P Dow Jones Indices, 27 Dec 2018.

Liquidity

  • Goldman Sachs, 18 Dec: the depth of the S&P 500 futures order book was down 70% in a year, to a ten-year low Fortune, 30 Dec 2018.
  • JPMorgan, March 2019: depth in the fourth quarter was less than a third of the average of other selloffs of the decade, so the same order moved the price about three times as far, in both directions Bloomberg, 21 Mar 2019.
  • The Fed’s minutes: the late-December selling was amplified by poor liquidity and thin year-end trading Fed minutes, Jan 2019.

Year-end flows: record fund outflows, tax selling, then pension buying

December’s selling had a calendar component. Investors sold mutual funds and losing stocks before year-end, partly for tax reasons, and pension funds had to buy stocks in the last days of the quarter.

  • Week to 12 Dec: a record $46.2bn left US stock mutual funds and exchange-traded funds (ETFs) together CNBC, 14 Dec 2018.
  • Week to 19 Dec: long-term mutual funds lost $56.2bn, the most since October 2008 InvestmentNews.
  • December in full: stock and mixed mutual funds −$115.4bn, the most since at least January 2008; money-market funds +$63.1bn; but ETFs +$50.1bn Lipper, Jan 2019. Part of the mutual-fund outflow was a switch into ETFs, which is how a tax loss is booked without leaving the market.
  • Tax-loss selling: DataTrek estimated about $41bn in December against about $25bn of offsetting ETF purchases, accelerating from 14 December once a down year looked likely Yahoo Finance, 19 Jan 2019.
  • Pension rebalancing (funds restoring their stock share after stocks fell): Wells Fargo estimated about $60bn of stock buying into year-end, $35bn of it large companies. The late-day reversal of 27 December was attributed to it Fortune, 28 Dec 2018.
  • Buybacks: S&P 500 companies bought back a record $223bn of their shares in the fourth quarter S&P Dow Jones Indices, 25 Mar 2019. The dates of the pre-earnings blackout windows, often cited for early October, are not verified.
  • Small option traders bought nearly 3 million puts in the week before 24 December, the second-highest on record, and insiders bought their own shares at the highest rate relative to sales since August 2011 SentimenTrader, 24 Dec 2018 Bloomberg, 26 Dec 2018.

JPMorgan’s reading in March 2019 was that short covering, buybacks, dealer hedging and a little re-leveraging had driven the whole recovery Bloomberg, 21 Mar 2019. Margin debt agrees Wolf Street, 23 Apr 2019.

What to take from it
  • Leverage had been cut hard by the low: margin debt −17% from its peak, record fund outflows, hedge-fund leverage reduced.
  • Three flows reversed at the calendar turn: tax selling ended on 31 December, pension buying arrived in the last week, and forced selling by rule-driven funds stops when volatility stops rising.
  • The recovery was not built on re-borrowing. Prices recovered first; leverage lagged by many months.
