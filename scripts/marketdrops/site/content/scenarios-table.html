Correction or the start of a bear: what could be observed, gauge by gauge

The table compares four corrections inside rising markets (1998, 2011, 2015–16, 2018) with the starts of four bears (2000, 2007, and as further cases 1973 and 2022). For each gauge it shows what happened in each group, when the gauge moved against the index peak, how well it separated the groups, and its reading on 20 November 2026. No single gauge separated the groups cleanly.

Terms: leading means visible before the index peak; coincident, within about three months after it; lagging, later. The Baa spread is the extra yield on the lowest investment-grade company bonds over Treasuries. CAPE is price divided by ten years of inflation-adjusted earnings. Jobless claims are new weekly claims for unemployment benefit, four-week average.

The November 2026 readings, grouped

  • Like the start of a bear: other indices below their highs at the record; a Federal Reserve that is raising rates; margin debt that rose fast and peaked before the index; heavy new supply of shares and bonds; a fall concentrated in the former leaders while the average stock holds, as in spring 2000.
  • Like a correction: the Baa spread has barely moved; banks are not tightening loan standards; jobless claims are at their low.
  • Neutral or uninformative: the yield curve; earnings forecasts (rising, as in spring 2000); valuation (far below 2000 on forward earnings, near it on CAPE); depth; the 200-day average.

A similar mix of readings was present in spring 2000 and in January 2022. In 2000 the credit and labour confirmation arrived six to nine months later. In 2022 it never came and the decline ended at −25.4%.

What to take from it
  • The gauges that separated the two great bears from corrections were credit, bank lending, jobless claims and the behaviour of former leaders on rallies. All confirmed after the peak.
  • The gauges visible before the peak (leadership divergence, yield curve, valuation, margin debt) were present in the bears but also gave false signals.
Limits Eight episodes; the reliability column is a reading of that small sample. The corrections were measured, not researched for events. The page describes readings and does not say which case 2026 is.
