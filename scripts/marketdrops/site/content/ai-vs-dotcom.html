The case that 2026 is not 2000: real profits, lower valuations, visible demand

On price against expected earnings the market of November 2026 is far from March 2000. The largest companies earn very large profits, the technology sector trades below its own 25-year average multiple, and demand for computing is still being reported as stronger than supply. Each of these points is supported by 2026 data. Each also has a counterpart in the record of 2000, set out in the next section.

Terms: forward P/E is price divided by the next twelve months’ expected earnings. Capex is capital spending, here mostly data centres and chips. Free cash flow is cash from operations minus capex.

Profits are real

  • S&P 500 earnings per share rose 31.9% in the third quarter of 2026 on revenue growth of 14.4%. Analysts expect +33.3% for 2026 and +16.2% for 2027, and they raised fourth-quarter estimates during October, which is unusual. FactSet, 13 Nov 2026
  • Nvidia reported revenue of $115.8bn and net income of $65.3bn for the quarter to 25 October 2026, with a gross margin of 74.8%. Nvidia, 17 Nov 2026
  • In 1999 and 2000, 76% and 81% of companies that went public were losing money. In 2025 the share was 53%, on a fifth of the number of offerings. Jay Ritter, IPO statistics

Valuations are lower

  • The S&P 500 traded at 18.3 times expected earnings on 12 November 2026 and at about 16.7 times at the 20 November close (an estimate that holds forecasts constant). At the March 2000 peak it was 25.2. FactSet, 9 Nov 2026
  • The technology sector’s forward P/E was 17.3 on 5 November 2026, below its 25-year average of 20.0.
  • At its peak in March 2000 Cisco was valued at more than $500bn against $2.67bn of net income in its fiscal year 2000: more than 187 times. Nvidia at $128 trades at about 12 times its latest quarter’s earnings annualised (derived here; consensus figures not verified).

Demand and funding

  • Anthropic’s revenue was $33.2bn in the first nine months of 2026 against $2.5bn a year earlier, according to its listing document as summarised by a tracker. Leverage Shares tracker, Nov 2026
  • Rental prices for older Nvidia chips were 14% higher in September 2026 than a year before, so existing capacity was not being dumped. In 2002, by contrast, at most 2% of long-distance fibre was in use. Brookings, Dec 2002
  • The big builders’ debt is low against earnings: 1.3 times a year’s operating profit before depreciation, against 2.7 times for the median investment-grade company (30 September 2026). J.P. Morgan Guide to the Markets, 4Q 2026
What to take from it
  • The buyers of AI equipment are among the most profitable companies that have existed; the buyers of telecom equipment in 2000 were start-ups living on borrowed money.
  • On forward earnings the index is roughly 30% cheaper than in March 2000.

The case that it rhymes: the capital cycle, the financing, and what happened to the profitable leaders of 2000

The dot-com leaders were also highly profitable, and their earnings collapsed within a year once customers stopped ordering. In 2026 the spending on AI has outrun the cash the builders generate, funding has moved to bonds, leases and new shares, and the weakest borrowers are being repriced. Concentration in the index is higher than in 2000.

Cisco, Intel and Lucent were money-making leaders

  • Intel. Net income fell from $10.5bn in 2000 to $1.3bn in 2001 (−88%) on a 21% fall in revenue. Intel annual report 2001
  • Cisco. From a profit of $2.67bn in fiscal 2000 to a loss of $1.01bn in fiscal 2001, after writing off $2.25bn of unsold stock. Revenue fell 15% the year after. Cisco annual report 2001 In the study’s data the shares fell 88% during the bear; they first closed above their 2000 high in December 2025 (not verified beyond a headline).
  • Lucent. It had promised $8.1bn of loans to its own customers by September 2000 (vendor financing). It lost $14.2bn the next year and its shares fell 99%. Lucent annual report 2001
  • In the study’s data Intel and Oracle each fell 81% and Qualcomm 81% between March 2000 and October 2002.

The capital cycle in 2026

  • The four largest builders (Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta) spent 115% of their operating cash flow on capex in the third quarter of 2026, against 41% in 2023. Their combined free cash flow was −$26bn in the quarter. Yield Theory tracker, Nov 2026
  • Their net cash fell from $178bn to $10bn in a year. Five builders sold $284bn of bonds in 2026 to mid-November, against $108bn in all of 2025. Yield Theory tracker
  • Shares are being issued as well: the two largest initial public offerings ever (SpaceX in June, Anthropic on 18–19 November 2026) and large sales by Meta, Alphabet and Oracle. One investment firm had named this new supply of shares in September as the thing that ends bubbles. GMO, 24 Sep 2026
  • The weakest credits are under strain. Oracle was cut to the lowest investment grade in July 2026 and paid 7.00–8.65% on bonds in November. The cost of insuring CoreWeave’s debt passed 12 percentage points a year. Yield Theory tracker
  • Suppliers finance customers. Nvidia holds about $110bn of securities and has backed an OpenAI data-centre lease with $105bn; suppliers bought $26bn of Anthropic shares at its offering. Wikipedia, OpenAI
  • Scale: AI investment was put at 3.6% of GDP in September 2026, against 1.1% for the internet build-out of 1996–2003 (Wall Street Journal figure, read through a secondary page). Wikipedia, AI bubble

Concentration and the other measure of value

  • The ten largest companies were 40.6% of the S&P 500’s value on 30 September 2026. The figure usually quoted for 2000 is about 27%: not verified.
  • CAPE (price divided by ten years of inflation-adjusted earnings) was 40.6 in October 2026 against a record 44.2 in December 1999. It is high because today’s earnings are far above their ten-year average. multpl.com
  • The Federal Reserve is raising rates, as it was in 2000: +0.25 point on 16 September 2026. FOMC, 16 Sep 2026

How the tape compares so far

From the 6 October 2026 record to 20 November the S&P 500 is −11.6% and the Nasdaq 100 −19.8%, while the equal-weight S&P 500 is −2.3% and 47% of large stocks are higher. The semiconductor index is 42.5% below its 22 June high. Staples are +10.6% and health care +6.1%. In spring 2000 the picture was similar in kind: the Nasdaq fell 37% in ten weeks while the Dow rose and the median surviving large stock ended the whole bear roughly flat.

What to take from it
  • Profitability did not protect the leaders of 2000: their customers’ spending stopped, and earnings followed.
  • The financing of the build-out changed during 2026, from cash flow to debt and new shares.
  • The first stress in 2026 is in financing, not in reported demand. In 2000 it also began with customers running out of money.
Limits This section describes both sides and makes no forecast. Estimates were also still rising in spring 2000, so rising estimates settle nothing either way.
