# 2018 US market drops: research notes (raw material)

Compiled 20 Nov 2026. Scope: the Jan–Feb 2018 "Volmageddon" correction and, above all, the 20 Sep – 24 Dec 2018 decline and the Jan–Apr 2019 recovery.

**How to read this file**

- Every fact carries a source key in square brackets; keys resolve to URLs in the source list at the end.
- `[UNVERIFIED]` = written from memory or from a single weak source and **not** confirmed against a page in this session. Treat as a lead to check, not as a fact.
- The web-search budget for this session ran out part-way through. After that only direct page fetches of known URLs were possible (Fed, FINRA, CBOE, FRED, FactSet PDFs, Value Line daily recaps, CNBC, etc.). Gaps that result are listed in section 12.
- Price levels and returns are deliberately sparse (you compute those). Where an index level appears it is quoted from the cited article.
- Data pulled straight from primary files: FINRA margin statistics (xlsx), CBOE put/call history (csv), FRED (VIX, VIX3M, Treasury yields, WTI, mortgage rate, core PCE index), FactSet Earnings Insight PDFs (text extracted locally), Fed minutes/statements/FSR, BLS releases.

### What practitioners said

- Mike Wilson (Morgan Stanley), 19 Nov: "Buying the dip has not worked in 2018 for the first time since 2002"; the only years it failed were bear-market years. **Caveat for the study:** the market also shrugged off bad news for one or two days at earlier failed lows (23 Oct intraday recovery after CAT/MMM; 6 Dec recovery from −785 after Huawei; 10 Dec hammer). ## 8. Character of the price action

### The down-legs

- **Sudden, and not on scheduled news.** 10 Oct (−3.3%), 24 Oct (−3.1%), 4 Dec (−3.2%) each erased one to three weeks of gains in a day. 10 Oct had no trigger at all; Kolanovic attributed it to option gamma hedging [BBG-KOL-1012].
- Three legs: 3–29 Oct (rates + earnings reactions), 8–23 Nov (oil, Apple, NVDA, FAANG), 4–24 Dec (trade, curve, Fed, shutdown, year-end). Each started from a lower high and went to a lower low.
- The final leg was a persistence event, not a single crash: from 14 Dec the S&P fell on 7 of 8 sessions (Russell 13 of 14) [CNBC-1224]; SentimenTrader on 20 Dec: first time since 2008 that 3 of 4 sessions fell 1.5%+ to a 52-week low, otherwise mostly a 1930s pattern [ST-1220].

### Rallies inside the decline, and how they failed

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12–16 Oct | bank earnings, strong data | 3 sessions | 18 Oct rates/China; broke the low on 23–24 Oct | [VL 10-12-2018], [VL 10-16-2018], [VL 10-18-2018] |
| 30 Oct – 7 Nov | oversold; FB; Trump–Xi call; midterms | 7 sessions, to 2,813 | FOMC with no dovish shift (8 Nov), oil, Apple suppliers (12 Nov, Dow −602) | [VL 10-30-2018], [FORTUNE-1107], [VL 11-12-2018] |

Common features: started by a headline, biggest gains in the first one or two days (often gaps), led by the most beaten-down groups, no 90% up day and no breadth thrust [ST-0107], on 28 Nov more new lows than new highs [VL 11-28-2018], each stopped at a lower high. Wilson called the early-November bounce a "dead cat bounce" [BI-MS].

### Intraday behaviour

- **Selling was a day-session phenomenon.** Bespoke: "all of the declines have come during regular trading hours"; buying every open and selling every close would have lost 20.4% in 2018, while the overnight (close-to-open) strategy kept rising until the intraday one "started to collapse" [BESPOKE-INTRADAY, via Yahoo/AOL summary; original page 404 in this session].
- **Gaps up were sold; morning strength faded.** 11 Oct (morning rally sold), 29 Oct (strong open, ~900-pt Dow range, close on the lows), 2 Nov (+200 → −300), 7 Dec (opened higher, sold all day), 11 Dec (opening gain gone by midday), 19 Dec (+300 → −352), 20 Dec ("sold off dramatically in the afternoon") [VL recaps for each date]. Bespoke flagged a reversal from +1.5% intraday to −1.5% at the close as the first since Feb 2009 [BESPOKE-INTRADAY].
- **Intraday recoveries from big losses happened repeatedly and did not mark the low**: 23 Oct (−549 → −126), 6 Dec (−785 → −79), 10 Dec. Only 27 Dec (−611 → +260), which came **after** a 90% up day, held.
- Liquidity: futures depth a third of normal; the same flow moved price three times as far [INV-KOL-0319]. This is why both the last down-leg and the first up-leg were so steep.

### What day and swing traders said worked

Evidence here is thin and I will not invent it. What is documented:

- Buying dips failed all quarter (Morgan Stanley's statistic; Wilson's "lost it every turn") [CNBC-BTD], [CNBC-MS-1220].
- Selling strength / fading gaps worked, by construction of the intraday record above.
- The IBD method had traders out: the market status was "in correction" until the 4 Jan follow-through day [MONEYSHOW-FTD]. An October follow-through attempt `[UNVERIFIED]` failed within days, as did one in late November/early December; this is the IBD experience most often cited, but I could not retrieve the dated IBD pages.
- Kerry Given (MoneyShow, 7 Jan 2019) after the follow-through day: still cautious, "this market is going to have to show me that it has gotten over its 'sky is falling' fears" [MONEYSHOW-FTD]. Disbelief was the norm.
- I found **no** first-hand, dated day-trader account for this period in the session. `[UNVERIFIED / GAP]`

### The rebound: speed and the retest debate

- 26 Dec: +5.0%. 27 Dec: intraday −2.8% (a buyer who waited got a second chance within 24 hours, about 2% above the low), closed up. 28 Dec – 2 Jan: sideways. 3 Jan: −2.5% on Apple/ISM, closing about 4% above the 24 Dec close: this was the only "retest", a higher low. 4 Jan: +3.4% and the follow-through. After 4 Jan the index never traded near those levels again.
- So a trader had **seven sessions** (26 Dec – 4 Jan) in a ~7% range above the low, then it was gone. Ten straight days without a meaningful pullback followed; the S&P was above its 50-day on 17 Jan [BNN-0117].
- Piper Jaffray's Craig Johnson (23 Jan): a 13% gain in 17 sessions had 12 precedents in 50 years; eight were V-bottoms after major lows, four were double bottoms that were not major lows [CNBC-0123].
- **Who expected a retest:** Mike Wilson (Morgan Stanley), mid-Jan: S&P likely "back to its December lows at around 2,350", buy cyclicals there [BBG-MS-RETEST]. Tom Lee (Fundstrat): history says it may revisit the low; would buy it [YAHOO-LEE]. Adam Robinson: fast declines "statistically ... always been followed by a retest"; 4/7 odds it was a bear-market rally [YAHOO-RETEST]. Wellington Shields (Q4 review): "not yet time to signal an all-clear"; several months of basing is normal [WS-0119]. A strategist on CNBC as late as 11 Feb: "stocks are heading back to December lows" [CNBC-0211]. Many technicians called a V "possible" but "not the most likely scenario" [INV-RETEST].
- **Who did not:** Jonathan Golub (Credit Suisse), 31 Dec: "the bottom has been put in this market"; fundamentals did not justify the fall [CNBC-1231]. Nick Colas: after a +6% January the market has "never been down for the year"; any retest "should be bought" [YAHOO-RETEST]. Frank Gretz (Wellington Shields), 18 Jan: a test is less likely given the strength and the "two 95% up-volume days" [WS-0119]. Dudack: December prices likely "the worst of the decline" [WS-0119].
- Why the retest camp had a case: both prior lows of 2018 **were** retested (Feb → late Mar/2 Apr; Oct → Nov, then broken in Dec) [YAHOO-RETEST]. Why it was wrong: this time the cause (Fed) changed within seven sessions and breadth thrust signals fired, neither of which happened in February or October.
- JPMorgan, March 2019: investors did not chase; exposure still low [INV-KOL-0319]. Margin debt confirms [FINRA]. The rally was climbed on under-positioning.

## 9. The January – February 2018 correction

- **Set-up:** S&P record 26 Jan after one of the strongest Januaries on record; VIX 11.08 [FRED-VIX]; margin debt jumped to $665.7bn in January, +$23bn in a month [FINRA]; forward P/E 18.4 at the start of the year [CNBC-1231]; record-low realised volatility in 2017 and a crowded short-volatility trade (XIV $1.9bn) [CNBC-XIV].
- **Cause:** 2 Feb jobs report with wages +2.9% y/y [BLS-0218] → fear of faster Fed tightening, yields up → Dow −666. Then a mechanical accident on 5 Feb: VIX +115.6% [FRED-VIX], inverse-VIX products forced to buy VIX futures, volatility-targeting and risk-parity funds forced to cut equities (Kolanovic: ~$100bn) [CNBC-XIV].
- **Speed:** 10% in nine sessions (26 Jan – 8 Feb), then the fastest 10% correction from a record high on record `[UNVERIFIED superlative]` [PBS-0208].
- **Low:** intraday washout and reversal on Friday 9 Feb ("bottomed out in early afternoon", bargain hunters before 2 pm) [VL 2-9-2018]. `[UNVERIFIED]` The low tagged the 200-day average.
- **Sentiment at the low was much milder than in December:** total put/call 1.31 (9 Feb) vs 1.82 (20 Dec); equity put/call 0.88 vs 1.13 [CBOE-T], [CBOE-E].
- **Retests:** a rebound to mid-March, then a second decline on tariffs (22–23 Mar) and a retest on 2 Apr (China retaliation, Amazon). VIX 23.62 on 2 Apr vs 37.32 on 5 Feb; total put/call peaked at 1.54 on 23 Mar [FRED-VIX], [CBOE-T], [VL 4-2-2018]. The 9 Feb intraday low held. A textbook retest with lower volatility, seven weeks after the low.
- **Time to new highs:** Nasdaq and Russell 2000 by June `[UNVERIFIED]`; S&P 500 on 24 Aug, seven months after the peak [FORTUNE-0824]; Dow in late September.
- **What led:** technology/FAANG, small caps and growth; industrials, materials, financials and staples never regained their January highs [CNBC-1224].
- **Fundamentals:** earnings +24.6% (Q1) and rising estimates [FS-060118]; no credit stress. A positioning accident inside an earnings boom.
- **Difference from Q4:** February was a volatility shock with fundamentals improving, so the low was a single washout plus a retest. Q4 was a growth/policy scare with estimates falling, so it took three legs and a policy reversal.

## 11. Anything else relevant

1. **The S&P's record was narrow.** Materials, financials, industrials and staples topped on 26 Jan 2018 and never confirmed the September high; Russell and Nasdaq topped three weeks earlier; margin debt topped in May; homebuilders were in a bear market by September; EM and China since June. The index top was the last domino, not the first [CNBC-1224], [FINRA], [BBG-BUILDERS].
2. **Sentiment extremes arrived early and were useless for timing for two weeks.** AAII bears at a five-year high, record outflows and the first undercut of the October low all occurred around 10–12 Dec with the S&P near 2,650; it then fell another 11% [AAII-1213], [CNBC-OUTFLOW].
3. **The capitulation readings clustered one to two sessions before the price low** (put/call 20–21 Dec, volume 21 Dec), and the low itself came on a low-volume half-day. A trader waiting for a high-volume reversal day on the low never got one [CBOE-T], [R-1221].
4. **VIX made its high at the low** (36.07), unlike textbook divergences; the divergence came on the 3 Jan higher low (25.45) [FRED-VIX].
5. **The earnings-reaction flip is measurable.** Q3 season: beats −1.5% at the worst / misses −3.8%. Q4 season: beats +2.1% / misses −0.8% [FS-1029], [FS-110218], [FS-030119]. This can be tracked live every season from FactSet's weekly report.
6. **Price bottomed two quarters before earnings did**, and while estimates were still being cut. Forward P/E 14.1 → 16.6 between January and June 2019 while 2019 growth forecasts went from 7.4% to 2.7% [FS-010419], [FS-062819].
7. **Leverage did not come back.** Margin debt and hedge-fund exposure stayed low for months; the advance was not chased [FINRA], [INV-KOL-0319].
8. **Consensus at the low was defensive** (utilities/staples as top 2019 picks, at P/Es above tech's) [R-DEFENSIVE]. The first week of January saw utilities lag and retail, energy, tech lead [VL 1-7-2019].
9. **Mutual-fund outflows overstated the selling**: ETFs took in $50bn in December [LIPPER-DEC].
10. **Calendar mechanics mattered**: full-size QT began in October; the buyback blackout fell in early October and again from mid-December `[UNVERIFIED dates]`; tax-loss selling from 14 Dec; quad witching 21 Dec; holiday half-day 24 Dec; pension rebalancing 26–31 Dec; new-year inflows.
11. **Official reassurance was read as alarm** (Mnuchin, 23 Dec). The market turned when the cause was addressed (Fed, 4 Jan), not when officials said things were fine.
12. **Year-end funding stress** showed up in repo (GC +260bp on 31 Dec), a preview of September 2019 [BIS-0319].
13. **2018's three lows compared** (Feb, Oct, Dec): Feb retested and held; Oct retested (Nov) and broke (Dec); Dec did not retest at all. The lazy rule "wait for the retest" was right once, lethal once, and cost the whole move once.
