**Main similarities**

1. A Fed that is tightening and says so plainly, with a new-ish chair who does not want to give guidance; the president attacking the Fed in public.
2. The sell-off started in early October from a record high and was led by the year's leaders (technology), with a midterm election in the middle and a one-day post-election rally that failed.
3. Long yields at multi-year highs just before the top (3.24% then, 5.37% now), with the 10-year peaking the day after the index.
4. Strong reported earnings and strong GDP (+3.4% both times) while prices fall: a de-rating, not an earnings collapse.
5. A December FOMC with a hike expected, and shutdown risk in December.
6. Margin debt already below its peak when the index peaked (May 2018; June 2026).

**Main differences**

1. **Inflation.** 2018: about 2% and falling with oil; the Fed could stop (and did on 4 Jan 2019). 2026: 3.7% and rising, core PCE above 3%, PPI 6.5%, "above target for five and a half years". Almost all FOMC participants expect more hikes, two dissented in October for hiking faster, and a governor said on the day of a 3.7% fall that financial conditions are "too accommodative". A pivot is much harder. Verified from the minutes and Cook's speech. [S13] [S51]
2. **Where the Fed is in its cycle.** 2018: hike nine, near neutral. 2026: hike one of a new cycle.
3. **Oil.** 2018: collapsing (helped the pivot). 2026: war-driven and volatile; it has fallen about 20% in three weeks, which is the one development that points the 2018 way, but tanker attacks continue.
4. **The Fed's attitude to the stock fall.** The minutes describe lower AI valuations as a possible "source of market discipline". Nothing comparable in 2018.
5. **The bubble element.** 2018 had no single financed capex boom. 2026 has $680bn a year of AI debt, negative free cash flow at Alphabet, Amazon and Meta, record IPOs (SpaceX, Anthropic), and neocloud credit stress. That is the 2000 ingredient.
6. **Leverage.** Hedge-fund leverage at a record in Fed data; margin debt 22% higher than a year ago. In 2018 margin debt was roughly flat on the year.
7. **Politics.** 2018's election result was neutral for the leaders. 2026's result is read as directly negative for the leading theme.
8. **Speed.** S&P -11.6% in 32 trading days so far, with -8.6% in the last week. In 2018 it took until late November to make a similar first low, then the December leg took it to -19.8%.
9. **Valuation starting point.** Forward P/E 19.4 at the 2026 top vs about 16.8 in 2018; but estimates are rising now and were falling then.
10. **Safe havens.** Gold and bitcoin are falling hard in 2026; bonds only started helping on the worst days.
