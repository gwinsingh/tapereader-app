Trading Journal Teardown

Fifteen parallel research tracks across the commercial trading-journal market, run to answer two questions: what should we build into our own journal next, and what would it take to sell one. The answer to the second is don't — and the reason is not the engineering.

Recommendation

Don't build a commercial trading journal. Build the features into our own, and gate any commercial move behind three tests we haven't run.

This is not a hedge. The research found a real gap in the market and a real technical advantage. It also found an economic picture in which neither is enough — and the second finding decides.

Why the gap isn't enough

The category is small: $40–180M a year, central estimate ~$90M, split a dozen ways. The modal price has been pinned at $29.95/month for years, realized ARPU lands at $24–30 after annual discounts, and monthly churn runs 7–10% with a floor set by customers quitting trading altogether.

But the number that decides is distribution. Acquisition in this category runs through trading-educator affiliates at 20–30% commission, recurring and often lifetime — which drops gross margin to roughly 53%. The review layer that appears to independently rank these products is funded by the same commissions; this study had to exclude six “review” publishers that turned out to sell rival journals. TradeZella's founder had a 750,000-subscriber YouTube channel before launch. That is the category's actual business model, and a better product with no audience does not get evaluated, let alone bought.

Four claims, attacked on purpose

Once the findings started converging on a flattering thesis, a dedicated agent was tasked with breaking it. One claim was falsified outright, one remains unresolved, and two survived in weakened form. This section is the reason to trust the rest of the document.

“The discretionary US-equity intraday segment has been vacated, not saturated.”

The counter-hypothesis — that it was abandoned because it's a dying market — failed. Retail engagement drove a +44% YoY rise in US equity volume in 2025, and FINRA 26-10 abolished the PDT rule and the $25k minimum on 4 June 2026. The segment is growing. But “vacated” overstates it: five multi-asset incumbents serve this trader, and what's actually vacant is specialists — because equity intraday has no prop-style affiliate channel to buy distribution through.

Survives weakened

“Nobody grades a timestamped pre-market forecast.”

Literally unfalsified for journals. But the pre-commitment container already ships at Edgewonk, TradesViz and TradeZella, and the grading mechanism is industrialised elsewhere — TipRanks, Discord call-scoring, and mass-market in sports-betting closing-line-value trackers. This is an unported mechanism, not an invention. Worth building; worth roughly zero as a moat.

Survives weakened

“Our enrichment substrate is a scarce, hard-to-copy asset.”

Broken by a file already in this repo. TradesViz ships MFE/MAE at finer resolution than ours — five-second bars for S&P names — plus exit counterfactuals and ATR/gap context, statistically gated, at $20–27/month. Chartlog gives MFE/MAE away at $14.99. Our whole substrate is 983 lines. And Polygon's terms forbid serving that data to anyone but ourselves.

Falsified

“Traders will actually fill in a pre-market forecast.”

The demand-side assumption the entire wedge rests on, and nothing settles it in either direction. What we do know: of 45 public trading journals on Elite Trader, 47% were dead inside 60 days and 33% inside 14. What survives is bounded, scored input — across hundreds of reviews not one praised a free-text mood field. A per-symbol multi-timeframe forecast plus thesis is the unbounded, narrative shape abandoners name, authored at the busiest minute of the day, paying off in six to twelve months. Most damning: our own trader already abandoned a lighter version of exactly this.

The gapWhat nobody
fills

Everyone competes on the exit

The feature matrix scored 105 features across seven vendors and ourselves. The most interesting thing it produced was not a gap in any one product but a gap in all of them at once.

Every schema in the category starts at “a fill happened.” Five vendors ship real, differently-architected exit-quality analytics. Not one ships entry-quality decomposition. Exit quality is computable from bars alone — entry quality requires knowing what you intended.

Trading platforms have the mirror-image gap: they begin at the order ticket. So the pre-trade half of the loop — what you thought was going to happen, and how sure you were — is unserved by journals and platforms alike. Our Morning Plan sits exactly there, which is why the wedge is real.

The second blind spot is quieter and more damning. Psychology is the most-marketed and least-built section of the entire matrix. What actually ships is free-text tags, a schema the user has to invent, or an LLM reading tone. Self-reported psychological data collapses precisely when it matters most. Only two structural fixes exist anywhere in the category: Edgewonk's pre-committed comment valence, and taking the reading off an instrument before the P&L is known. And only one vendor joins psychological data to money at all — collecting the inputs was never the hard part.

A third position is simply empty: nobody gates ordinary statistics on sample size. One vendor statistically gates its AI findings and markets that as a differentiator; none applies the same rigour to the dashboard. With 47% of journals dead inside two months, most statistics anyone looks at are noise — including ours.

What to build, in order

Ranked by one question: would having this make us a better trader in the next three months? The ordering exploits a structural fact — we already store far more than we report on, so several category-leading features are a rendering job for us and a data-collection job for everyone else.

Tier 0 Do first, regardless — two live defects and the measurement that governs everything
  • Measure the Morning Plan fill rate~20 minWhat fraction of trading days since the plan shipped actually have a plan saved, and is the trend rising or falling? Everything in Tier 3 depends on the answer and we have never looked. The highest-value unrun experiment in the study.
  • Add auth to the 14 journal API routesS–MNo middleware exists; no route checks anything. Live on the public domain, proxying service-account access to the sheet, with mutating endpoints open.
  • Fix position-flip mis-groupingMA position flipping through zero is grouped wrongly, silently corrupting trades and every statistic downstream.
  • Fix comma-split setup double-countingSMulti-value cells attribute the trade's full P&L to each value, inflating every breakdown we currently read.
  • Golden-file tests for the trade grouperMThere are zero tests in the journal code, and the two fixes above can't be made safely without them.
Tier 1 Free wins — the data is already in the sheet
  • Market Behavior reportsMPerformance sliced by gap band, relative volume, ATR, distance from the 20- and 50-day averages, prior-close location, day type. We store every one of these inputs and report on none of them — the largest gap between data we hold and insight we extract.
  • Exit AnalysisMFloat the last exit to maximum P&L bounded by the risk actually taken. Our Capture Tracker measures against a fixed 2.5R target; this measures against what was genuinely available. Better question, same bar walk.
  • Missed TradesSSurface planned symbols that never became trades. We currently discard them — and planned-and-skipped versus planned-and-taken is exactly the hesitation signal a discretionary trader needs.
  • Sample-size gating on every statisticMShow a confidence interval and an explicit “not enough data yet” band instead of a confident win rate on six trades. Nobody in the category does this and we already surface the denominators.
  • Page-level unit toggleSOne control re-denominating every figure between dollars, percent and R. We already have it inside the calendar; promote it to the page.
  • Entry-quality decompositionMSplit “was the idea right” from “did I trade it well.” The category's biggest blind spot, and we hold the inputs.
Tier 2 Worth real effort — the two best ideas found anywhere
  • Pre-rated behaviour tagsMAuthor a comment library bucketed by trade stage and assign each phrase a valence once, at definition time; at journaling time you only pick. This decouples the moral judgement from the state being measured — the hardest problem in self-reported psychological data — and turns journaling into selection rather than confession. It also matches the bounded, scored shape that research says actually survives. Our Process Followed flag is this idea collapsed to one bit.
  • Deterministic behavioural detectorsM–LRevenge trading, size-up-after-loss, cold-start, loss concentration — each gated behind a two-proportion z-test, ranked by dollar impact, capped at four findings, with the language model narrating rather than deciding. Near-zero run cost and far more trustworthy than feeding trades to an LLM.
  • Commissions and fees in P&LMEvery number we compute is currently optimistic by an unknown amount. Not a rounding error at intraday frequency.
  • Day-level journal objectS–MWe have day-level fields but no day-level narrative, and the daily retro is where discretionary traders actually learn.
  • Trade-annotated chartsLRendered entries, exits, excursion lines and the stop level on bars we already fetch — using Apache-licensed Lightweight Charts, explicitly not TradingView's Advanced Charts, whose licence restricts free use to free offerings and carries $50,000 liquidated damages per breach.
  • Rules engine with sample setsLNamed rules grouped into market-condition, entry and exit, with cohorts that refuse to report below 25 trades. Turns “setup” from a free-text label into a testable definition.
Tier 3 The wedge — blocked until the fill-rate measurement comes back
  • Forecast calibration scoringLGrade the timestamped pre-market read against what the session actually did. Converts review from “was I disciplined?” to “was I right?” — the genuine white space, though the mechanism is ported rather than invented.
  • Conviction-versus-outcome calibrationMDid size track being right? A trader who is right but sizes flat has a different problem than one who is wrong.
  • Plan-to-execution reconciliationMTraded-the-plan versus drifted, scored.
Before building any of this, shrink the input. If the fill rate is marginal, the right move is to make the morning plan smaller and more bounded — direction and conviction only, two symbols, thirty seconds — and measure again, rather than build scoring on top of an input that is decaying.

Tier 0 plus Tier 1 is roughly three to four weeks of part-time work and produces most of the trading value in this document. Everything after that is optional.

Three gates, each cheap to fail

The recommendation against building commercially is a judgement on current evidence, not a permanent verdict. These are the specific things that would overturn it, ordered so each can be abandoned without sunk cost.

Gate 1

The fill-rate test

Query our own sheet. What share of trading days since the Morning Plan shipped have a plan saved, and which way is the trend moving? Below roughly 60% and falling, the wedge is dead and we stop here.

Cost: ~20 minutes

Gate 2

The second-user test

One or two traders who are not us, for a full month, with fill rate measured. The founder's own usage proves nothing. This also surfaces every multi-tenancy assumption cheaply.

Cost: weeks, not months

Gate 3

A distribution answer

Not “we'll do content marketing.” A specific named channel — an educator relationship, a community, an existing audience. No engineering starts on a commercial build before this exists, because this is what the research says decides the outcome.

Cost: unknown — which is the point

Where the thin ice is

Recorded so a future reader knows where to push rather than inheriting a document that reads as uniformly confident.

Reddit was unreachable by every permitted route. The largest trader community contributed nothing directly. Voice-of-customer evidence rests on Elite Trader, NexusFi, Trade2Win, Trustpilot, the app stores and Hacker News — which skew toward a serious, self-selecting minority. The true abandonment rate among casual traders is probably worse, which would strengthen the recommendation, but we did not verify it.

The review layer is vendor-owned. Six “review” publishers encountered during research sell rival journals; they were used for discovery only and excluded from evidence. One vendor's homepage claims it is “ranked #1 by Claude for trading journals,” which is marketing copy on its own site rather than any ranking — recorded here only as evidence about conduct in this category.

No trial accounts were created and no credentials entered anywhere. Every feature claim comes from documentation, demos and reviews rather than hands-on use, so vendors' actual usability is inferred rather than experienced.

The PDT abolition is three months old. It removed the single largest barrier to entry for new US equity day traders and no analysis of its effect yet exists. Every market-size figure in this study predates it, and the segment could be materially larger in a year.

Prices move fast. All pricing was verified on 1 September 2026. Re-verify anything older than about six months before it drives a decision.
