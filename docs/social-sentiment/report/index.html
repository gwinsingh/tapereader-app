Verdict — Don't build it

The red team's verdict is don't build it, and the rest of the research supports it. No journal columns, no Morning Plan panel, no public page, no signup, no multi-quarter analysis programme.

But keep collecting. That is an archival decision, not a reduced build. ApeWisdom publishes no history and no vendor sells it, so a day not captured is gone permanently. It costs 66 MB/year. The collector is running and took its first capture on 8 September.

Spend $0. Not from thrift — the ≤$10 rung is empty. Every subscription product surveyed starts at $19.99 or above. The only sub-$10 option that exists at all is X's pay-per-post, where $10 buys about 95 posts a day across an entire watchlist.

The question, and why the answer is no

The ask was to add crowd sentiment to TapeReader and study how it interacts with a discretionary intraday breakout trader's results — on a budget of effectively nothing. The research says the study cannot answer that question on any useful horizon, for reasons that are arithmetic rather than opinion.

Half the trades in the May journal export are index and sector ETFs — QQQ 7, SPY 6, SOXL 5 of 36 — which carry no ticker-level attention signal and drop out by construction. That puts analyzable accrual at ~18 trades/month, not 35.

Because those horizons are honest, the whole study came to rest on a single confirmatory test measured across the market rather than across trades — and that test is broken in four independent, measured ways. The clearest: its "surging attention" threshold is crossed by a ticker going from one mention to five, and 612 of 797 tickers sit at exactly one mention. The risk was never too little data. It was a well-powered test of a noise-dominated predictor — a precise answer about nothing.

Do this in October, not July 2027

The cheapest way to settle this is already written into the pre-registration. Run the RVOL gate after about twenty days of collection: if attention correlates above 0.5 with relative volume, every attention feature is dropped by the study's own frozen rule. It reads no outcome column, so it costs nothing statistically and the stopping rule explicitly permits it.

It can close the question eight months early, for about five hours of work.

The five findings that mattered most

  1. Polygon's news insights is authorized on the key already held — per-ticker sentiment plus written reasoning, so it is auditable rather than a black box. Coverage is 100% from mid-2024, which means every trade already journalled backfills for free. This was not expected.
  2. Ticker resolution, not access, is the binding constraint. Only 0.6% of Reddit comments carry a cashtag, and 39 of 51 common English words tested are real tickers — $OPEN, $NOW, $ALL, $LOVE. The one source with free, exact, author-declared resolution is contractually closed.
  3. Tradestie's sentiment_score is a static constant. Zero within-ticker variation across 480 tickers over 5.4 years, while the docs claim a 15-minute recompute. Using it would have injected silent look-ahead bias into every downstream result.
  4. The expensive problem is licence, not data. Private journal use is nearly free. Public display of derived market analytics starts at $499/mo confirmed, and $2,499/mo from the incumbent vendor — whose consumer tiers are machine-readably personal at every rung, so paying more buys rate limit and history, never licence scope.
  5. A real share of small-cap chatter is other people's breakout scanners echoing price — "$SATL breakout, up 13.9% on 4,801,697 volume". That is a lagging transform of price wearing the costume of sentiment. Left in, it manufactures the headline result out of nothing.

What escapes the verdict

Two things survive, and neither is social sentiment — which is its own finding about where the value actually was.

  • Polygon news sentiment against the Catalyst column, which is currently 100% blank across the entire journal.
  • SEC EDGAR full-text search — keyless, reaching back to 2001, exact ticker resolution, returning 8-K item codes filed under legal liability rather than guessed by a model. It also argues for adding an Offering/Dilution catalyst, absent today and the class most invisible on a chart.

An unrelated finding worth acting on

The licensing work surfaced a problem that predates this study: the planned public market-scans product cannot ship on Polygon data. Display and redistribution are prohibited across three independent clauses, with no reconstruction carve-out, so breadth aggregates and the movers table fail together.

The recommended fix costs nothing: put it behind Cloudflare Access, as the trade journal already is. Ingesting bars privately was never blocked — only publishing them was. That ships the spec whole and unblocks the daily-bar work immediately.

One genuinely live exposure: the journal writes Polygon-derived columns into a Google Sheet documented as shared, and Cloudflare Access does not cover a spreadsheet. Checking who that sheet is shared with outranks every design decision in the research.

How much to trust this

Everything carries an as-of date of 8 September 2026, and this category moves fast. Claims are tagged: [M] measured directly against a live endpoint, [V] verified on the source's own material, [R] reported by third parties, [I] inference.

Two caveats stated plainly. First, the entire first research wave ran without being able to read the governing brief — a concurrent session switched the shared checkout's branch mid-run. Those tracks were written from their task prompts, which carried the constraints faithfully, but not against the full contract. Second, Reddit's primary terms were never read; they are unreachable from every network path available to this run, so every Reddit claim is second-hand.

Three cross-track contradictions were resolved rather than smoothed — FINRA, Polygon and StockTwits each got a dedicated pass. Two of them reversed a track's recommendation. Where the research disagrees with itself, the disagreement is recorded in 13-open-questions.md rather than tidied away.
