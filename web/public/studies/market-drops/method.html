Method, data and limits

How the study was built: the definitions, the data and its coverage, what was computed, and the limits that apply to every page.

On this pageDefinitionsData and coverageResearchLimits that apply everywhereRebuilding

Definitions

  • Drop. The S&P 500 closes 10% or more below its highest close of the previous 252 sessions. Peak = that high. Low = the lowest close before the next new 52-week closing high, which ends the drop. 36 since 1950.
  • Rally, failed rally, the turn. Inside a drop, closes are cut into swings by a 5% reversal rule. A rally is a rise of 5%+ from a swing low; it failed if a later close in the same drop went below that low; the rally from the final low is the turn.
  • Sessions. Trading days. “After the low” counts from the close of the low day.
  • Stock universe. 503 members of today's S&P 500, the Nasdaq-100, and a hand list of past and present leaders that still trade: 665 stocks with daily bars from 1998 or their listing. Breadth = shares of this universe.
  • Returns. Index levels are price only. ETF and stock returns use closes adjusted for splits and dividends. Charts show split-adjusted prices.
  • Fed stance. “Hiking” / “cutting” = the policy rate moved by a quarter point or more over the previous six months (before 1983: half a point in the effective rate).
  • Follow-through day. Sessions 4 to 15 after a swing low, index up 1.25%+ on higher volume than the previous day, low not broken first.
  • Breadth-thrust stand-ins. A: best 10-session average share of advancers within 20 sessions ≥ 61.5%. B: ≥ 55% of stocks at a 20-day closing high on one day within 20 sessions. C: ≥ 90% of stocks above their 50-day average within 40 sessions.

Research

Four research passes (2018; the 2000–02 and 2007–09 bears and the AI comparison; other drops and the evidence on bottoming signals; the present) collected dated facts with sources. Their notes are on Research notes and sources. The narrative sections on the pages are written from those notes; anything that could not be re-checked is marked not verified.

Limits that apply everywhere

  • Small samples. 36 drops since 1950; 13 since 1999 with stock-level breadth; three long bears. Medians of small groups move a lot when one case is added.
  • Hindsight. A “low” is known only afterwards. Every table of what surrounded lows is a description of the past, not a rule that could have been followed.
  • Survivorship. The stock universe is today's membership. For 2000–02 and 2007–09 the companies that failed are missing; breadth and leader tables for those years are flattering.
  • One market, closing prices. US large caps, daily closes. Intraday extremes, other markets and smaller stocks can differ.
  • No strategy is tested. No entries, exits, costs, slippage or position sizing. A share that “would have” been captured is not claimed anywhere.
  • An unfinished event. Everything about 2026 describes a drop still running on 20 Nov 2026.
  • Not advice. The study describes; it does not recommend.
