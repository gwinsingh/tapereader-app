The shape of the day

How the trading day itself behaved inside drops and after lows: whether the damage came overnight or during the session, what happened to gaps, where the index closed in its daily range, and, for the two most recent drops, in which half-hours the moves fell.

Free intraday data does not exist for 2018 or earlier. Two sources are used instead. For every drop since 1993, the daily open, high, low and close of SPY (the S&P 500 ETF) split each day into an overnight part (previous close to the open) and a session part (open to close). For the last two years, 30-minute bars show the inside of the session.

Compounded returns of SPY. “After the low” = the first 40 sessions. Over the whole period since 1993 the overnight part earned almost all of SPY's long-run gain and the session part almost none; that makes a drop whose loss comes entirely in the session stand out.

What to take from it
  • Late 2018 was a regular-hours decline. From 20 September to 24 December SPY gained +2.1% overnight in sum and lost −21.8% between the open and the close. Morning strength was sold day after day. After the low it flipped: in the next 40 sessions the session part gained +18.9%.
  • The present drop has the same signature so far: overnight 0.0%, session −11.4%.
  • Drops driven by news that arrives outside trading hours look different: in 2020 and 2022 most of the loss came overnight, as gaps down.
  • In the two long bears both parts were negative, the session part far more.

For all sessions since 1993: 46% of gap-ups closed below the open and 52% of gap-downs closed above it. Counts inside a single drop are small; read them as colour, not as rates.

In the 2018 fall 68% of gap-ups were sold (19 cases), against 46% normally. In the 40 sessions after the low 77% of gap-downs were bought. Most drops show a milder version of the same switch; the two long bears show no clear pattern, with gap-ups sold about half the time throughout.

“Before” = the 60 or so sessions up to the index high. After the December 2018 low the average close was at 0.70 of the day's range and only 3% of days closed weak: the clearest change of character in the table.

  • In falls, the average close sat in the lower half of the range (0.44 to 0.53) and about a third of days closed in the bottom quarter.
  • After real lows the average close moved to 0.54–0.70. The size of that shift, more than any single day, is what “buyers are back” looked like on daily bars.
  • Daily ranges roughly doubled or tripled inside drops against the calm before them, and stayed wide for weeks after the low. Half of all days in a fall moved 1% or more.

Three legs down, two failed rallies, and nearly all the damage in the day session

The decline came in three legs, each starting from a lower high. Single days of −3% erased weeks of gains. Over the 65 sessions the overnight moves compounded to a small gain and the open-to-close moves compounded to more than the whole fall.

The down-legs

  • 3–29 October. Rates and earnings reactions. 10 October (−3.3%) had no news trigger; 24 October brought −3.1%.
  • 8–23 November. Oil, Apple, Nvidia and the large technology stocks.
  • 4–24 December. Began with −3.2% on 4 December and ended as a persistence event: the index fell on 7 of the last 8 sessions and the small-cap index on 13 of 14 CNBC, 24 Dec 2018.

The average daily range (high to low) was 0.66% in the 63 sessions before the high and 1.68% during the fall. The index closed up or down 1% or more on 2% of sessions before the high and on 42% of sessions during the fall (study price data).

The failed rallies shared several traits: a headline started them, the largest gains came in the first one or two days, the most beaten-down groups led, and none produced a day of very broad buying SentimenTrader, 7 Jan 2019. Two of them closed back above the 20-day EMA (exponential moving average) and one above the 50-day average before failing (study price data).

The daily reports show the same thing session by session: a morning rally sold on 11 October; a strong open turned into a 900-point Dow range on 29 October; +200 to −300 on 2 November; +300 before the Fed to −352 on 19 December; an afternoon collapse on 20 December Value Line, 11 Oct 30 Oct 20 Dec 2018.

Large intraday recoveries occurred three times before the low and did not mark it: 23 October (Dow −549 to −126), 6 December (−785 to −79) and 10 December. The one that held, 27 December (−611 to +260), came after the 5.0% day.

Liquidity explains the speed. The futures order book held about a third of its usual depth, so the same order moved the price about three times as far, down in December and up in January Bloomberg, 21 Mar 2019.

What is documented about short-term trading in the period

Evidence here is thin. No first-hand, dated account by a day trader or swing trader was found. What exists is indirect.

  • Buying dips failed. Morgan Stanley measured the average return in weeks following a down week and found 2018 the first year since 2002 in which it was negative. Its strategist said in December that institutions had tried to buy each dip and lost each time CNBC, 19 Nov 2018 CNBC, 20 Dec 2018.
  • By the arithmetic in the table, the day session lost 21.8% and two-thirds of gap-up opens closed lower. That describes what the tape did; it is not a record of what anyone traded.
  • Followers of the Investor’s Business Daily method were told the market was “in correction” until the follow-through day of 4 January, when the status changed to a confirmed uptrend MoneyShow, 7 Jan 2019. Whether follow-through signals were given and failed in October or November is not verified.
  • Even after 4 January the same writer remained cautious and wanted to see more. Disbelief was the common tone of the first weeks.
What to take from it
  • During the fall: strength at the open was sold on about two days in three, and closes were in the lower half of the range on average.
  • After the low: the same statistics flipped within days. Gap-down opens were bought on about three days in four and the average close moved to the upper third of the range.
Limits The intraday statistics are computed from daily open, high, low and close only. They say nothing about the path within the session or about execution in thin markets.

What this suggests about intraday styles

Inference from the tables above and the research notes, not a test of any method.

  • In a regular-hours decline of the 2018 kind, the repeated event was a firm open that faded: gap-ups were sold about two times in three and a third of days closed in the bottom quarter of their range. A style that buys early strength was working against that current; a style that waits for the open to fail, or that trades from the short side of failed opens, was working with it.
  • Ranges were two to three times normal. The same setup carried two to three times the usual move in both directions, which is the same as saying that an unchanged share size carried two to three times the usual risk.
  • The largest up days also happened inside drops. A decline is not one-way intraday: 2018's fall contained 17% of days up 1% or more.
  • The change of character at a real low showed up as closes: days started to finish near their highs and gap-downs started to be bought. It took one to three weeks of sessions to be visible as a pattern rather than as one good day.
  • Leveraged ETFs turn a wide-range, two-way tape into decay: a 3x fund loses ground when the index goes down 4% and up 4%. That is arithmetic, not a forecast.
Limits Daily bars cannot show what happened inside the day beyond open, high, low and close. Gap counts inside one drop are small. The 30-minute study covers two drops, both from the last two years, so it describes those two and proves nothing general. No intraday method is back-tested here.
