How 2018 bottomed and turned

The low came on a half-day session, 24 December 2018, on unremarkable volume. The proof arrived in stages over the following three weeks, and each stage cost part of the rebound. This page lays out what could be seen on which day.

What the 2018 low teaches
  • There was no retest. A trader had 7 sessions (26 December to early January) with the index within 8% of its low, and never saw it within 4% again after the first week.
  • Nothing on the day of the low marked it. Volume was light, the session was short, and the news (the Treasury secretary's calls to banks, the shutdown, the president's remarks on the Fed) was bad.
  • The earliest evidence that separated this rally from the two that had failed in October and November was breadth and persistence: a follow-through day on session 7, a breadth thrust inside three weeks, days closing near their highs.
  • The evidence that failed rallies rarely produce (most stocks above their 50-day average; a close above the 200-day) arrived when the index was already +12% to +17% off the low.

The capitulation was on 20 and 21 December, not on the low day

Capitulation means selling without regard to price. By every measure that records it, the peak came on the Thursday and Friday before the low. A trader waiting for a high-volume reversal on the low day never saw one.

1.82total put/call ratio, 20 Dec 2018, highest of the year
1.13equity-only put/call ratio, 21 Dec 2018, highest of the year
2.46×volume against its 50-day average, 21 Dec 2018 (study data)
0.84×volume against its 50-day average on the low, 24 Dec 2018

The put/call ratio divides the number of put options traded by the number of calls; a high reading means heavy buying of protection. On 20 December SentimenTrader found that only October 1987 and October–November 2008 had seen a larger share of NYSE and Nasdaq stocks at 52-week lows SentimenTrader, 20 Dec 2018. In the week before the low about 60% of financial stocks and over 70% of energy stocks were at 52-week lows, and small option traders bought nearly 3 million puts, the second-highest weekly total on record SentimenTrader, 24 Dec 2018.

Friday 21 December was a quarterly options and futures expiry, which inflates volume. Monday 24 December was a half-session before a holiday. Put/call ratios were already falling on the low day: 1.32 total and 0.79 equity-only. The VIX did the opposite and made its high on the low day.

Two details differ from the usual textbook picture. The VIX peak of 36.07 was lower than February’s 37.32 although the decline was twice as deep. And there was no divergence at the low: breadth and the VIX were at their worst on the day of the lowest close. The divergence came afterwards, on 3 January.

What to take from it
  • Sentiment readings were extreme for eight sessions before the low and were no help in timing it.
  • The cluster of options, volume and new-lows extremes fell within two sessions of the low.

26 December → 4 January → 9 January

Three dated events turned a bounce into a confirmed advance: a 90% up day one session after the low, a second one with a change in Fed language on session 7, and a breadth thrust on session 10.

  1. 26 December, session 1. +5.0% on thin news and lighter-than-usual volume. Earlier rallies in the decline had no day like it. SentimenTrader noted on 7 January that the November and December rally attempts had shown no strong breadth days and both failed SentimenTrader, 7 Jan 2019.
  2. 3–4 January, sessions 6 and 7. The index fell 2.5% on the Apple warning and held 4.1% above the low with the VIX at 25. The next day brought +3.4% with seven advancing stocks for each decliner, the follow-through day, and the Fed chair’s “patient”. The VIX fell back below the three-month VIX.
  3. 9 January, session 10. The ten-day ratio of NYSE advancing to declining stocks reached 2.37. Walter Deemer calls a reading above 1.97 “breakaway momentum”; by his count, as reported in 2023, there had been 24 such signals since 1949 and the index was higher a year later after 23 of them Carson Group. Some sources date the signal 8 January.

Across all the episodes in this study, a follow-through day was not rare in rallies that failed: it appeared in 53 of 91 failed rallies and in 28 of 36 that held. A broad ten-day advance was more selective: 15 of 51 failed rallies against 10 of 13 that held (study data). In January 2019 both were present.

The retest that many expected did not come

Both earlier lows of 2018 had been tested, in April and in November. Many strategists expected the same in early 2019. The weakest close in sessions 5 to 60 after the low was 4.1% above it.

Why there was none

  • The stated cause changed. The Fed moved from “automatic pilot” on 19 December to “patient” on 4 January and to a formal pause on 30 January. After the February and October lows nothing had changed.
  • Selling pressure had a calendar end: tax-loss selling stopped on 31 December and pension funds were buyers in the last week.
  • Positions were light. Margin debt had fallen 17%, fund outflows were at records, and JPMorgan found investor exposure still low in March Bloomberg, 21 Mar 2019. Those waiting for a retest were themselves buyers on small dips.
  • Thin order books worked in both directions: the same lack of depth that deepened the fall steepened the recovery.
What to take from it
  • In 2018 the three lows behaved three ways: February was tested and held, October was tested and broke, December was not tested.
  • The December low differed from the other two in two observable ways within ten sessions: breadth thrusts and a change in the cause.
Limits The explanations for the missing retest are after-the-fact readings. On 4 January the expectation of a retest was reasonable and widely held.

Through the fall, strength at the open was sold and a third of days closed near their lows. From 26 December the session part of the day carried the whole advance, gap-downs were bought three times in four, and almost no day closed weak. More on The shape of the day.

Why late 2018 stopped at −19.8% and did not become 2000 or 2008

Earnings were still growing, the valuation had already reset, credit stress was brief, banks were sound, and the Fed had room to stop and used it within seven sessions of the low. The sources of the time said so in their own words, and the bears among them framed the decline as cyclical.

How the bears themselves framed it

Morgan Stanley’s strategist had been the most prominent bear of 2018. In October he described a cyclical bear market inside a longer bull market, with sectors correcting one after another since January. On 20 December, four sessions before the low, he said it was too late to sell CNBC, 29 Oct 2018 CNBC, 20 Dec 2018.

This is not the place to be selling. — Morgan Stanley, 20 Dec 2018

Wellington Shields noted that 2018 saw the fifth-largest one-year fall in the P/E since 1945 while earnings rose about 22%, and cited a UBS count of 26 such years with a median gain of 16% in the period that followed Wellington Shields, Jan 2019.

What it shared with real bear markets

It could not be dismissed at the time. Parts of the yield curve inverted for the first time since 2007. The factory index had its largest one-month fall since 2008. Earnings estimates were falling. Buying dips had failed for the first time since 2002. The share of stocks at 52-week lows had been exceeded only in 1987 and 2008. The Nasdaq and the small-cap index were both down more than 20%. The Fed was still raising rates, and a few of its officials already opposed the December rise Fed minutes, Dec 2018.

For scale, the same high-yield spread had been near 9% in early 2016 and near 20% in 2008 not verified. In the study’s own data the Baa spread (yield of Baa-rated corporate bonds over Treasuries) peaked at 2.48 points in this episode, against 3.90 in the 2000–02 bear market and 6.16 in 2007–09.

What to take from it
  • The decline cut the price paid for earnings by a sixth while earnings held. In 2000–02 and 2007–09 earnings themselves fell hard not verified (not researched for this page).
  • Credit never broke: spreads widened for three months and reversed in days.
  • The episode stayed a correction because policy changed. The Fed could change because inflation was at its target.
Limits This is hindsight. On 24 December the list of similarities to earlier bear markets was as long as the list of differences, and the policy change had not yet happened.
