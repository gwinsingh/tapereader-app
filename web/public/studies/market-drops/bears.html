2000–02 and 2007–09

The S&P 500 fell −49% over 637 sessions from March 2000 and −57% over 355 sessions from October 2007. Neither looked like a bear market in its first two months. This page sets out how each unfolded, how many rallies failed on the way, and what the first weeks looked like at the time.

What to take from it
  • Neither bear announced itself. After 33 sessions both were shallower than the present drop, and both later rallied back near their highs before the real decline.
  • What distinguished them was not the first leg but persistence: 15 and 15 failed rallies, a 200-day average that turned down and stayed down, earnings that fell by more than half, and (in 2007–09) credit spreads that kept widening.
  • In both, the Fed was cutting rates for most of the way down. Rate cuts did not end them.
  • Their final lows were lows of exhaustion: VIX and new-low counts had peaked months earlier.
Limits Two episodes, both with recessions and both with a broken financing channel (telecom and dot-com equity in 2000; mortgages and banks in 2007). They show what a bear can look like, not what one must look like. The 1973–74 and 2022 bears had other mechanics (inflation and rates).
