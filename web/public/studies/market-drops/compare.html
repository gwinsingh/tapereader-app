Now against 2018

Autumn 2026 and autumn 2018 share a calendar, a hiking Fed, strong earnings and a regular-hours sell-off. They differ in how deep the fall is after the same number of sessions, in what is falling, in inflation, in valuation and in leverage. On several of those the present looks less like 2018 and more like early 2000.

  • Depth. 33 sessions in, 2018 was down 6.0%; the low of that first leg (29 October, −9.9%) had already been made and a rally was under way. The present drop is −11.6% and made its low for the move on the latest session.
  • What is falling. In 2018 the equal-weight index fell with the cap-weight one and no sector was up. Now the equal-weight index is −2.3% against −11.6% for the S&P 500, 5 of nine sectors are up, and the gap between the best and worst sector is 37 points. The only earlier column with that kind of split is 2000 (37 points, 5 sectors up).
  • When it is falling. In both 2018 and 2026 the loss came during regular hours, with overnight moves summing to about zero.
  • Fear and credit. The VIX (36) is higher than at the same point in any of the four. Credit is the opposite: the Baa spread has widened 0.06 point, less than at the same stage of 2018 (0.13), 2000 (0.36), 2007 (0.50) or 2022 (0.31).

The 2026 column is a drop in progress; the other two are complete. That alone makes 2026 look milder than it may turn out. The comparison is about shape: who is falling relative to whom.

2018 was a de-rating of the whole market: the strong and the weak, growth and value, fell together by about a fifth, and when the pressure lifted the same leaders (software, payments, semiconductors) led again. 2000 was the unwinding of one theme: the previous leaders fell by half or more while the rest of the market rose, and the theme did not lead the next advance. So far 2026 has the second shape. The AI boom against the dot-com bubble looks at whether the fundamentals behind the theme resemble 2000's.

November 2026 against autumn 2018: the same shape, a different Fed problem

By the study’s macro measure, September 2018 is the closest past peak to October 2026, followed by January 2018 and 2000. The chart shapes are alike. The main difference is inflation: about 2% and falling then, 3.7% and rising now, which makes a Fed retreat much harder.

Main similarities

  1. A Fed that is raising rates, a chair who avoids guiding markets, and a president attacking the Fed in public.
  2. A record high in early autumn, a fall led by technology, and a midterm election in the middle of it.
  3. Long-term yields at multi-year highs within days of the top.
  4. Strong earnings and GDP growth of 3.4% while prices fell: a fall in valuation, not in profits.
  5. A December Fed meeting with a rise expected, and a possible government shutdown in December.

Main differences

  1. Inflation. In 2018 it was near target and falling with oil. In 2026 it is 3.7% and rising, and core inflation is above 3%. Almost all officials expect further rises; two voted to raise in October. On 20 November, the day of the 3.70% fall, a governor called financial conditions “too accommodative”.
  2. Stage of the cycle. Late in 2018; the first rise in 2026.
  3. Oil. Falling throughout the 2018 drop. War-driven in 2026, though down about 20% in three weeks.
  4. The Fed’s balance sheet. Shrinking in 2018, not in 2026.
  5. A financed spending boom. 2018 had none. 2026 has the AI build-out described above, which is the feature shared with 2000.
  6. Leverage. Margin debt is 2.3% of GDP against 1.4%, and the Fed reports record hedge-fund leverage.
  7. Starting valuation. Higher in 2026 on every measure in the table.
  8. Estimates. Rising now; they were being cut in late 2018 not verified.
What to take from it
  • The 2018 fall ended when the Fed changed course. The conditions that allowed that change, low inflation and falling oil, are only partly present in 2026.
  • On the items where 2026 differs from 2018, it more often resembles 2000: valuation, leverage and a debt-funded investment boom.
Limits Two periods will always share some features and differ on others; which ones matter is only known afterwards. The 2018 column mixes computed values with remembered ones, marked as such.

What to take from it
  • The 2018 comparison is strongest on the surface (the calendar, the hiking Fed, the regular-hours selling) and on earnings.
  • It is weakest where it mattered most for how 2018 ended: inflation gave the Fed room to stop, the valuation had reset to its long-run average by the low, and the whole market had been washed out together.
  • The features 2026 shares with 2000 (extreme valuation, a high and growing level of margin debt, a financed capital-spending theme, leaders collapsing while the average stock holds) are features of how 2000 began. They do not say how this ends: 2000 itself took 30 months, and other narrow leadership breaks (1998's, 2015's) ended as corrections.
