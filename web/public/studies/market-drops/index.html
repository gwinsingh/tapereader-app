Anatomy of a Drop

A study of how US stock-market drops unfold and end, built around one question: how far does the autumn of 2026 resemble the autumn of 2018, and what would it look like if it turned out more like 2000 or 2007 instead? 36 drops since 1950, measured the same way, with the charts to train the eye on.

On this pageThe main findingsA reading orderCharts to train the eye on

The study has 23 pages. Each can be read alone; repetition between them is deliberate. Charts are interactive: hover or tap for dates and prices; wide charts scroll sideways on a phone. Candles are white for up days and black for down days, with the 9- and 20-day EMA and the 50- and 200-day SMA. This is a historical study and not advice: it describes what happened and how often, and nothing in it says what to buy or sell.

The main findings

Each links to the page with the evidence.

  1. A 10% fall is a fork, not a verdict. Of 35 earlier drops that reached −10%, 16 ended before −15% and 11 went to −20% or more. A year after the first −10% close the index was higher 27 times. Every drop since 1950 →
  2. The look-alike chart is weak evidence. Measured from the peak, 2018 ranks 53 of 95 as a match for the present path. Across 95 past declines, the closest-matching charts had no forecasting value for the next 60 sessions (correlation -0.04). Do look-alike charts mean anything? →
  3. On the macro numbers the comparison with 2018 has a basis. The backdrop at the October 2026 high was closest to September 2018 among all peaks since 1987: a hiking Fed, low unemployment, calm credit, strong earnings. March 2000 ranks third. Earnings, rates and credit →
  4. What is falling is unlike 2018. In 2018 the median stock fell −20% with the index and no sector rose. Now the median stock is −1%, 47% of stocks are up, staples and health care have risen, and the previous leaders are down −22%. That shape is how 2000 began. Now against 2018 →
  5. Three things that ended 2018 are missing or weaker. Inflation was 2% and falling, so the Fed could stop within two weeks of the low; now it is 3.7% and rising. The valuation reset to its long-run average; it started far higher this time (Shiller P/E 41 against 33). Margin loans were flat on the year; they are up +31%. Now against 2018 →
  6. Profits do not settle it. Cisco, Microsoft, Intel and Oracle were highly profitable in 2000; their shares fell 60% to 90% and took 15 to 25 years to regain their highs. Being a money-making machine protected the company, not the share price. The AI boom against the dot-com bubble →
  7. A pop is not a low. In their first five sessions the 35 real lows and the 91 failed rallies were nearly identical in size, volume and number of up days. A pop alone was the low about 28% of the time. Failed pops and the real turn →
  8. What separated real turns was breadth and persistence. A follow-through day came after 58% of failed rallies as well. A broad breadth surge within three weeks came after most real lows and few failed ones. 67% of failed rallies got back above the 50-day average. Failed pops and the real turn →
  9. Proof costs part of the move. At real lows the index was back above its 50-day average after a median 12 sessions and +7% off the low, and above its 200-day after 22 sessions and +10% (17% to 39% in the V-shaped lows of 2018, 2020 and 2009). Moving averages as a filter →
  10. December 2018 gave no second chance. The low came on a quiet half-day; the next session rose 5%; there was no retest; the stages of proof arrived over three weeks. It is one of very few clean V-bottoms in 76 years. How 2018 bottomed and turned →
  11. Signs at lows: washout first, proof after. VIX peaked at the low in fast drops and months before it in slow ones. Volume on the low day meant little. At every deep low since 1999 fewer than 21% of stocks were above their 200-day average; today it is 42%. Signs at the low →
  12. The fall has come in regular hours, as in 2018. From its high to its low in 2018 SPY gained +2.1% overnight and lost −21.8% between open and close; 68% of gap-ups were sold. So far in 2026: overnight 0.0%, session −11.4%. After the 2018 low the pattern flipped within days. The shape of the day →
  13. Shelters lag afterwards; early relative strength leads. Staples, utilities and health care fell least in every drop and trailed for six months after every low. In 2018 the relative-strength lines of semiconductors, homebuilders and software bottomed one to two months before the index and those groups led in 2019. Sectors and themes →
  14. Who leads after depends on the kind of drop. In the first month off a low the hardest-hit stocks bounced most, almost every time. After 2018, 2020 and 2025 the leaders from before the drop led again. After 2000–02 they did not. The stocks that led →
  15. Leverage was higher going in than in 2018. Brokers' margin loans stood at 2.28% of GDP (Apr 2026), up +47% in a year; at the 2018 high they were 1.45% and flat. The Fed reports hedge-fund leverage at its highest since its data began. Leverage and positioning →
