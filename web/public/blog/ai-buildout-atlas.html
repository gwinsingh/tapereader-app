AI Buildout Atlas: where the money goes, in the West and in China

A 32-page atlas of the AI build-out in ten layers, from the power grid to the apps people pay for, mapped once for the West and once for China on the same definitions.

This is a multi-page study with its own navigation, charts, glossary and source list. It explains the AI build-out one layer at a time: power, data centres, chips, the semiconductor supply chain, networking, cloud, model labs, and applications and agents. For every layer it shows where the money comes from, how it moves between layers, and which layers keep the profit.

Edition 2 is dated 25 September 2026. It adds a full China track and a page that compares the two systems on the same definitions. Every figure below is the atlas’s own estimate for calendar 2026 as of that date.

The answer in two paragraphs#

The West. In 2026 the Western world is spending about $1.0 trillion on AI data centres, up from about $290 billion in 2024, mostly through five companies: Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and Oracle. About two-thirds of it buys chips and servers that wear out in five to six years. Most of the money does not come from AI customers yet. About half is the existing profit of large technology companies, a fifth is new debt, and a fifth (about $200 billion) is what consumers, businesses and advertisers pay for AI. The suppliers keep the profit: chipmakers and memory makers take about two-thirds of the operating profit, the clouds about 5%, and the model labs lose money.

China. On the same definitions China spends about RMB 0.9 trillion (about $134 billion), mostly through Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance and Baidu, with the state directing about 19%. It builds mainly on its own chips and on cheap, abundant power. Usage is very large and revenue is small. Almost nobody keeps a profit.

What is in it#

  • Two tracks of twelve pages each: an overview, ten layer pages and a synthesis, for the West and for China.
  • A comparison page: 35 measures side by side, the cost of power, chips and tokens in each system, who funds the build-out, and who keeps the profit.
  • Reference pages: a glossary of 393 terms, a directory of 259 companies, a timeline of 1,025 events, and 2,151 sources.
  • A method page: how the figures were checked, and the list of claims that could not be confirmed.
Limits

The atlas is a snapshot of 25 September 2026 and is not updated automatically. Many 2026 figures are estimates, and some figures for private companies come from press reports and not from filings. The atlas marks each number with how well it is supported.
